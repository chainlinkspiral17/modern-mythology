# KWIK STOP MANAGER
### RANCH · 2002 · MANAGER SIM · our first game
### twelve weeks · nine employees · one landlord

---

## THE BOX COPY

*Summer 1998, Highway 101. The last manager left a note. You get
the keys, a crew with lives of their own, a cooler full of soda,
and Mr. Aldous, who counts the cars in the lot from his sedan
before he comes in for the rent.*

*Other games would hide the numbers from you. We put the score in
the corner, in gold, recalculated while you watch. Run the
counter. Watch the summer. See what you're worth in September.*

---

## GETTING STARTED

Kwik Stop Manager lives on the slowstick shelf under Volume 7.
CLOCK IN starts Summer 1998; the game saves every week, and
CONTINUE reopens the store where you left it. The title screen
keeps count of every summer you've managed and how each one ended.

**Controls:** check boxes to schedule the crew, − and + to order
stock, RUN THE WEEK to run it. Arrows or d-pad move focus, accept
chooses. ESC clocks out between weeks.

## THE NUMBERS · posted, all of them

- **CASH** — you open with $300. Everything true ends up here.
- **STOCK** — units on the shelf. Shelf max is 12. Opens at 5.
- **MORALE** — the crew's, 0 to 9. High morale sells; the counter
  can tell when the people behind it are all right.
- **LANDLORD** — your standing with Mr. Aldous, 0 to 9. Read the
  section below. Read it twice.
- **THE SCORE** — top right, in gold:

  **SCORE = CASH + MORALE × 40 + LANDLORD × 25 + STOCK × 10**

  Yes, the formula is printed in the manual. Of course it is.

## HOW TO PLAY · THE WEEK

Twelve weeks, each one the same honest shape:

1. **Read the note.** Every week tells you what's coming — a heat
   wave, Fourth of July traffic, the mid-summer flat, a broken-down
   tour bus. The traffic forecast is printed right there. Plan on it.
2. **Pick a crew of three** from nine employees. Each shows wage,
   skill, and life. Skill runs the register; wages come out
   Saturday. The life column is not decoration — see below.
3. **Order stock.** $30 a unit in, sells at $55, shelf max 12.
   Empty shelves turn customers away and the ledger says exactly
   how much money walked. Overstock in a flat week just sits.
4. **Run it.** Sales come from traffic × crew skill × morale. The
   ledger reports every line.

**Rent is $220, due weeks 4, 8, and 12.** Pay it from a solvent
drawer and Mr. Aldous warms a point. Come up broke and he cools
two.

Once a summer the county repaves the highway out front: flaggers
thin the traffic to a crawl, but a road crew eats where it works —
guaranteed lunch-counter money.

## THE CREW

Nine people, and every one of them has a life that will cross the
counter exactly once a summer — a week when the person you
scheduled needs to not be there. You'll get the choice: **cover
the shift yourself** (it costs you their wage, and the crew clocks
it — morale up) or **they work anyway** (the work is fine;
something else isn't — morale down). There is no third option
because there isn't one in real life either.

## MR. ALDOUS · read this section

The landlord is not a score ingredient you can buy back with cash.
He is a relationship, and he is the one number that can override
everything: **end the summer with LANDLORD at rock bottom and the
lease is not renewed, whatever the score reads.** The weekly
report warns you plainly when it's cooling — when he gets short
with you at the register, believe it.

## WEEK EIGHT

Week eight is week eight. It cannot be prevented; the last manager
knew that too. Nobody is hurt. The till takes the hit, and then
the real decision arrives: close for two days with everyone paid,
open at 6 AM like always, or cover the till from your own pocket.
Each answer costs something different and pays something
different. The design is what you do after. It always was.

## THE FALL REOPENING *(spoilers · read in September)*

After week twelve the reopening sign tells you what your summer
was. Checked in this order:

- **NOT RENEWED** — Mr. Aldous at 1 or less. It isn't the money —
  the money was fine. No score buys this back.
- **THE CREW'S STORE** — MORALE 7 or higher. The sign says UNDER
  SAME MANAGEMENT, painted by the crew, as a joke that isn't one.
- **THE NEW LETTERS** — CASH $900 or more. A franchise logo and a
  committee-built slogan. Head office calls you a success story.
- **SHUTTERED FOR THE SEASON** — none of the above. The sign never
  goes up. Mr. Aldous is decent about it, which is worse.

Then the final score, in gold, because we promised you a number
and RANCH keeps its promises.

## THE FINE PRINT

*This is RANCH's first game, and we made the kind we wanted to
play: one where the numbers look you in the eye. The score formula
is printed above and will not change after purchase. Any
resemblance between Mr. Aldous and any actual landlord is a
statistical inevitability rather than a legal liability. RANCH
recommends covering the shift. RANCH acknowledges you can't
always. That's the game.*
