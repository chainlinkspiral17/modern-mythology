# THE SISTERS WYRD
### Sagebrush Engineworks · 1983 · hexcrawl RPG / dark western
### four witches on a compass that lies

---

## THE BOX COPY

*The territory is a weave. You are a thread in it — a drifter
with iron on the hip, a home hex at the dead center, and a
porch you'd rather not see again too soon.*

*Four sisters keep the far corners. North, east, south, west.
The compass on this box has eight points. Nobody at this
company will discuss the other four.*

*Ride out. Deal with them. Come home. What the center opens
into depends on how you dealt.*

---

## GETTING STARTED

The Sisters Wyrd is the oldest cart on the slowstick shelf.
Choose SADDLE UP to ride out fresh, or CONTINUE THE RIDE. The
title plate keeps score across rides: each sister you deal with
sets her witch-sign at her compass point, and the center burns
only when you have seen what the center hides.

**Controls:** click any adjacent hex to ride to it, or use the
arrows / WASD. Everything else is a choice on screen; ESC hangs
your place on the pommel and backs out.

**Reading the territory:** the light rides with you — full
where you stand, dusk two hexes out, near-dark at the horizon.
The pale ring on the ground is you. Townships and her seats
are named on the map; the message log at the bottom of the
screen is your narrator, your weather-glass, and your only
honest instrument. Read it every hex.

## HOW TO PLAY · THE THREE POUCHES

The drifter carries three things and nothing else that counts:

- **GRIT** (starts at 6) · resolve. Weather bites it, bad
  choices bite it, drawing iron always bites it. **Grit at zero
  folds the territory back to your porch** — you keep nothing
  about the day but the lesson. Worse than dying. Ask anybody.
- **SILVER** (starts at 3) · blessed rounds and money. The same
  pouch, on purpose. Every silver you spend at a bar is a
  bullet you don't have, and the game will not remind you of
  this until it matters.
- **LORE** (starts at 0) · witch-knowledge, earned in
  encounters and bought in dime novels. Spent on nothing.
  **Required for unweaving.** The one thing the territory
  cannot take back off you.

Encounters fire as you ride: a dry preacher, a salt peddler,
bones that point wrong, a township that knows your name one
hex early. Some choices cost silver or lore up front — the
button shows the price. Some threads recur; the peddler's is
three parts long, and worth following to its end. And somewhere
out there is one hex whose ink contradicts its ground. A prior
owner of this cart left a note about that. Keep both.

## TOWNSHIPS · five of them keep hours

WET MERCY in the north · PROVIDENCIA east · NINEMILE south ·
COFFIN FLATS west · DOGTOOTH near home. Ride in and the
trading post opens:

| door | price | what you get |
|---|---|---|
| THE HOTEL | 2 silver | grit to full |
| THE SALOON | 1 silver | +1 grit, and a rumor pointing at the nearest undealt sister |
| THE BOOKSTALL | 2 silver | +1 lore · twice per town per ride — the dime novels are wrong in useful ways |
| THE NOTICE BOARD | — | bounties |

**Bounties are the living.** Take a notice, ride to the marked
hex (the log whispers when the trail freshens), and face a
two-choice dealing: bring them in for silver, or let it go for
lore or grit. Collect at any board. One active at a time.

## THE FOUR SISTERS

They sit at the four far corners, and an undealt sister leans
on her whole quadrant: within five hexes of her seat the north
cold bites your grit, the east light goes red and **makes the
home-distance readout lie**, the south thirst deepens every
encounter's cost, and the west dark comes early and doubles
what the dark brings. The log tells you when you're inside
weather. Believe it over the map.

At her seat, three verbs:

- **PARLEY** · she names a price and you pay it, and the price
  stays paid on the whole territory forever after. She might
  want your hat. She might want a memory. She might want your
  asking itself — and there is one sister you should parley
  LAST or not at all, and you will know her by her corner when
  the sun starts setting early.
- **DRAW** · iron and silver (needs 2 silver, costs grit either
  way, win or lose). Her quadrant wears black afterward:
  mourning weather, and town prices up where the grief is.
- **UNWEAVE** · needs lore, more of it than you'd guess. Unmakes
  her thread without malice. Her quadrant genuinely calms —
  encounters thin out, the land goes peaceable. This is the
  verb the game was built to say.

## HOME

The dead center. Ride home whenever you're done — with however
much of the territory you've settled — and hang up the iron.
The territory keeps.

Ride home with **all four sisters unwoven**, and the center
does not stay a porch.

## ENDINGS *(spoilers · read after you've hung up the iron)*

- **THE PORCH** · home with less than four dealt, or dealt by
  parley and powder. You hang up the iron anyway. The undealt
  sisters keep their weather, and the ride is written into the
  title plate: how many of four, and the manner of it.
- **THE LOOM** · home with all four unwoven. The center opens.
  The four unlabeled points of the compass are accounted for —
  seen, not explained — and the game asks its one question out
  loud. The four-note motif you heard rotated at each sister's
  seat plays all four ways at once, quietly, in tune. This is
  the most frightening thing the cartridge does, and it does it
  gently.
- **THE B-SIDE** · once the loom has been seen, the title
  screen offers READ THE B-SIDE: the same cart read the other
  way. The sisters swap corners — north for south, east for
  west — and the whole territory re-weaves under the same
  theology. Sagebrush shipped nothing else. This is the
  manual's last page read in a mirror.

## ADVICE FROM THE TRAIL

- Lore before iron. An unweave run needs the bookstalls, the
  peddler, and every choice that pays in knowing.
- Bounties fund everything. The board is the only wage the
  territory offers a drifter who won't draw.
- The saloon's rumor is the cheapest compass you'll ever buy,
  and the only one on this cart that doesn't lie.
- If the home readout stops making sense, you are in the east.
  Ride by the sun.
- Grit is spent riding INTO weather and saved riding around it.
  The long way is the short way more often than pride allows.

## THE FINE PRINT

*Sagebrush Engineworks is two people in Amarillo, Texas: one of
us programs, one of us paints. The territory in this cartridge
is not generated. It is woven, identically, every time, and the
loom does not change its mind — if your map and your ground
disagree, trust the one you can stand on. The compass rose on
the label has eight points. The game names four. The painter
painted it the way she saw it and declines, cordially, to
elaborate. Letters on this subject will be kept, and not
answered.*
