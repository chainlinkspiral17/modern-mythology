# THE EARTHMAN CHRONICLES
### Astro-Cortex Software · 1985 · turn-based CRPG
### a lost 1948 manuscript · adapted

---

## THE BOX COPY

*In January of 1946, in a Craftsman house on Orange Grove
Boulevard, Doctor Jack Whiteside — rocket-fuel chemist, student
of the older sciences — performed a specific propellant
experiment combined with an old text. The next thing he knew
was PARSA: a red desert world under two moons, one of them
dying. A lost civilization. Its four-armed warriors and its
silver-blue scholars. Its enslaved, child-shaped, ancient
people. Its Order of the Prophet.*

*Nine rituals wait. Six chapters. Six endings. One dedication.*

*Adapted from an unpublished manuscript by L. Rafayette Hubbard.*

---

## GETTING STARTED

The Earthman Chronicles lives on the slowstick shelf under
Volume 7, in the Kelait-brown sleeve. Choose NEW GAME. Before
the gate opens you will choose Jack's emphasis — CHEMISTRY,
MYSTICISM, or the SYNTHESIS between them — and the game will
hold you to it.

**Controls:** everything is a choice on screen. Move focus,
accept to choose, ESC to close the book and keep your place.
Chapter scenes play as beats with decisions; between chapters,
the city of Talikan sits open to walk. Headphones are
recommended. The back of the case tells you why.

## HOW TO PLAY · SIX CHAPTERS

1. **PASADENA, 1946** · the laboratory, the text, the working
   that goes wrong — or goes exactly right, depending on whose
   account you believe.
2. **THE APPROACH** · the red desert, and a four-armed warrior
   named Hel Velli who duels first and befriends after.
3. **TALIKAN** · the Kyrindi city. Sara Nai, the scholar who
   has been dreaming about a man named Jack for years.
4. **THE MINES** · the Kelait, the Kel-Rethani who hold them,
   and the first ritual with a cost you can't take back.
5. **THE ACADEMY** · the Order of the Prophet, a teacher named
   Rafaton, and the ninth ritual on its single old page.
6. **THE FINALE** · what Jack does with everything he is.

Your choices in every chapter log into the ending arithmetic.
Nothing is decoration. The narrator is not always your friend —
listen for when the prose is admiring the wrong things.

## TALIKAN · the hub

From Chapter 3 on, Talikan can be revisited any time from the
title screen. Eight places keep hours: Sara's library, the
music halls, Scribes' Row, the Kelait kitchen, the marketplace
(currency: shenin — Jack lands with a ritual stipend of 36),
the rooftop observatory, the undercroft, and — if you have
recruited a certain cartographer's assistant — her stall.
Talikan is where dispositions grow, where lore is bought and
traded, and where more than one thing is hidden. Walk it
between chapters. The city rewards the unhurried.

## THE PARTY

Four slots, and recruits do not leave. Hel Velli joins in
Chapter 2 if the duel goes as duels should. Sara Nai joins in
Chapter 3 and loves easily and fully. Two more may join later —
one only through a large branch of choices, and one only if you
talk to the right person and ask the right question. It matters
who is with you, and it matters what they think of you: the
game tracks every companion's disposition, and the endings read
it.

## THE WORKINGS · nine rituals

Instead of experience points, Jack climbs a ladder of nine
Workings — each a ritual with components to gather, a fast to
keep, and a power granted:

| # | working | grants |
|---|---|---|
| I | The Star Ruby | protection from panic, one battle |
| II | The Lesser Ritual of the Pentagram | banishes one hostile effect |
| III | The Bornless One | summons an ally, one battle |
| IV | The Hymn of Pan | +2 all stats, one battle |
| V | The Mass of the Phoenix | massive single-target harm · costs 1 HP, permanently |
| VI | Liber Reguli | opens the Academy path |
| VII | The Great Work | Sara Nai's true form, one battle |
| VIII | The Star Sapphire | absorbs one fatal blow |
| IX | The Babalon Working | the endgame · locks and unlocks endings |

**Your party has opinions.** Some of them will refuse to stand
in the circle for specific Workings, and they will say so out
loud, and the manual's advice is: when a friend refuses a
ritual, ask yourself what they know. The count of Workings you
perform — and the ones you decline — decides which endings can
reach you. Some you should refuse. The game means that.

## COMBAT

Turn-based boss encounters, played as overlays inside the
chapters. Jack attacks, guards, and casts his completed
Workings — the ladder you climbed is your moveset. Each
companion contributes one action per fight: Hel strikes with
everything he has, Sara's Kyrindi tone staggers, and the
cartographer's assistant *analyzes* — after which you hit half
again as hard, because knowing the weak point is most of the
fight. Defeat is never death; you are dragged clear, and the
story continues with the loss written into it.

## JACK'S CODEX

From the title screen, Jack's notebook: tabs for the nine
WORKINGS (performed ones marked), the six CORRECTIONS (see
below — found ones show their full text), the NPCS you've met
and how they currently feel, the ENDINGS you've seen, and — the
tab this studio is quietest and proudest about — the MANUSCRIPT
itself: Hubbard's 1948 typescript, page by page, with a QA
reader's blue-pen marginalia beside it. Read both columns.

## THE SIGNAL *(read this paragraph twice)*

A specific 220-Hz tone plays under specific scenes. If you have
headphones on, you might notice. There is a piece of lab
equipment Jack can build in Chapter 1 that notices for you.
Six documents are hidden behind that signal. The Codex counts
them. This manual counts them too, and says nothing further
outside the spoiler section.

## ENDINGS *(spoilers · read after the credits)*

Six, decided by Workings performed, Corrections found, and who
still loves you:

1. **THE WARLORD OF PARSA** · the ladder climbed, nothing
   found. The intended power fantasy. Watch Sara's hesitation
   at the coronation and decide who intended it.
2. **RETURN TO EARTH** · Working IX refused, some truth in
   hand. January 20, 1946, again. It buys six years. It does
   not buy more.
3. **BABALON COMES** · the ninth Working completed with the
   Scarlet Woman at your side. A new star over Los Angeles.
   A closed door. Unresolved on purpose.
4. **REFUSED THE WORK** · stop at four Workings, having found
   most of what was hidden. Jack stays. Talikan. A house built
   with someone. A funeral rite in a language you never
   learned, seventy years from now. The team that made this
   game preferred this ending, and said so.
5. **THE CORRECTION** · all six hidden documents found, the
   ladder left unclimbed past IV. The manuscript completes
   itself, in a handwriting that is not Hubbard's, and the game
   tells you who the story actually belonged to — a chemist and
   mystic who died in his own laboratory on June 17, 1952, at
   thirty-seven. The dedication reads "For Jack." This is the
   true ending.
6. **HUBBARD TAKES THE CREDIT** · the ninth Working done with
   nothing found and nobody's help. The credits roll under a
   soliloquy. Then the cartridge itself objects: PLEASE INSERT
   CORRECTED CARTRIDGE. You missed something. It missed you.

**Sara's REFUSAL:** when the ninth ritual is offered, Sara Nai
speaks first — she has been prepared to speak first since the
road. You may perform it over her word. The endings remember
whether you did.

## THE SECOND READING *(after finishing once)*

The title screen gains a new option: read the book again with
all six Corrections in hand from page one. The chapters replay
knowing what you know; the correction-aware paths are open from
the first scene; the finale knows this is the second time
through. The fastest road to the true ending — and a different
book, cover to cover.
