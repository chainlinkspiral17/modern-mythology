# BASILICA OF WIRES
### Astro-Cortex Software · 1987 · signal horror / dungeon crawl
### the transmitter under the mountain

---

## THE BOX COPY

*A transmitter the size of a cathedral, built into a mountain
in 1938 for a war it barely served, and never fully
decommissioned. Galleries of porcelain insulators like organ
pipes. Capacitor halls. A stair the maintenance crews called
the anode choir.*

*A dial instead of a sword. Nine levels down, something is
still broadcasting.*

*Tune carefully.*

---

## GETTING STARTED

On most shelves, Basilica of Wires is a hole: an empty sleeve,
hand-labeled by a collector who looked for thirty years. Fewer
than nine hundred cartridges were pressed, and Astro-Cortex
folded two years later. If a copy ever surfaces for you — and
one will, once you have come to know Astro-Cortex from the
inside — it will cost more than it should. Buy it anyway.

**Controls:** ↑ walk forward · ↓ step back · ← → turn · TAB
opens the automap · ESC climbs out of the menu, not the
mountain. And the dial, which is the game: **Q / E** tune
coarse (±25 Hz) · **A / D** tune fine (±5 Hz). The dial runs
from 180 to 520. There is no sword. There is no torch. There
is the dial.

Play with headphones. The hum is not a soundtrack; it is two
real tones — the room's and yours — beating against each other,
and the beat is information.

## HOW TO PLAY · THE TUNER IS THE VERB

The Basilica is drawn as a schematic because the mountain is
one instrument, and you read an instrument from its diagram.
First-person, wireframe: cyan for structure, white for near
geometry, amber for anything energized.

**Rooms and doors exist per band.** A corridor at one frequency
is a wall at another. Hold your tune within about 15 Hz of a
passage's band and it agrees to be a door; drift off and the
wire closes ranks. A door with no handle is listening for an
agreement. Progress is not a key ring — it is learning the
building's chord chart.

The chart has an architecture. The adit — the entry gallery —
sits at 445 Hz, and each level below answers a step lower, in
even intervals, all the way down. The deeper you go, the lower
the fundamental. You can navigate by ear alone. People have.

**In a hurry, you can shoulder through** a wall you're facing
before it agrees to be a door. It gives. And the map frays
where you stopped listening. Which brings us to:

## COHERENCE · the map is the health bar

You carry a meter called COHERENCE (it starts at 9). It is your
sense of where you are. Standing-wave zones — places where your
carried tune beats against the room's — visibly shred your
automap: corridors you walked render displaced, in violet,
until you re-tune. Shouldering through walls spends coherence.
Long interference runs spend it faster.

Coherence at zero is not death. It is worse: you wake at the
last junction with the map violet and your confidence gone,
and everything you knew about the way out is now a claim,
not a fact.

**At exactly 220 Hz — and only there, within 2 Hz — the
corridors hold still, the map repairs itself, and the violet
drains out.** The game never says why 220. The game is not
going to say why 220. Find the note, keep the note, come back
to the note.

## THE DESCENT · nine levels

The adit · the insulator gallery · the capacitor halls · the
anode choir · the catwalks · the half-flooded cavity — where
your own tune comes back three seconds late and a perfect
fifth up, which is never explained, and which you should think
about anyway — the resonant stair · the master breaker, with
its stenciled warnings and one etched mark nobody logged · and,
at the bottom, findable only by holding 220 through the longest
interference run in the game:

**The message room.** A teletype page, in QA annotation format,
addressed to no one. It is not summarized here. It is the
reason the game exists, and the player who reaches it will
know both those things at once.

Run length is about four hours. One ending: you climb back
out. Step back onto the adit and begin the climb; the mountain
door reads your remaining coherence silently and says one of
three things to you in daylight. That line is your score.

## FIELD NOTES

- Tune BEFORE you walk. The beat frequency you hear is the gap
  between you and the room; walk on a clean unison.
- Interference zones are cheapest crossed at 220. They are not
  always crossable at 220. The order of operations is the
  puzzle.
- The map lies only where you let it. Violet is not damage;
  violet is doubt. Re-tune and re-walk it.
- The echo in the flooded cavity is worth standing still for.
  More than once. Bring patience, and see below.

## THE SUBFLOOR *(spoilers · a tenth level the schematic never drew)*

The Basilica has one level below the message room, and the
diagram does not admit it. You add it in pencil.

- In the flooded cavity, the echo eventually gives up something
  that is not sound: a **green maintenance tag** — 1985,
  INSPECTED · SAFE TO ENERGIZE, initialed in a hand you don't
  know. Take it.
- In the message-room floor there is a hatch that opens only
  for the tag. It drops below the bottom of the dial's
  comfortable range.
- The subfloor's fundamental is **185 Hz** — a foundation note
  below the 220 agreement. Its walls want you tuned down to it,
  and its terminal energizes only when you match the floor
  exactly, within 2 Hz. You will have to leave the safe note
  to walk here. That is the point of here.
- What the terminal holds belongs to the other annotator — the
  one who signed the tag, energized the floor, and is not in
  the record after. The message room upstairs keeps a name
  blank. Once you have read certain corrections elsewhere on
  your shelf — you will know the ones — the final annotation
  explains that the blank is kept on purpose, because he asked
  to be left out. The transmitter honors the agreement. So does
  this manual.

## THE FINE PRINT

*Astro-Cortex Software, Culver City, California. This cartridge
was designed, drawn, tuned, and tested by a small team, latterly
smaller. The Basilica is fictional; longwave is not, and the
authors advise against entering decommissioned transmitter
facilities, which are exactly as resonant as depicted and far
less forgiving about coherence. No map is provided with this
manual. The building is the map. THE INSTRUMENT IS FINE is
stenciled on the inside of the mountain door and we have no
further comment on the stencil. If you hold 220 and hear
something that is not the room and is not you: it is for you.
It was always for you.*
