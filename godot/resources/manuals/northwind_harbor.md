haulers with cracking hands — everyone here is busy, and everyone

## GETTING STARTED

Northwind Harbor lives on the slowstick shelf under Volume 7.
Click the cartridge once to read about it, click it again (or
press BOOT) to slide it home. At the title screen, choose
**SAVE 2 · WALK THE WEEK**.

(SAVE 1 belongs to somebody named Sam, from 1988, mid-pocket,
holding a glove. It is not yours. Leave it alone.)

**The wooden signs along the bottom are how you walk.** This is
the whole game: walk, listen, and do the small thing in front of
you. The lamp-orange lines on the right are things your hands can
do. If the right side says *nothing needs hands here* — believe
it, and walk on.

## CONTROLS

| You want to | Do this |
|---|---|
| Walk to the next screen | Click a wooden sign at the bottom (or d-pad + accept) |
| Do something here | Click a lamp-orange line on the right |
| Read everything you've heard | H, or the HEARD button |
| Pet the dog | Click the dog. Once a morning. He keeps count. |
| Put the morning down | ESC (or B). The harbor keeps your place. |

## YOUR FIRST MORNING (a guided five minutes)

Do this once and you will never need this page again:

1. You wake on **Maddox's porch**. Read the line at the bottom
   left. That line — and every line anyone says — is the game
   talking to you.
2. Find the **wooden signs** along the bottom edge. One of them
   says **bosun looks this way**. The dog knows. Click it.
3. Keep following Bosun's signs until you reach the **fuel dock**.
   A man named Karl will say something about a glove, without
   looking up. You didn't press anything. That's *listening* —
   it's automatic, and it just went into your HEARD list.
4. Karl lost that glove **off the jetty**. Walk there. Bosun will
   nose at a coil of rope — a lamp-orange line appears on the
   right: **pick up: work glove**. Click it. One pocket used.
5. Walk back to the fuel dock. **give: work glove.** Watch what
   Karl does. That's the whole ceremony, and the whole game.

Now put this manual down. The bait shop lady was saying something
about a cat.

## THE CLOCK

Every morning runs 5:47 to the horns at 7:00 — and the clock only
spends when you do. Walking costs four minutes. Doing a thing
costs one. Lending a hand costs three, because hands take longer
than eyes.

There is more morning than the minutes allow. **This is not a flaw
in the morning.** Choose.

**The horn ends the morning whether or not you finished.** There
is no failing here. An errand left open keeps — things here keep —
and it carries into tomorrow, and the town notices (*"still got my
glove, then"*). The horn then reads the morning back to you.

## POCKETS

Five slots, drawn as an open coat lining. Five is five. If your
pockets are full, something must be delivered before something
else can be carried. No, you cannot get a bag. It's 1988.

## BOSUN

Uncle Maddox's black-mouth cur. Always on screen. He is the hint
system: what he sniffs, noses, or stands pointedly beside is
*live*, and the walk-sign he'd take is marked with his name.

He can be petted once per morning. The game will never ask you to.
The mornings add up anyway. Earn enough of his trust and a new
option appears at the edge of a screen: send Bosun for a plain
object one screen over — one errand of nose per morning, delivered
like the crown jewels.

## THE SEVEN MORNINGS

1. **THE GLOVE** — the breakwater gate is chained. Start small.
2. **THE FLASK** — the coffee chain. Learn the boat horns.
3. **THE SCALE** — the fish-cleaning station. You'll see.
4. **THE LETTER** — addressed to a boat that hasn't docked in years.
5. **THE ENGINE** — the CLARA B. won't start. All twelve screens.
6. **THE FOG** — visibility two screens. The walk-signs become what
   each place *sounds* like. You have been training for this since
   morning one.
7. **THE POSTER** — the shortest morning. Nothing is wrong.

## THE GOOD WEEK *(spoilers · read after the seventh horn)*

On the seventh morning you walk the dog. How he walks it depends
on six mornings of your hands, and the game says so, plainly, and
never scolds — zero pets is answered too, without grudge.

If every morning's errand closed by the seventh horn — every
chain, all week — the game quietly names it THE GOOD WEEK, and
tells you the manual lied by one week. The kindness chains are not
counted toward it. They were never for counting.

At the cannery wall there is a half-torn poster. Bosun will not go
near it. The horns blow. There is no explanation, then or ever,
and we will not be answering letters about it.

## THE FINE PRINT

*Oneironautics Inc. is a small company on a real coast, making its
first of these, whatever these turn out to be. No dogs were
hurried in the making of this cartridge. The fog is accurate. The
horns are accurate. If you finish a morning with time left over,
check your pockets; if you finish with nothing left undone, check
