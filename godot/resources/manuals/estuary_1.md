# ESTUARY
### Oneironautics Inc. · 2001 · simulation
### one lever · twelve weeks · the report card

---

## THE BOX COPY

*At the mouth of the creek there is a tide gate, and on the tide
gate there is a lever, and the lever takes three positions. Mr.
Tolonen kept the log for thirty-one years. Now the county has
handed it to you for a summer.*

*The estuary was doing this before you. Your job is to notice
what it needs, one week at a time, and to touch the lever only
when you mean it.*

---

## GETTING STARTED

Estuary lives on the slowstick shelf under Volume 7 — the small
school-library one; the shelf copy is the OPB pledge run. Boot
the cartridge and you are at the gate. Put it down mid-season and
the same water is waiting: the summer's tides are rolled once and
kept, so a continued save replays the same weather.

**Controls:** the lever is drawn on the gate housing at frame
left. Click a notch, or press **1 · OPEN**, **2 · HALF**,
**3 · CLOSED**. SPACE or ENTER (or *let the week pass*, bottom
right) turns the week. ESC puts the log down. That is everything.

## HOW TO PLAY · THE WEEK

One fixed screen for the whole game: the estuary seen from the
gate, looking seaward, always overcast. Each week you do exactly
two things — read the screen, then set the gate and let the week
pass. One line of field notebook appears at the bottom. The
screen re-renders. Twelve weeks; the week number is carved into
the gate post.

**Read before you touch:**

- **The heron is the readout.** By the gate when the estuary is
  ordinary; far out on the flat when the shrimp beds are rich;
  hunched on the piling when the estuary is hungry. Learn her
  before you learn anything else.
- **The shimmer band** at the waterline is the chum fry. Thick is
  good. Watch it in the weeks before the run.
- **The air-holes** peppering the exposed flat are the mud
  shrimp. Dense flat, deep beds.
- **The water word**, painted small on the gate housing, is this
  week's water: STORM WATER · CHOP · ORDINARY WATER · SOFT TIDE ·
  GLASS CALM. The lever means something different in storm water
  than in glass calm. The calmer the word, the more a raised gate
  gives.

## THE THREE TENANTS

- **THE FRY** have one out-migration window: **weeks 4 through
  6**. Passage each week is gate openness times the water. Miss
  the window and the cohort is stranded above the gate for the
  rest of the game — the shimmer thins and stays thin.
- **THE SHRIMP** want stability. Every CHANGE of the gate costs
  bed density; every week you leave it alone regrows it. The
  best shrimp summer touches the lever twice.
- **THE CHANNEL** wants the sea. Any openness freshens it; three
  closed weeks in a row and the water behind the gate goes flat
  and gets a smell to it.

The fry want the gate worked. The shrimp want it left alone.
Both are right. There is no setting that wins — there is only a
summer you are responsible for.

## THE REPORT CARD

At week twelve the season ends and a card prints on ruled school
stock. It grades the ESTUARY, not you:

| grade | what it reads |
|---|---|
| WATER | channel health at season's end |
| PASSAGE | how much of the cohort got out — three quarters is an A |
| PATIENCE | lever touches, counted all summer · 2 or fewer is an A, 4 a B, 7 a C |

The card is signed "the estuary."

## THE QUIET WEEKS *(spoilers · read after the card)*

- **She isn't there one week.** Work the lever while both the
  fry and the beds are poor and the heron is simply gone the
  next week — once a season, no music, nothing to click. She
  comes back the week after, low over the reeds, and does not
  look at you.
- **Week 13.** Touch the gate two times or fewer across the
  whole game and there is one more screen after the card. Night.
  No lever, no log, the full chord. One line. Then the title.
- The drone you have been hearing gains a voice for every tenant
  doing well. A thriving week 9 is a chord; a wrecked one is the
  bare drone. You were mixing the soundtrack all along.
