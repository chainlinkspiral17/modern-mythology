# THE TIDELINE
### Oneironautics Inc. · 2004 · survey walk
### one king-tide morning · twelve stations · a notebook with room for less than you'll see

*with an appendix for TIDELINE SURVEY (Meridian Heritage

## THE BOX COPY

*December, 2003. The county's annual king-tide wrack-line survey
needs volunteers, and you are one. You get a pencil, a printed
notebook, and the stretch of beach between the creek mouth and
the point — twelve stations, walked in order, once.*

*The notebook has eight ruled lines. The beach has more to say
than that. So does everything.*

---

## GETTING STARTED

The Tideline sits on the slowstick shelf under Volume 7 — Olaf's
copy, which arrived on Ines Rocha's own insistence via a mutual
friend. Three slots down sits Tem's copy of the 2048 remake; see
the appendix. Everything is a choice on screen; ESC puts the
pencil down and the walk keeps your place, station to station.

## HOW TO PLAY · THE STATIONS

You walk the twelve stations in order. At each one: a paragraph
of what is there, then the observable things — a dead gull
mid-disassembly by live ones, a float twenty years at sea, the
wrack line itself doing something the manual has a code for and
the beach doesn't. For each you may **RECORD** (one ruled line,
in your hand, forever) or **WALK ON**.

**The notebook is the game.** Eight ruled lines for the entire
walk, and never more than two usable at any one station. Lines
you don't spend stay blank forever — the notebook keeps your
restraint as faithfully as your attention. What you choose to
rule down is who you were on this beach.

## THE TIDE CLOCK

It is a king-tide morning and the water is coming back. You set
off at 6:40 AM on the low slack, with the whole beach out. The
turn comes about half an hour in; then the flood; then the king
flood, when the beach becomes a shrinking country. The clock and
the water's mood print at the top of every station.

Everything costs minutes: **walking on costs six, each recorded
line costs three, and watching costs eight — more than anything
else on the beach, which is the point.**

Some of what is out here lives inside the tide's windows. Hurry
and you will reach things the water has not brought yet; linger
and the low beach goes back under with its floats and its
tidepools still on it. What you missed prints anyway, in faint
pencil, as a line you can read and cannot record. The notebook
keeps those too, in its way.

## THREE MARKS FOR THE MARGIN

- **Station 6 · the bell buoy.** The notebook is pre-printed:
  *buoy, bell, tone (A3 · 220 Hz)*. The notebook is right. That
  is all this manual will say about it.
- **The same gull works the whole beach.** So does the seal.
  Some things recur; the lines evolve if you keep choosing them.
- **Station 11 offers a third thing that is not an observation.**
  It spends every line you have left at that station and writes
  nothing, and it costs more minutes than anything else. Some
  entries are not entries.

## THE REPORT · station twelve

At the point, the survey reads your notebook back as a
coastline — your lines, assembled in order, and then a register
named from what you attended to. It also reads your pace: the
walk knows whether you finished ahead of the flood, walked the
last stations with the water at your boots, or reached the
point when it was an island of minutes. The survey does not
dock marks for cutting it fine. The sea keeps its own
attendance.

## THE REGISTERS *(spoilers · read after handing in the notebook)*

- **THE LIVING SHORE** — your lines leaned to what was alive.
- **THE SHORE OF LOST THINGS** — your lines leaned to what the
  sea had taken and given back.
- **THE LINE ITSELF** — your lines leaned to the wrack line and
  its codes.
- **THE WHOLE BEACH** — six lines or more, spread evenly across
  all three. The hardest register to walk on eight lines.

A notebook handed in blank is also a reading of the beach. The
last line is always: *Filed with the county. Read, later, by
exactly one person, carefully.*

## APPENDIX · TIDELINE SURVEY (Meridian Heritage Interactive, 2048)

*The following is reproduced from the Meridian Heritage product
literature, per license.*

**TIDELINE SURVEY** brings a beloved classic of the Oneironautics
catalog to modern standards. All twelve original stations have
been faithfully preserved and instrumented. At each station the
onboard sensor suite logs every available observation instantly
and without user error — typical station capture: 4 observations,
0.3 seconds, confidence 99.2% — leaving you free to CONTINUE at
your own pace. A live metrics strip tracks coverage and
efficiency throughout your visit. Total walk time: approximately
three minutes. Nothing is missed.

Please note: at Station 11, one manual interaction from the 2004
release has been preserved unaltered, as required — *HERITAGE
COMPLIANCE · original interaction preserved per license §4(c)*.
We apologize for the interruption to the automated experience.

Your completed report — SURVEY COMPLETE · 41/41 · NOTHING WAS
MISSED — files under the register THE COMPLETE RECORD, and may
be archived on exit.

*Owners of both cartridges are invited to walk the same beach
twice and compare reports. The publisher of this manual declines
to characterize the difference, except to note that the 2004
notebook had eight lines because limits test poorly, and that a
sensor cannot put the pencil down.*

## THE FINE PRINT

*Oneironautics Inc. supplied the pencil; the beach was already
there. This cartridge contains twelve stations, eight ruled
lines, one bell buoy whose tone is exactly what the notebook
says it is, and no score. The tide tables are accurate for a
December that does not exist. If you finished with blank lines
and no regrets, the survey worked. Somebody will read your
report carefully. It may take them until 2016.*
