# COMMUNITY PLANNED
### the Planned Community inset · one summer · one board

---

## WHAT IT IS

Frasier Temple sits over a board of regions and routes a network of
human agents and demons to suppress multiplying problems — while
planting a fragile foothold that keeps getting swatted down. The
game is set across the middle of the summer that *Planned
Community* (Volume 6) depicts: the book's part one, *Summer's
Start*, closes on a long pause; the game IS that middle; part two,
*End of Summer Begins*, waits on the other side.

The game adds flavor to the saga but does not author it. The novel
proceeds exactly as written no matter how your summer goes. What
playing earns you instead are interludes — vignettes, transcripts,
dossier excerpts — a shelf of extra reading beside the canon.

## GETTING THERE

COMMUNITY PLANNED unlocks in the GALLERY when you reach the seam
between the book's two parts. There is no right order: read the
whole book then play, play at the seam, interleave a week of game
with a chapter of prose, or skip the game entirely — the book
reads cleanly in every case, and the shelf stays unlocked if you
change your mind years later.

## THE DAY

A turn is a day. The summer is one hundred of them, Memorial Day
to Labor Day. Each day: returning dispatches resolve, problems
tick and escalate, new ones spawn, you send out up to three
agents, and you press **Advance Day** — the one filled amber
button on the screen, because it is the game's one primary verb.
Six quiet days of running the board, then Sunday night, when
everything decompresses onto the BBS.

The game opens narrow and unfolds. Trust that: regions, agents,
meters, and even what the numbers mean are revealed over the first
weeks, at the pace the story wants you to have them.

## THE REGIONS

Four file folders, each with its own colored spine:

- **GRAUSTARK** — the town. Dusk rooflines and the riverboat
  memory. The board's center of gravity.
- **HARMONY CREEK** — the subdivision. Seven identical gables;
  the repetition is the region.
- **SMALL WOOD** — the front. A night treeline, and above it the
  tower. This is the foothold that keeps getting swatted down.
- **THE COUNTY SEAT** — opens late in the summer, and it is the
  only region where the palette is offense, not defense: records
  requests, filing deadlines, a registry pull whose answer is a
  matryoshka. Paper is its own binding — demons hate certified
  mail.

## AGENTS · HUMANS AND DEMONS

Your roster is people and demons, and the game never lets you
forget which is which.

**Humans** carry obligation. Every dispatch costs them something
at home, and the thresholds are written in words, not numbers —
you will know what obligation 3 means because the game tells you
what stopped happening at their house. Quiet weeks at home heal.

**Demons** carry corruption, in named tiers: steady, hungry,
restless, close to turning, turned. A hungry demon can leave a
signature on a successful dispatch. The tiers have voices; the
crossings are announced. Manage the roster's appetite or the
roster manages you.

The roster grows over the summer. Some recruits arrive through
choices; some potential partners will simply refuse you, with a
voice — a no is content here.

## DISPATCHES · MISSIONS · STAGES

Problems appear in a region's folder with a severity color you can
read from across the room. Dispatching an agent starts a mission —
and most missions are staged: at each stage you make a real choice,
and the choice, not a die roll, resolves the problem. Some choices
are only available if you have read the right BBS thread — the
board and the terminal are one game, and reading is load-bearing.

You can send a **pair**: a second agent rides along, and the picker
previews how the two get on before you commit — warm, loud, or
cold, with real costs either way.

Neglected problems escalate, and escalations chain: dock rot
becomes grief, buyout letters become surveillance. By mid-summer
the board stops repeating and starts compounding — severity four
with six households at one kitchen table is the late-summer weight
class. Pressure arrives in marked waves: the deepening weeks, the
mid-summer crisis era, the late-summer squeeze around day 75, and
the approach to Labor Day. The storm week, when it comes, is the
reward for having paid attention all summer — not the punishment.

## THE BBS

Sunday night, the strategic board yields to a phosphor-green
terminal — fully diegetic, navigated with single letters: M for
mail, G for glossary, N to dial a new number, D to dial again, B
back, Q to hang up.

- **Boards and sysops.** Each BBS has its own sysop with an
  unmistakable register. Posts that break a board's voice are
  posts you learn to distrust.
- **DMs.** Named people write to you. Replies have consequences —
  some deferred, arriving on the board days later.
- **Hidden boards.** Seven-digit dial numbers are planted in
  plain sight inside public posts. Dial one and see.
- **The glossary.** Unlocks mid-summer and retroactively makes
  weeks of seemingly-flavor posts legible. The patient player
  spends a whole Sunday night re-reading.
- **Thread gating.** What you have read opens mission choices on
  the strategic board. Amber highlights mean something new was
  discovered; orange means cover was spent or a burn landed.

## THE TOWER

Small Wood's banner shows the treeline at night, and the tower
above it has four states: dim, warming, bright, white. It is the
only legible reading you get of what Dean is doing, and it is the
game's central dread rendered as a picture that brightens rather
than a word that changes. Watch it. Everything you choose not to
deal with feeds it.

## DEAN'S LEDGER

Anomalies and fingerprints accumulate as you observe the summer's
wrongness. Collect enough and the shelf offers first the napkin,
then the Ledger — the closest thing to an answer the summer will
give you before the book does. This is where the interlude shelf
earns its place beside the novel.

## SAVES

Three slots. The slot picker opens every session so you can keep
separate summers, and the game remembers which slot you left off
in. Saves are never broken by updates — old summers load clean.

After the Labor Day finale has played on a slot, that slot gains
**SEPTEMBER AND AFTER**: an endless continuation on the same
board, seasonless, with the spawn ratchet slowly rising. The
tower becomes the run's health bar — endless has no finale to
absorb a white tower, so white ends the run, with a ledger of
weeks survived, problems resolved, and the brightness curve drawn
as a sparkline. Your finished campaign save stays untouched and
replayable; retire the board voluntarily whenever the summer has
said what it needed to.

---

## A CLOSING NOTE

COMMUNITY PLANNED is a game about triage, which means it is a game
about what you let go of. The board will not be clean on Labor
Day; it is not supposed to be. Send people you can afford to
spend, read more than you think you have time for, and look at the
tower before you look away. The book already knows how the summer
ends. The game is for knowing what it cost.
