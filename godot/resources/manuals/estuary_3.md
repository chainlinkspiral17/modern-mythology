# ESTUARY 3
### Oneironautics Inc. · 2003 · four acts · four games
### the last summer the Kwik Stop was open

---

## THE BOX COPY

*It starts as a job. Twelve nights behind the counter of a
Kwik Stop on the coast highway, 2:14 to 5:14 AM, ringing up
truckers and tourists and one customer who sits in the corner
booth and never orders anything.*

*It does not stay a job. We are not going to tell you what it
becomes. The seams will give when they give.*

---

## GETTING STARTED

Estuary 3 is the first stick on the cabin shelf under Volume 7 —
the one Tem tells you to try first. Boot it and choose NEW
SUMMER, or CONTINUE THE SUMMER; the game saves every night and
at every act join. Below NEW SUMMER, in smaller type, sits
**NEW SUMMER · MANAGER MODE** — read that section before you
touch it.

**Controls change per act, because the game does.** Each act
teaches its own. ESC always backs you out to the shelf with
your place kept.

## ACT ONE · THE SHIFT

Twelve nights, fifteen turns each. One room: the counter. Six
verbs on the bar — **L**ook at · pick up (**T**) · **U**se ·
**O**pen · o**P**erate · tal**K** to — click a verb, click a
thing. The clock ticks whether you act or not.

Worth your attention across the twelve nights: Mr. Aandahl's
scratcher, every night, because on night eleven it lands. The
kid on the bike, who speaks for the first time on night five.
The other clerk from the shell station up the road, until night
seven, when the shell station closes at midnight. The phone,
which rings three or four times a night and is not always
anybody. The radio — six stations; 1150 AM reads the fishing
tides, and you will want to have been listening later. And the
2 AM customer in the corner booth, who never orders, and who on
night twelve stands up and opens the backroom door.

The door was never locked.

## ACT TWO · THE ESTUARY

Inside the backroom is not a backroom. A year over the estuary,
top-down, four seasons and a second spring. Each season you set
three things: the **tide gate** (open · partial · closed), the
**riparian buffer** (minimal · moderate · wide), and **two
species boosts** from the roster. Each season has weather, a
narrative beat, and a soft target the landscape will tell you
about after — the season settles or the season strains.

The register tape from Act One sits foldable in the corner of
the map. Pull it up. Your nights at the counter seeded this
landscape, receipt by receipt, and the Kwik Stop is a small
yellow square at the top right with Sam still in it.

At the second spring the screen dims to one question: *Sam is
on shift tonight. Should Sam keep working here next summer?*
Answer it. The game does not end. The answer is Act Three's
opening frame.

## ACT THREE · THE TOWN

Labor Day morning, 08:32, four hours of sleep. You are Sam, and
you walk the town — nine places on a hub map, fifteen minutes
of the day per visit, hard stop at 19:30: the Kwik Stop in
daylight, the closed shell station, the pier, the mill office,
the bookstore, the Aandahl house, the school, the tide gate,
and the cabin road you cannot walk down. The night people are
out here in the light. Each conversation weighs the answer you
gave at the second spring. At dusk you return to the Kwik Stop,
where Jules is behind the counter, and the keys change hands in
one direction or the other.

## ACT FOUR

The fourth act does not say what kind of game it is, and
neither will we. You will need one key — SPACE, once per bar,
in time with the drone — and a beach, and the patience to find
out what the timing of your pressing draws. The tide is coming
in much faster than the world's. Things will come and
look at what you make. What remains of the summer is its shape.

## MANAGER MODE

The Kwik Stop half of this game was, famously, a failed
management sim before it was welded to an estuary. The
management sim is still in there. MANAGER MODE turns it on:

- **The till opens at $200.** Three cooler shelves — sodas,
  energy drinks, OJ and milk — stocked twelve deep on night
  one. Restocking refills four at a time.
- **Customers now have want lists, prices, and patience**
  (twenty seconds to nearly a minute, depending on who).
  Pull their items, get to the register, OPERATE. A clean ring
  adds the sale and a tip. A timeout is a walkout — not a fail
  state, a data point. The tape remembers.
- **Every night rolls a condition:** clear · rain (patient
  customers, empty lot) · county fair (nobody has time) ·
  shipment delay (coolers open at eight-and-eight-and-eight) ·
  full moon (people tip like it's Christmas) · a sheriff
  check-in · a counterfeit twenty. Same summer, same seed —
  a replayed summer rolls the same nights.
- **Guests.** Five rare customers walk in maybe a night in
  three, never on night twelve, never in the rain: a regional
  manager, three high-schoolers, a lost couple, a biker who
  tips like he means it, and the church lady. Serve them clean
  and the summer keeps a page about it.
- **Act Two reads your tape.** Four confident nights ($8 or
  more in tips) and the estuary gives you a third species-boost
  slot each season. Four stressed nights (three or more
  walkouts) and help comes harder all year.

## YEAR TWO

Finish a Manager Mode summer all the way to one of its own
endings, then boot Manager Mode again: the game notices, and
the second summer starts where the first one left you — the
stock in the cooler, the count in the till, and the opening
words on night one are all inherited from how it ended. Year
Two also puts one new resolution on the table at the second
spring that no first summer can offer. You will know it when
you have earned it.

## THE ENDINGS *(spoilers · finish a summer first)*

The second-spring question always offers three answers: **Yes** ·
**No** · **The question is wrong.** Each seeds a different
epilogue, and the epilogue is finished by the shape of your Act
Four line — the game composes the two together on the last
screen.

Manager Mode can add up to three more, each with a threshold
the tape must actually show:

- **Buy out Jules.** Ring $4,200 across the summer, cash and
  tips together. Five years later the name-tag says MANAGER.
- **The clean ledger.** Twelve nights, zero walkouts. The
  summer where Sam let no one down, at a cost Sam does not
  admit to Jules.
- **Close the store. Walk out.** Three or more walkouts on
  three consecutive nights. Not a bad ending — a legible one.
  Sam leaves a note at 3:47 AM and comes back the next night.

And in Year Two only: **Train the next one. Hand it on.** The
one thing the first summer couldn't offer — you have done this
long enough to give it away. Reyes runs it five years on,
waving off a short quarter exactly the way Jules taught Sam.

None of these is the right one.

## THE FINE PRINT

*Oneironautics Inc. spent six months building a convenience
store, three months admitting it wasn't fun, and one Labor Day
weekend discovering what it was for. This cartridge contains
four games, one of which we will not name, and a door that was
never locked. Reviewers are asked to play past night twelve
before filing. The Kwik Stop's prices reflect 1998 coastal
Oregon and are not a commentary. The estuary, as always, was
here first.*
