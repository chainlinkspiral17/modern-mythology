# HANE NO NIWA · 羽の庭
### Yumemi Denshi, Kyoto · 1993 · shrine-keeping · epistolary
### four seasons · a neglected shrine · letters from something unseen

---

## THE BOX COPY

*There is a shrine on the hill above the town. Twenty-three
steps. Nobody climbs them anymore, which is not the same as
nobody being there.*

*Sweep. Mend. Offer. Sit. One letter will be left for you at the
end of each season, written in exactly the language you have
taught the hill to speak. You will never see who writes them.
You will be seen.*

*(This copy is the 1995 grey import. A photocopied fan
translation is folded into the case, annotated in two hands. The
notes below follow it where the printed manual runs out.)*

---

## GETTING STARTED

Hane no Niwa surfaces on the slowstick shelf under Volume 7 —
once you have reason to know it exists. Boot the cartridge and
CLIMB THE HILL, or CONTINUE a kept season; the shrine remembers
you between visits. ESC comes back down the steps and keeps your
place.

The screen is a hanging scroll: a tall center panel, wide paper
margins. The margins are used — season, visit count, actions,
and the feather tally live there, written downward.

## HOW TO PLAY · THE VISIT

**Four seasons × nine visits.** Each visit begins in the town at
the hill's foot — one menu, not a location — where you may bring
one thing up, or nothing. Then the hill: eight spots (the torii,
the steps, the cistern, the offering shelf, the honden door, the
camphor tree, the fox statues, the brazier). Click a spot to
stand at it. **Four actions a visit**, then LEAVE.

- **SWEEP** — the steps, all twenty-three. The sweeping holds a
  few visits, then the leaves come back. The broom knows them by
  now.
- **MEND** — re-twist the shimenawa where it frays, set the
  chipped fox's pebble back. Small repairs. The rope frays back
  slowly. The shrine notices small.
- **OFFER** — offerings go on the shelf; that is what a shelf is.
  See below. This verb is the language.
- **SIT** — at any spot. Sitting spends an action and gives
  nothing you can count. Sit anyway.
- **GATHER** — some spots, some seasons, leave something on the
  hill worth picking up. What is there changes with the season.

The shrine keeps a quiet upkeep it never shows as a number. It
shows it other ways: the steps read swept or unswept, the rope
whole or frayed — and at high upkeep, the chipped fox's
expression changes. Something around the mouth. Approving.
Players argue about whether that is real. *(margin note, first
hand: "it is.")*

## OFFERING · THE LANGUAGE

**Twenty offerable things** — ten bought in the town, ten found
on the hill in their seasons. Anything offered enters the unseen
one's lexicon: offer a persimmon and it can speak of sweetness;
offer a comb, of untangling; offer sake, of warmth and of your
grandfather. Offer nothing all season and that season's letter
is shorter and gentler than any other.

What you give it is what it has words for. There is no right
gift. There is only what you chose, read back to you.

## THE LETTERS

At each season's last visit, a letter waits on the shelf, under
a feather — black, then grey, then white. The letter is composed
from your own offerings: what you gave, how you kept the place,
whether you gave at all. Fold it carefully. The game keeps every
letter you have been left.

The fourth letter closes the year. We will not describe it here,
except to say the fan translation's margins go quiet before it
too.

## THE ENDINGS *(spoilers · read after winter)*

The fourth letter takes its shape from the whole year:

- **A letter of weather** — the offerings skewed to seasons,
  rain, leavings.
- **A letter of food and small comforts** — sweetness, warmth,
  the rice ball, the candle.
- **A letter of grief** — the offerings skewed to belongings:
  the photograph, the old letter, the worn coin.
- **The true ending** — a broad lexicon, the shrine well kept,
  three letters already on the shelf: the fourth page comes
  BLANK, under a **gold feather**. The game files it as the only
  letter that says everything.

Whatever its shape, the fourth letter contains one sentence in
English, uncommented, spelled the way it is spelled. *(margin
note, second hand: "she spelled it like that on purpose. ask her
yourself.")*

Then spring again. The honden door — which never opens — is open
exactly one pixel wider. It never opens more.

Sit at all eight spots across the year and the shrine offers a
ninth seat: the top step, facing down the hill, the way the
foxes face. And a keepsake from a certain traveling faire, if
you carry one, is known here also — the summer letter will say
so.

## THE FINE PRINT

*Yumemi Denshi thanks you for your visits. The shrine in this
program is not a real shrine, but it is kept the way real ones
are kept, which is to say: by whoever climbs the steps. The
cicadas are mixed a little loud on purpose; you will understand
on the first visit of autumn. Yumemi Denshi accepts no
responsibility for what the fox statues do or do not do with
their faces. The door does not open. The door has a way of not
opening that feels like attention.*
