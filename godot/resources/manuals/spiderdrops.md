### Pretty Dreams · Beaverton, OR · 1993 · arcade · physics puzzle
### one spider · one web · one storm · REAL PHYSICS!

## THE BOX COPY

*It's raining! It's pouring! Your web is ENORMOUS and the storm
wants it DOWN! You are the spider — crawl the strands, shake off
the raindrops, brace for the big gusts, and spin back what
snaps! How long can YOU keep the web up?*

*Featuring REAL PHYSICS! Every strand stretches, sags, and
snaps for real. Every drop lands where it lands and rolls where

## GETTING STARTED

Spiderdrops shows up on the slowstick shelf under Volume 7 —
once you have learned that Pretty Dreams made real games. Boot
the cartridge and take the storm. A whole storm runs five to
eight minutes, one sitting; the save keeps your best result and
your run count. ESC climbs off the web mid-storm.

**Controls:** arrows / d-pad to MOVE · Space (accept) to PLUCK ·
hold SHIFT to BRACE · S to SPIN.

## HOW TO PLAY · THE WEB

The web is real: a center hub, **eight radial spokes**, four
rings of soft capture spiral, the outer ring anchored to
branches at the storm frame. Spoke silk is the structure — it is
tougher. Spiral silk is the soft stuff — it breaks first. Any
strand stretched too far past its rest length SNAPS. A node that
loses every strand falls away into the rain.

**Water is mass.** Raindrops fall from the top of the storm,
land with a splash, and the strand visibly catches the weight.
Pooled water grows, and — REAL PHYSICS! — it **flows down the
strands** and pools at the bottom of the web. Heavy drops hang
low and stretch. Weight is sag, sag is tension, tension is
snapping. The pooled water at the bottom is the slow killer.

**Debris is worse.** Leaves and twigs fly in sideways on the
gusts and stick, adding weight AND wind drag to whatever they
land on.

## THE SIX GUSTS

The storm arrives in **six numbered gusts**, each bigger than
the last, each telegraphed: a lull, a hum, a pulsing arrow
showing the incoming direction — then the hit, from that
direction, longer and harder every wave. Then a recovery lull
(the rain does NOT take the lull off; it pools harder every
wave). Reading the arrow and getting ready IS the game.

## THE FOUR VERBS

- **MOVE** — crawl to a connected node. Your own weight rides
  with you: sitting on a strained node makes it worse; a slack
  one carries you free. Position is survival.
- **PLUCK** — sharply shed the pooled water on your node and its
  neighbors, and shake loose stuck debris. Short cooldown. The
  realest verb against the slow drowning — and since water runs
  downhill, plucking the low drops is usually the right play.
- **BRACE** (hold) — pin your node solid and stiffen its strands
  against the gust. Brace the RIGHT node during the telegraph
  and you save the most silk. Bracing drains **stamina**;
  stamina refills in the lulls, twice as fast as under fire.
- **SPIN** — re-spin one snapped strand back onto your current
  node. Costs **silk** (about a fifth of the pouch per strand);
  silk regenerates slowly, faster in the lulls. The only way to
  rebuild what the storm takes.

Silk and stamina never refill fast enough to do everything every
wave. You triage. That is not a flaw in the plan. That is the
plan.

## SCORING

Every second, you score for every strand still up — and intact
spokes count triple. A web kept whole early is worth more than a
heroic rebuild late, but a heroic rebuild late is worth the
watching.

## HOW IT ENDS *(spoilers)*

If the hub is lost, or the web falls to two spokes or fewer, the
run ends there. Survive all six gusts and the run ends anyway —
and either way the game reads the web's final shape back to you:

- **THE WEB HELD** — 7–8 spokes standing, most of the spiral
  kept (60% or more). You kept it whole.
- **HELD** — you survived, in between. Tattered but standing.
- **THE EIGHT-POINTED STAR** — 7–8 spokes, almost no spiral
  (20% or less). The storm took everything soft and left only
  the frame: eight points. The game says nothing about the
  figure. Neither will we.
- **THE STORM** — the hub lost, or the spokes gone. This is not
  the losing screen. Read it again. The storm was always going
  to win eventually; the game only ever asked what shape the web
  was in when it went, and how long you held it.

Certain shapes are remembered beyond this cartridge.
