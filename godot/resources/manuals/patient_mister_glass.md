# THE PATIENT MISTER GLASS
### RANCH · 2004 · SLOW DETECTIVE
### fourteen evenings · one suspect · one kitchen

---

## THE BOX COPY

*$31,000 is missing from the Northwind–Anchor Bay ferry accounts,
1971 to 1974. The clerk of record is A. GLASS, retired, age
seventy-one. The file is thirty years old and weighs nothing.*

*He agreed to the interviews by return post. His handwriting is
very steady. He will let you in, he will cook, and he will answer
every question you ask. That is the problem.*

---

## GETTING STARTED

The Patient Mister Glass lives on the slowstick shelf under Volume
7. OPEN THE FILE starts a fresh case; the game saves after every
evening, and CONTINUE picks up at the next drive out. The weather
for the whole case is rolled when the file opens and stays rolled
— some evenings it rains, and reloading will not dry them out.

**Controls:** the question deck is the column on the right — focus
with arrows or d-pad, accept to ask. OPEN THE LEDGER (also L, or Y
on a pad) opens the ledger. ESC leaves the kitchen. When you're
done for the night, let him get back to his kitchen.

## HOW TO PLAY · THE EVENINGS

Thirteen evenings in the kitchen, then the fourteenth, which is
different. Each evening you may ask **three questions**. Twelve
questions stand on the deck all case long — the fare box, the
auditor, the Anchor Bay run, his late wife, why he never left —
and three more are locked, because they cannot be asked cold.

**The same question does not return the same answer.** Ask about
the fare box on evening 2 and evening 11 and you get rotations:
the anecdote picks up a detail, drops a name, moves a date. What
changes the answer:

- **The evening.** Some answers only exist early. Some only late.
- **What he's cooking.** The menu is October on the coast —
  chowder week, the bread days, jam at the end — and it modulates
  his candor. The oven has opinions about timing.
- **Rain.** Rain, when it comes, changes what he remembers.
- **Trust.** Built by which questions you DON'T press. Ask two or
  fewer in an evening and he warms. Some answers will not come out
  until he's willing to leave the pot to talk.

Ask a question past its last variant and he gives you the answer
he's already given, softer, watching to see if you noticed.

## THE LEDGER

The core mechanic. Everything he says is kept, dated by evening.
Open the ledger and **pin any two answers against each other**:

- A **true contradiction** becomes a FINDING. There are nine.
  Certain findings unlock the locked questions.
- A **false pin** — a rotation, not a contradiction — costs you an
  evening of trust. He saw you take the bait. From across the
  kitchen, without turning around: *"find it?"*

The findings are the case. What you can file at the end is what
you found, and nothing else.

## A NOTE ON SITTING QUIETLY

Nothing requires you to spend all three questions. Nothing
requires you to spend any. The manual will say no more than that.

## EVENING FOURTEEN *(spoilers · read after the verdict)*

The county wants a box checked. What boxes exist depends on your
ledger:

- **ACCUSE** — available with at least one finding on file.
  Recovery proceedings against a man with sixty-one dollars in
  savings. The winter card shows the kitchen dark.
- **CLEAR** — always available. You falsify nothing, exactly; you
  file the findings in the order that makes them weather instead
  of evidence. He thanks you at the door, and both of you know.
- **CLOSE THE LEDGER** — only if you have found THE LEDGER
  BALANCES. The third thing, the one with no box on the form.

The solution is fixed, discoverable, and mundane: the money is
real and Glass took it. From 1976 to 1979 it went, in cash, in
envelopes, to four families up the coast, after the Camp Sweetgum
summer collapsed the season and the settlement was denied. He
clerked the ferry that carried those families to the hearings. He
kept a second ledger of every envelope, and it balances to the
cent, and he has been waiting thirty years for someone to ask.

The game grades nothing. But only one verdict makes the drive-home
theme resolve — and only one plays it in the kitchen, very
quietly, and lets you notice who has been humming it all along.

## THE FINE PRINT

*RANCH thanks you for your patience, which this cartridge will
spend slowly and on purpose. No timers were used in the making of
this mystery. The kitchen is drawn once and lit fourteen ways; we
counted. If you finish in fewer than fourteen evenings you have
made a mistake somewhere, probably in the ledger, possibly in
yourself. The kettle is red because something had to be.*
