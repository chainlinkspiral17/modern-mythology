# MRS. WU'S GARDEN
### Oneironautics Inc. · 2003 · garden tending · neighbor story
### one backyard · nine beds · one fall · the frost is coming

---

## THE BOX COPY

*Corvallis, Oregon, fall 1995. You are the new neighbor. In your
first week Mrs. Wu — eighty-one, funny in the spare way — knocks
on your door with a plate of moon cakes and a request phrased as
an observation: "you have good evenings free."*

*Behind her house: nine raised beds, and a person sewn into every
one of them. Six evenings, October into November. The radio says
the frost is coming. The sheets only cover so much.*

---

## GETTING STARTED

Mrs. Wu's Garden lives on the slowstick shelf under Volume 7.
Boot the cartridge and take the gloves, or pick up a saved fall
where you left it — the garden keeps your place after every
evening. ESC (or B) sets the trowel down and keeps your place.

**Controls:** everything is a button on screen. D-pad or arrows to
move focus, accept to choose. F4 hides the overlays if you want a
clean picture of the dusk.

## HOW TO PLAY · THE EVENING

Six evenings, October 3 to November 7. **Three actions an
evening** — "more than three and you are gardening at the garden.
It notices."

- **TEND a bed** (1 action): meet the need it shows tonight —
  water · weed · mulch · cut back · tie up · just look. ("Just
  look" is a real instruction and it takes the whole minute.) A
  tended bed steadies. Beds carry a quiet condition the text
  describes in words, never numbers: *thriving · holding ·
  struggling · gone quiet.* Miss a bed two evenings running and
  it declines. A bed gone quiet cannot be tended back.
- **SIT** (1 action): tea on the porch. One story per evening,
  keyed to whichever bed you tended last — the garden is the
  index of her life. Sitting is mechanically "wasted" and is the
  entire point. Sit before tending anything and she approves of
  that most of all.
- **WASH A SHEET** (1 action): see THE LINEN CHEST. Only works in
  drying weather.

She hums while she works — three notes in waltz time, over and
over. Sit with her three or more evenings and you will hear the
whole thing.

## THE SKY THAT EVENING

Every evening arrives with its own weather, and the sky rewrites
the work:

- **Dry and clear** — good drying weather. The only evenings
  laundry dries on the line.
- **Wind off the gorge** — the dahlias and the peas are pulled at
  by their own height. Tie them up TONIGHT or they take damage at
  dusk; the wind does not wait two evenings.
- **Rain since noon** — nothing wants water tonight (the rain
  does the watering for free), but the slugs have come up for the
  bok choy like it's a dinner invitation. Slug patrol TONIGHT or
  the practical rows host them until dawn.
- **Clear and cold** — the last of the drying weather, says the
  radio, before the real cold.

## THE LINEN CHEST

The frost will come, and the sheets only cover what you have
ready. You start the fall with **two sheets** in the chest. On a
drying evening, WASH A SHEET spends one action and adds one to
the chest — wash at the outside tap, pin it on the line while the
light lasts. The chest holds **five**. It holds five.

**On frost night, your actions ARE your sheets.** Every sheet
washed in October is a bed saved on Halloween.

## THE NINE BEDS

Gong's dahlias · the coffee-can camellia · Marcus's pumpkin · the
practical rows of bok choy · the anniversary rose · the
impossible fig · the November chrysanthemums · the calendar
gamble of late peas · the moon garden. Every one is somebody's.
Tend a bed, then sit, and you'll learn whose.

One of them belongs to a boy two doors down who has never once
watered it. **Water it for him — twice, across the fall — and see
who comes to the fence on the coldest night.** And if you spent a
summer at a certain camp once, someone else may stop at the fence
too. The garden says no more about it.

## EVENING 5 · THE FROST WARNING

October 31. The radio says tonight, hard, mid-twenties. Mrs. Wu
has the sheets out on the porch rail, folded, counted, not
enough. She will not choose — "they are all somebody's."

One sheet covers one bed. The frost screen tells you what it can:
which beds *want a sheet*, and which are *hardy · may stand it* —
the woody, established things can survive bare **if they're
holding or better**. Everything uncovered and tender takes the
frost hard; what was already struggling goes black. The triage is
the whole game: what you cover is what you have learned to love,
and there is not enough of you.

## EVENING 6 · AFTER

The walk-through, two cups already poured. What was covered
stands. What was strong stood it bare. What was neglected went
black at the edges — she touches it once and moves on. Then the
ending, read from the fall you actually had.

## THE ENDINGS *(spoilers · read after the frost)*

- **THE GARDEN GOES ON** — 7+ beds alive AND 3+ stories heard.
  The spring flier, and your name on the back in her hand.
- **HALF-SLEPT** — 4–6 beds alive. "Half a garden is a garden.
  The other half is spring's problem."
- **A GOOD NEIGHBOR ANYWAY** — fewer beds, but you sat 3+
  evenings. "Gardens die back. Company doesn't."
- **THE FLIERS STAY IN THE DRAWER** — otherwise. The gate stays
  unlatched all winter, is the thing.

Bring all nine beds through the frost and the catalog remembers
it. Hear the whole melody and you may notice you have heard it
somewhere before. You will hear it somewhere again.

## THE FINE PRINT

*Mrs. Wu's Garden was made in stolen evenings and it shows, on
purpose. Oneironautics Inc. thanks you for your three actions,
and reminds you that the fliers were free, which is why the wrong
date was kept. No slugs were reasoned with. The camellia cleared
customs in 1947 as BREAKFAST and we see no cause to reopen the
matter. Tie like you might be wrong.*
