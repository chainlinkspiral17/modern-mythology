### Pretty Dreams · Beaverton, OR · 1991 · music · instrument
### twelve club meetings · one wonderful machine · your song at the end

*You got a PD Riffrocker for your birthday! (We assume. Why else

MARCIE and TODD meet every week to play the Riffrocker, and there

## GETTING STARTED

Riffrocker Melody Club lives on the slowstick shelf under Volume
7. Boot the cartridge and JOIN THE CLUB · MEETING ONE, or
CONTINUE — the club saves your seat after every meeting. ESC
leaves the clubhouse; the club says goodbye kindly even then.

**THE MACHINE:** the Riffrocker fills the bottom of the screen —
eight fat rubber keys, two chunky OSC sliders, the SUB button
shaped like a frog, and the famous smiling power light (it
smiles brighter while you play).

**THE KEYS ARE LIVE. ALWAYS.** Keys **1–8** on your keyboard play
the eight keys — a full major scale, do to do. Hold **SHIFT** for
the high octave. Every note plays through the real Riffrocker
voice: three oscillators in a trenchcoat — saw, a quieter saw an
octave up, and the sub-triangle underneath (that's the frog's
department). The keys light up as you play. Mash away during any
meeting. The machine stays on. That is club policy.

## HOW TO PLAY · A CLUB MEETING

Each meeting, one member hosts and teaches one thing the machine
can do. Then: **call-and-response.**

1. The host names a little phrase and plays it. LISTEN. Watch
   the keys light up — the machine shows you exactly what it
   played.
2. Your turn! Play the notes back on keys 1–8 (SHIFT for the
   high ones), then press DONE.
3. The club grades your echo on the **pitch sequence** — the
   right keys in the right order, generously and with applause.
   Rhythm is between you and the tape.

Get it and the club celebrates. Miss it and you get the call
again — MARCIE: "close. the shape was right." **Three tries and
then BEEBO declares it JAZZ** ("jazz is when it is different on
purpose!!"), the club applauds, the club MEANS it, and the
meeting moves on. There is no fail state. There never was.

## THE TWELVE MEETINGS

1. KEYS · 2. HOLDING NOTES · 3. RESTS ARE NOTES (BEEBO's
meeting; the rests are load-bearing) · 4. THE SUB FROG ·
5. LOUDER ISN'T BETTER · 6. SAD KEYS · 7. THE SECOND SAW ·
8. VIBRATO (MARCIE's meeting) · 9. SLIDES · 10. ARPS (DOT-DOT's
meeting) · 11. WRITING FOUR BARS (Todd's meeting. Todd finally
speaks. It's good.) · 12. **OPEN MIC.**

## MEETING TWELVE · OPEN MIC

The club is quiet. The tape is ready. **Your first note starts
the recording.** Play whatever you have — up to about sixteen
seconds — and when you stop playing for a couple of seconds, the
tape clicks off. Then the club plays it back, and DOT-DOT starts
the applause, and BEEBO makes it a weather event.

**Your take becomes the title music of THIS CARTRIDGE. Forever.**
Every time anyone boots Riffrocker Melody Club from this shelf,
your song is what the clubhouse plays. There is no re-do button
on the box because there is no re-do button on a tape. Play it
like you mean it, or play it like jazz. Both are true.

(A note on used cartridges: if your save file already holds
somebody's loop, that is somebody's song. The club kept it for
them. Be nice to it.)

## AFTER THE CLUB *(spoilers)*

MARCIE, at the last meeting: "now every song you ever hear on
this machine is partly yours." She is being literal. The
Riffrocker voice — these eight keys, these three oscillators —
is the instrument a whole catalog of games is scored on. Finish
the club and you will hear it everywhere, and certain other
cartridges will find their way to your shelf, and one radio in
one cabin may someday tune to a station playing four bars you
recognize completely.

And: the club knows an old song. Five notes, falling, then
holding. If you ever play it on the live keys, the club will go
quiet, all four of them, and nobody will ask how you know it.
Nobody plays anything for a while, and the while is the song.
