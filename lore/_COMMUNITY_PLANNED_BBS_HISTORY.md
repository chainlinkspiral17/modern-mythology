# COMMUNITY PLANNED · THE NETWORK
## a history of the circle's boards · 1988–2026

The dial-up network the player walks in COMMUNITY PLANNED — seven
dial numbers, six sysops, five hidden boards — has been running
continuously since October 1988. It is not a retro affectation
and it is not a leftover. It is an institution that made a
decision, sometime around the turn of the century, and has kept
the decision every year since.

The network has no name. The circle has never named it. Naming
it would make it a thing that can be asked about.

This file is the authoring reference for the network's history.
Every archival date stamp in the board JSON is reconciled here;
when adding historical posts or references, date them against
this timeline. The game's present is summer 2026 (playbook Core
rule #0). Everything below is how the network got there.

---

## 1988 · the first dial tone

RUST_CODE.BBS came up in **October 1988**, on a single line, out
of the parish. The machine was a smoke-damaged XT clone bought at
an estate sale, and the first board was MAINSTREET, and the first
night there were two callers.

October 1988 is also the season the Glitch Report collective,
three hundred miles east, first documented the substrate as
anomalous radio interference. The deck reserves whether the two
events are connected. The circle has never claimed they are. The
circle has never claimed they aren't.

RUST_CODE — the sysop, the handle, the register — has run the
board from the first night. RUST_CODE's identity is reserved, on
the boards and in this file. The circle knows. The player may
come to suspect. The deck does not confirm.

## 1989–1992 · the build-out

The network grew the way the circle does everything: one vouched
call at a time.

- **CALICHE** (Lubbock) came up in **spring 1989** — WIRE_MOTHER's
  board, two lines off her shop's service entrance, wired by her
  own hand and grounded, by her own insistence, better than the
  county's. The barn's binding-code sticky carries a date stamp
  of **April 7, 1981** — eight years before the board existed.
  WIRE_MOTHER has never explained the stamp. THE_QUARRY has asked
  (BL_001: *is 1981 the age of the binding code, or the age of
  the sticky?*). The circle's practice is to leave the stamp
  alone. Authoring rule: the stamp stays, and nobody explains it.
- **DRY_BLOOM** (Santa Fe) came up in **autumn 1989** — PALOMINO's
  board, one line into the studio, answered only between second
  light and the named hour.
- **THE_ATTIC** opened in **January 1990** — the memorial board,
  the room upstairs. It was PALOMINO, not RUST_CODE, who wrote
  the room's rules (TA_001, January 7, 1990), and the rules have
  not changed a word in thirty-six years. Threads are *moved*
  into the attic; the room keeps the dates they were written.
  This is why the attic's stamps run 1990–2004 and beyond while
  the rest of the network's archive turns over: the attic is the
  one board where old is the point.
- **OVERPASS** (Mobile) came up in **1990** — STEEPLE's board,
  run from the harbor office annex, tide tables first, everything
  else second.
- **BEDROCK** came up in **1992** — THE_QUARRY's board, out of
  the limestone country, questions only. The same winter, the
  first hidden board — **THE_BACKCHANNEL** — went live on
  RUST_CODE.BBS (earliest surviving posts: January 14, 1992).
  The backchannel was hidden not because its content was secret
  but because the circle had learned, by 1992, that a board's
  existence is information.

## November 1991 · the number

MAINSTREET has been at its current dial number since **November
1991** — the welcome sticky says so, and the sticky is accurate.
The board is older than the number. The number changed because
the old number surfaced, in October 1991, in a place it should
not have surfaced. The circle does not say where. The number was
changed inside a week, passed by hand and by voice, and the
discipline that grew from that week is the network's oldest
standing rule: **a number is given to a person, not published to
a place.** The hidden boards' dial-number clues — seven digits
planted in the flavor text of a public post, legible only to
somebody already reading carefully — are the 2026 form of the
same discipline.

## the nineties · the public scene next door

Through the nineties the region ran a second, louder board
culture — HARMONY-NET, BAYOU-GATEWAY, BEAUMONT-FILE-EXCHANGE,
WAR-OF-NUMBERS, the file areas and the door games and the
chiptune uploads (documented in
`planned_community/digital_subculture.md`). The circle's network
and the public scene overlapped in geography and in exactly two
regular callers, and the circle's boards never appeared in any
public dial directory, and no file ever crossed from one network
to the other. This was not contempt. It was fence-keeping. The
public scene was documenting the region's strangeness for
whoever came; the circle was working it, for nobody.

The network's one door game dates from this era: **THE TALLY**,
RUST_CODE's weekend build of winter 1993, running continuously
since. One line per caller per call — a thing counted that day,
and its number. No score, no ending. The annual reading (twelve
lines, selection method unpublished) prints the last Sunday of
July; Elicia posts it to THE_LIBRARY, where the archive of every
reading lives in the files section. The TALLY is the network's
own answer to the notebook games the bayou studios were making
in the same years, arrived at independently, which the circle
would say is not a coincidence and also not worth remarking on.

Two survivals from that era matter to the deck: **CYPRESS-LINE**,
E. Aday's one-board successor to HARMONY-NET, still online in
2026 with its seven monthly callers — and the circle. The two
longest-running boards in the region have, by the deck's
reserved accounting, exchanged four calls in thirty years. All
four were placed by RUST_CODE. All four were answered.

## 1999–2003 · the deli

ADELPHIA-DELI ran in Graustark for four years — members-only,
nineteen accounts, the NRM collective's board, sysop handle
**DICKENS**. The circle knew who DICKENS was. DICKENS knew who
the circle was. Neither network ever posted the other's number,
and the two boards' careful mutual silence was, in retrospect,
the last period of anything that could be called detente. The
deli closed in 2003. The 486 went to the warehouse. The enemy
has read MAINSTREET since at least that year.

The circle has known the enemy reads MAINSTREET since **2011**.
The circle's response was not to close the board — a closed
board tells the reader he has been noticed. The response was the
registers.

## the refusal · why it is still copper

Between 1999 and 2005 the internet emptied the region's boards.
The circle never discussed following, which was the discussion.
The reasoning, assembled from twenty years of side remarks:

- A call is a call between two named places on lines the sysops
  physically maintain. The internet is a broadcast into
  infrastructure the enemy owns pieces of.
- Copper fails legibly. When WIRE_MOTHER's line goes down, she
  walks it and finds the break. Nobody walks a cloud.
- Slowness is a filter. Anyone still willing to dial at 2400
  baud in 2026 has already answered the only vetting question
  the circle has.

WIRE_MOTHER keeps the network alive in the literal sense: spare
modems, line filters, a drawer of capacitors, one standing rule
(*"you do not replace a working board computer; you replace its
fans"*). The RUST_CODE machine has been upgraded twice in
thirty-eight years, both times by necessity, both times with the
old drive imaged and the image archived in two places, one of
which is the attic's own backup, which is the only backup the
circle keeps offsite. The other place is not written down.

## the registers · voice as fence

The sysops have always written the way they write — STEEPLE in
tide and keel, WIRE_MOTHER in work orders, PALOMINO in second
light, THE_QUARRY in questions, RUST_CODE in the lyric register.
Voice was personality before it was protocol.

It became protocol in **2020**, when the circle began tracking
Aria's interior life in earnest (she was eight) and needed to
discuss her on boards the enemy reads. PALOMINO proposed the
substitution vocabulary; each sysop coded the same concepts into
their own standing register, so the code would not read as a
code (the aria_glossary.json layer; the W11 unlock). SNACKS —
who had joined in **1995**, the last board and the plainest
voice — declined the substitutions and has said *Aria* plainly
for six years. SNACKS's stated reason, in full: *"she's a girl,
not a weather system."* The circle accepted this. The glossary's
own unlock mechanic — the player earns legibility by reading
SNACKS's bleached-counter entries — exists because SNACKS's
refusal is the one place the code and the plain text sit side
by side.

## 2019 · the Sinkhole, and after

The Sinkhole took Dante, the boat, and part of the parish. The
network's traffic in the year after was, by the circle's own
accounting, the heaviest in its history, and almost all of it
was the attic. Old threads were moved into the room upstairs
with their dates intact — Dante's 2004 thread about the building
that was not yet a building among them. The memorial fund's
checks date from 2020. The vol4 names were already on
chainlinkspiral's list. The room held.

Since 2019 the network has run at its settled temperature: half
operation, half memorial, all of it patient. The strategic
game's Sunday-night BBS session — the player dialing in, reading
the week, hanging up — is the network exactly as the friends use
it in 2026.

## the friends · when they arrived

The four regulars, in order of first call:

- **chainlinkspiral** · **1991.** The harbormaster who hired him
  wrote the number on the back of his first paycheck stub. The
  harbormaster died in '95 and is on chainlinkspiral's list in
  the attic. He has called from the same gulf town for
  thirty-five years.
- **uzhekwurm** · **1994.** Arrived from Bytom with two suitcases
  and a vouching letter the circle has never described. Posts in
  transliterated cadence, mostly on BEDROCK. THE_QUARRY's only
  consistent correspondent.
- **hasslein** · **mid-1990s** (he says '94; WIRE_MOTHER says
  '96; the logs from those years were on the drive that was
  imaged, and neither of them will check). Lubbock-adjacent,
  vouched by WIRE_MOTHER, called *the heretic* by her ever
  since, with affection, mostly.
- **jlowe** · **2019.** The last new caller the network has
  admitted. Portland. Vouched by hasslein after — by hasslein's
  own account — a year of deciding. The only regular young
  enough to have found dial-up as an act of devotion rather
  than habit. Keeps period iron alive on principle and posts
  about it on the porch.

## 2026 · now

Seven dial numbers. Six sysops, all still answering. Five hidden
boards. Two callers a night on the slow boards, twenty on a
storm night. One number that has not changed since November
1991. The enemy still reads MAINSTREET; the circle still lets
him; the registers still hold; SNACKS still says her name
plainly; the attic still keeps its dates.

The board turns thirty-eight in October.

## Authoring rules (for sessions touching network history)

1. **Date historical posts against this timeline.** A board
   cannot carry a post older than its founding year above.
   Archive stamps currently in canon: the barn sticky (Apr 7,
   1981 — the unexplained stamp; leave it), the attic (Jan 7,
   1990 onward), the backchannel (Jan 14, 1992), the bar (Feb
   1993), the workshop (Aug 1993), the MAINSTREET welcome
   sticky (Jan 6, 1996).
2. **The attic is the only board where old dates accumulate.**
   Everywhere else the working archive turns over; a deliberately
   old post outside the attic needs a reason (a sticky, a moved
   thread, an anniversary repost that says it is one).
3. **Numbers are given, not published.** Any new hidden-board
   clue follows the November 1991 discipline: seven digits
   planted in a public post's flavor text (playbook content
   rule 4).
4. **RUST_CODE's identity stays reserved.** The sysop can reply
   (rarely, briefly, in the lyric register). The deck does not
   confirm who is typing.
5. **The refusal is settled canon.** No thread should relitigate
   whether to leave copper; the circle does not discuss it. A
   new caller marveling at the fact is fine; the circle's
   answers are short.
