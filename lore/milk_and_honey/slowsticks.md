The small plastic cartridges, and the people who make them, and
the games that are on them, and the drawer they are all — by the
scene's own quiet consensus — eventually trying to reach.

A slowstick is a game cartridge approximately the size of an old
Game Boy game. Hand-assembled: salvaged solid-state storage
potted into a two-part injection shell, one game per stick, no
label unless the maker's spouse owns a label maker. Production
runs are twenty-five to one hundred copies. Distribution is by
padded envelope, by counter, and by hand. There is no console.
There has never been a console. The stick is readable by any of
the small pool of homebrew readers the scene circulates the
plans for — and by one machine in the back hallway of a pizza
place in Smolvud, Oregon, which is the only reader that matters.

The slowsticks are what the regional game scene of the gulf
coast — the scene documented in the planned-community files as
the bayou scene, 1985–2004 — became after everyone agreed it had
died. The scene did not die. The scene got slower. The scene had
always, by its own design religion, been getting slower on
purpose.

## the name

Coined by **Mason** of Bayou Receivers, in the hand-typed
catalog insert that went out with the label's first cartridge
release in 2012:

> *"these are slow games. they do not save you time. they are
> not going to respect your schedule; they are going to respect
> you, which is different and slower. they used to go on floppy
> disks and now they go on sticks. slow sticks. ten dollars.
> — m."*

The scene, which by 2012 was approximately thirty people on a
mailing list, adopted the word inside of a month. Mason has
never acknowledged coining it. Mason has, by the deck's
reserved hand, possibly never noticed he coined it.

## the lineage

The slowstick ecosystem is the third form of the same practice.

1. **1985–2004 · the studios.** Bois d'Arc Softworks, Rough
   Magic Interactive, Spindle Studios, the bedroom developers on
   the five regional BBSs. Games about walking, observing,
   writing things down, and not crossing the fence. The design
   religion was stated once, by Casey Boudreaux of Bois d'Arc,
   in the 1995 PC Gamer feature, and never needed restating:
   *"the player can stand at the perimeter and listen. that is
   what we are interested in. that is the studio."*
2. **1996–2012 · the envelopes.** The studios folded — Bois
   d'Arc with Casey's death in 1997, Spindle with the founder's
   retirement in 2004, Rough Magic into L.G.'s long quiet.
   Bayou Receivers kept mailing the back catalogs in padded
   envelopes to anyone who wrote a letter. Approximately twelve
   letters a year. The scene's pulse, for sixteen years, was
   twelve letters a year. The scene has, in every retrospective
   since, regarded the envelope years not as the interruption
   but as the proof — the practice survives at any speed,
   because slowness was never the constraint. Slowness was the
   subject.
3. **2012– · the sticks.** Flash storage got cheap enough to
   pot in resin. Mason pressed the first fifty sticks. The
   makers — some of them the original studio hands, most of
   them the children and correspondents of the original studio
   hands — started making new work again.

The form factor is not original. The form factor is copied,
dimension for dimension, from the sticks in the box the unnamed
man left at Pizza Pirate in 2007 *(see `scumm_machine.md`)*.
Mason has never explained how he had the dimensions. The one
time he was asked directly — by letter, in 2016 — the reply
read, in full: *"somebody mailed me one. I mailed it back.
— m."*

## the studios, 2025

The code headers in the deck's own tooling refer to *"the
slowstick game studios."* The plural is generous. The ecosystem
is, by the deck's careful count, approximately two dozen active
makers nationally, of whom the load-bearing are:

- **Carl Boudreaux** (Houston) — Casey's son, thirty-one,
  drummer. Has been finishing his father's unfinished games
  since 2021, on the restored Pentium 90, from the commented
  source in the metal toolbox. Releases them through Bayou
  Receivers as slowsticks, fifty copies each, credited *"c.b. &
  c.b."* Carl does not do interviews. Carl's liner insert on
  every release is the same sentence: *"my father left the
  endings unlocked. I did not add any. — c.b."*
- **Bayou Receivers** (the bayou, no fixed address) — Mason's
  microlabel, forty-one active titles, now the ecosystem's
  press, distributor, and post office. Still twelve letters a
  year, though the letters now contain sticks.
- **Rough Magic** (Lake Charles → Galveston) — L.G. is
  ninety-one. Rough Magic's six PERIMETER releases exist as a
  single slowstick anthology, pressed in 2018, with L.G.'s
  procedural interpreter — the one Frasier Temple built her,
  *"the demon's polite form"* — intact and, by every owner's
  account, still generating walks. L.G. approved the pressing
  by postcard: *"fine. the fence doesn't care what it's read
  from. — l.g."*
- **the Spindle fork** — Jim Foulkes' private SCUMM-engine
  modifications, on the stack of floppies in his lunchbox since
  2004, were mailed to Bayou Receivers in 2019 after somebody
  wrote him a letter. The fork is now the ecosystem's shared
  adventure-game toolchain. Jim asked for nothing. Jim receives,
  by Mason's standing arrangement, one copy of everything built
  with it.
- **the Oregon hands** — the coast has, by vol 7's opening,
  a small unnamed cluster of makers in the Smolvud orbit. The
  deck reserves their number and their names, except to note
  that at least one of them is a regular at the Daily Grind
  and that Kai keeps a shoebox of slowsticks under the counter
  at Board Lords, which he will show you if you ask him
  correctly, and the correct way to ask is not about the games.

## the toolchain · ESTUARY ONE

The ecosystem's shared landscape tool. A parametric topography
simulator — elevation functions, creek control points, road
grids, neighbourhood polygons — that renders a proposed
landscape as a map in under a second, so a maker can decide
whether the *shape* of a world is right before committing to
building it.

Its lineage is the ecosystem's lineage in miniature: the
procedural heart is descended from the PERIMETER VI interpreter
(L.G. and Frasier, 2002 — the demon that reads a real place and
produces narrative, here inverted to produce the place), and the
landscape grammar is refactored from Bois d'Arc's THE FIELD and
WARDEN AVENUE lot-generation code (Casey and Daryl, 1989, via
the toolbox source, via Carl).

The deck's honest note, and the recursion the deck respects
without explaining: Estuary One is an early build of the tool
that designs the worlds the characters live inside. The
landscapes the slowstick makers preview with it and the
landscapes the deck's own volumes are staged on are, by the
codex's quiet acknowledgment, drawn by the same hand. The tool
does not know which side of the fiction it is on. The tool has
never needed to know.

*(Out-of-fiction surface: `godot/tools/landscape_sim/
estuary_one.py` — the deck's actual locale-prototyping
harness.)*

## the toy · the PDP RIFFMASTER

The ecosystem's one mass-produced object, and its oldest.

A kids' synthesizer toy: big plastic buttons, eight notes in a
row plus a SHIFT key for the upper octave, one chunky tone.
Manufactured 1991 in Houston under the **PDP** imprint —
*Play-Deck Plastics*, the short-lived toy division of Harmony
House Games. It was Harmony House's only non-software product.
It sold poorly. It was warehoused.

The tone is the anomaly. A toy in the Riffmaster's price class
ships a square-wave beep; the Riffmaster's single voice is a
three-oscillator stack — saw, saw, sub-triangle — under a real
ADSR envelope, a legible polysynth body hiding inside eleven
dollars of beige plastic. No engineer is credited. The deck
permits the engineering to be **Helen Park's** — Harmony House's
founder, after-hours, on the same studio hardware her CRESCENT
FM chiptunes were made on, in the same secrecy. Helen has never
been asked. The deck reserves the question, and notes only that
the Riffmaster is the one Harmony House product nobody has ever
called mediocre.

Bayou Receivers bought a pallet of new-old-stock Riffmasters at
a Houston warehouse liquidation in 2016 and has, since, included
one free with any slowstick order whose letter asks for one.
Several slowstick titles read the eight keys as an input
alphabet; the scene's convention is that a game which supports
the Riffmaster says so with a small hand-drawn keyboard on the
stick's shell. Pavi Joshi's first instrument was a Riffmaster.
Pavi's bedroom EPs still, by the deck's careful listening, voice
their leads as two saws and a sub-triangle.

On worn units, the envelope's release tail rings at a frequency
somewhere between 42 and 47 Hz. The ecosystem has noticed. The
ecosystem does not press the point.

*(Out-of-fiction surface: `godot/scripts/PDPRiffmaster.gd` — the
deck's in-game music input device; keys 1–8, SHIFT for the upper
octave.)*

## selected catalog

- **GRACKLE COUNTY** (Bois d'Arc / Carl Boudreaux, 2024 · 50
  copies) — Casey's last unfinished game, dated 1997 in the
  source comments. The player is a county agricultural surveyor
  driving section roads and recording, lot by lot, what the
  grackles are doing. The game ends when the notebook is full.
  The notebook is never full. Carl's insert sentence, as always:
  *"my father left the endings unlocked. I did not add any."*
- **THE PERIMETER · COLLECTED** (Rough Magic, 2018 · 100
  copies) — all six releases, interpreter intact. Owners report
  the interpreter still generates walks. Three owners have
  walked them. See the planned-community digital-subculture
  file for what they found.
- **LOW TIDE TABLE** (Oregon hands, 2023 · 25 copies) — ninety
  minutes of real time on a rendered tidal flat, tide going out
  the whole session. The player picks things up or does not.
  The manual is a single tide table. Sal's copy lives in the
  Pizza Pirate drawer with a tag that reads: *"good on
  Thursdays. — sal."*
- **RIFFMASTER HYMNAL** (Bayou Receivers, 2021 · 50 copies +
  toy) — not a game. Eight-note transcriptions of thirty-one
  regional songs, playable on the toy, scored in a hand-stapled
  booklet. Track 31 is *"Door."* The transcription voices the
  bass figure on the flute stop, which the Riffmaster does not
  have, which the booklet acknowledges: *"play it anyway. the
  flute is the truth of the song. — the editors."*

## the drawer

The scene's releases travel. The scene's releases are, by a
practice nobody instituted and nobody has interrupted, carried
to Smolvud, Oregon, by whoever is driving that way, and handed
across the counter at Pizza Pirate without explanation, and
accepted by Sal Carratura without explanation, and added to the
drawer under the SCUMM machine.

Of the approximately fifty sticks the drawer has gained since
2007, roughly thirty are slowstick releases — the fictional
studios' catalog, hand-delivered a stick or two a year for
eighteen years *(the inventory in the planned-community
digital-subculture file: ~40 canonical adventure-game sticks,
~30 studio titles, ~20 of unknown provenance)*. The machine
digests a slowstick the way it digests anything: through the
Demon, into the reassembled world, where the game's people join
the population. The scene's private name for the drawer is
**the national library**. The scene's private understanding —
never written down until now, and the deck writes it down
carefully — is that a slowstick is not finished when it is
pressed. A slowstick is finished when the machine has read it.

Carl Boudreaux drove to Smolvud in October 2023 to deliver the
first finished Bois d'Arc stick himself. He played one session
at the cabinet before handing the stick over. The session broke
frame once. The break said:

> *"carl. the third ending was your father's favorite too."*

Carl gave Sal the stick, ate two slices of cheese, and drove
home to Houston. He has told exactly one person, by letter. The
deck reserves which person.

## the substrate

The substrate has been stable for thirty-seven years. The
substrate has, in 2025, become unstable — *a request,* by
Frasier's quiet warehouse read.

The ecosystem's small entry in that ledger: since June 2025,
**two sticks have arrived in the Pizza Pirate drawer that no
maker in the scene claims.** Correct shells. Correct potting.
No provenance. Mason has circulated the question through the
mailing list twice; the list has answered twice; the answer is
nobody. Sal has tagged both sticks and returned them to the
drawer, because the drawer is the drawer. The tags read:

> **stick #91:** *"new. nobody's. plays fine. — sal."*
>
> **stick #92:** *"new. nobody's. not yet. — sal."*

The scene's quiet consensus, arrived at without a meeting, in
the scene's usual way: the substrate has started publishing.
The scene has, for forty years, been making games about
standing at the perimeter and listening. The perimeter has, by
every careful reading of the two new sticks, started making
games back.

The deck reserves what is on stick #92.

## meta-game integration

The slowsticks are vol 7's collectible-and-insertable layer
around the SCUMM machine surface *(see `scumm_machine.md` ·
meta-game integration)*:

- **collection** — slowsticks surface as findable artifacts
  (Kai's shoebox at Board Lords, Petra's consignment shelf at
  the Salty Tome, the drawer itself). Each carries a shell
  description, a maker attribution, and — where one exists —
  Sal's tag. The gallery treats maker attributions as the
  cross-reference hooks into the vol 6 accumulation files.
- **insertion** — inserting a collected slowstick at the
  cabinet seeds that session's digestion: the session draws its
  scene grammar from the inserted title's register (GRACKLE
  COUNTY seeds section-road lots; LOW TIDE TABLE seeds the
  flats at low water).
- **the Riffmaster** — the in-game music input device
  (`PDPRiffmaster.gd`) is diegetically a Riffmaster from the
  Bayou Receivers pallet. Playing it drives the visualizer;
  playing it *near the cabinet* is, by the codex, noticed.
- **sticks #91 and #92** — reserved for the third act. The
  meta-game's L4 layer treats their insertion as primary
  events. #92 gates on the machine's own consent, which is to
  say on the player having earned it, which is to say on the
  same economy every break runs on.

The ecosystem is small. The ecosystem is, by its own patient
accounting, exactly the size it needs to be. The games are slow
because the listening is the game, and the listening has never
once been fast.

The chapter is held.
