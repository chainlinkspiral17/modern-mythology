## the object

A **slow stick** — also "stick," also "SCUMM stick," the terms
are interchangeable in the prose — is *"a dark plastic rectangle
about the size of his palm, with the gold connector at one end"*
(ch18_stick). Sticks ship in **waxed-paper sleeves** — *"the kind
the foundation had been packaging sticks in since the '30s"* —
with small labels; Cale's are handwritten (*"Estuary 7, Ines
Rocha 2046 in his small careful hand on the white label"*), and
owners annotate their own (*"Tideline Survey 2018 — Hjarvard
Ulrich — B. Tillman, January 2050"*).

The thing inside a stick is not the stick: *"the stick is a
stick. Kestrel Mountain is the name of the experience"*
(ch11_stick). The content-word throughout is **experience**.

(Terminology wobble, preserved not fixed: ch21_tower and the
epilogue call Tem's *Estuary 7* a **disc** in a disc-case, played
on a **player** with a **headset**.)

## the reader and the cap

A **reader** is the playback device. Eddvard's has been at the
cabin since 2042: *"The reader is a good reader. The reader is
not, by anything I know, the source of the trouble. The trouble
is the substrate. **The reader is just the door.**"*
(ch10_morning). Compact consumer readers exist (Finn's, thirty
tokens from Cale in '49); Cale repairs older ones at his back
bench. Owning one is not automatic — Cale told Lena *"to wait
until she had a reason."* A public cabinet form exists: the
SCUMM machine at Pizza Pirate (single scene, ch2_morning — see
`scumm_machine.md`).

The **cap**: *"a thin band of a fabric that wraps around your
forehead... the way the substrate gets in to you... The clock
pulls you out at ninety. The substrate cannot keep you past the
clock. The substrate cannot keep you against the cap. The cap is
the wall. The cap is the door. You go in through the cap, you
come out through the cap, and the cap is yours."* (ch11_stick)

## the session

Ninety minutes on the clock; one to two days of experience time
inside (Kai: "A day. Maybe more."). You inhabit a rendered body
that is close to yours and is not yours — *"Walk in the body the
experience gives you"* — down to scars and calluses you don't
have. Smell comes through the cap. Sticks differ on replay:
*"The sticks I have played twice have been different the second
time."* Playing is *"one of the more private things a person can
do."*

And it costs. Tem's warning, in full force (ch10_morning):
*"People who play sticks the way the people who really play them
play them have come out... having lost time. Having lost weight.
Having had relationships they were in dissolve... The sticks are
popular because the experiences in them are extraordinary. The
experiences are extraordinary because the substrate gives them.
**The substrate gives them by taking.**"* Aud's counterweight
(ch16_aud): *"It's not for everybody. It's for the person who
needs the work... The clear is the work. Live in it."*

## the substrate relationship

The volume's thesis (Tem, ch1_morning): *"the substrate that the
slow sticks run on is — by the design of the man who made it —
supposed to be in the sticks. It is supposed to live in the
silicon... I have been wondering whether the substrate, in some
places, has stopped being only in the sticks."* And ch12_kai:
*"The substrate is not waiting anymore for the slow-sticks to be
the door."*

The deep frame, from the Bergen carver's imprint (ch11_kestrel):
the cedar bowls are the older listening medium; *"The newer
medium can be carved in time the way the wood is carved in
matter."* Some of the old network called the silicon *a heresy*;
the carver's answer: *"the heresy is not the silicon. The heresy
is the way the network is going to fight you when you do this...
Do the work. The work will defend itself."*

## imprints

*"I am not Nate. I am the imprint of Nate... **a worn place in
the substrate** that has the shape of a sixteen-year-old kid in
1993 working at a desk."* Imprints know only what happened in
their room. They migrate — the substrate is *"porous between
sticks the way it is porous between sticks and the world"* (the
Bergen carver bled into Kestrel Mountain in '91 from another
stick). Known imprints: Nate at the desk; the monk who told Aud
to buy a diner; the old man in waders at the second pool.

## objects crossing out

The volume's central mechanic (ch11_kestrel): *"The substrate is
not very precise about objects that have been at a cabin
recently... The bowl is going to be at the cabin and the bowl is
going to be in your pocket. Both will be true. The substrate is
fine with this. Are you."* Kai carries out the substrate-carved
cedar bowl; Finn carries out Olaf's 1976 bell-piece and
Brandon's notebook (which materializes in his actual coat in his
closet). The stick replaces what is taken: *"The pool has
another bell now."*

## distribution

- **Cale at ChillWave** is the local source — cedar shelves
  alphabetized by designer, a new-arrivals crate, a back
  workbench. He holds sticks for people before knowing who:
  *"I've been holding it for you. I just didn't know it was you
  until you walked in here a minute ago."* Payment is loose —
  nineteen dollars, thirty tokens, *"Don't pay me. We'll settle
  later."*
- **Inheritance and hand-to-hand.** Eddvard's reader to Tem;
  Brandon's stick held by Tem two years in a paper bag for *"the
  person who would walk in with the right reason to play it."*
- **Institutional gifts.** The foundation gave Aud a Kestrel
  Mountain stick as *"a kind of pension"* — senior staff of a
  decade-plus *"had earned the right to play a stick the
  foundation knew was good."*
- **Choosing matters.** *"It matters which one. The wrong one
  would be a waste of the playing."*

## Oneironautics Inc. and the foundation

**Oneironautics Inc.** — Dean's company: the slow-stick
technology, the early sticks Dean produced himself, the move
into substrate-design in the late '20s, and sticks still
releasing for ten years after his 2042 death *"with no
announcement of who was running it."* Studio on the Tower's
second floor (racks, prototyping rigs, headsets on cedar
mounts, the poster archive). **The foundation** — Eugene, 11th
and Olive (H. Lindholm's 1969–77 build); packages the sleeves,
logs designers' session databases, runs the town's drones,
employs Marina, employed Aud; released Henrik's bowl on the
volume's Thursday.

## unratified pre-history (previous draft · not vol 7 canon)

The earlier revision of this file described a gulf-coast
cartridge lineage: the bayou studios (1985–2004 — this much *is*
established, in `planned_community/digital_subculture.md`) →
Bayou Receivers' padded-envelope years → hand-potted cartridge
releases from 2012, the name "slowstick" coined by Mason, Carl
Boudreaux finishing Bois d'Arc games, the PDP Riffmaster toy,
ESTUARY ONE as the scene's landscape tool, and delivery of the
scene's releases to the Pizza Pirate drawer.

None of that is in the vol 7 prose, and its dating collides
with the real slow-stick facts above. It is retained (in git
history and in this summary) only as **possible deep
pre-history** — a 2010s–20s cottage precursor whose name the
2030s+ experience medium could have inherited — pending the
author's ratification or veto. The 2026 COMMUNITY PLANNED
threads that reference mail-order potted sticks and a
plans-built reader (CP_007, TW_009, SN_006, DA_006) are written
without the word "slowstick" and stand or fall with that
decision.

*(Out-of-fiction surfaces: `godot/tools/landscape_sim/
estuary_one.py` — the deck's landscape prototyper, in-fiction an
early build in the Estuary tool lineage that runs to Ines
