# BOARD LORDS
## Kai's skate shop · Cypress Street · Smolvud, OR

A narrow storefront on the landward side of Cypress Street, under
the oldest painted-brick ghost sign in Smolvud (WAGNER'S — QUALITY
— HARDWARE, legible at the right hour in the right rain). One
room, high tin ceiling, wood floor worn pale in a lane down the
middle where sixty years of feet have gone to the counter and
back. The window says BOARD LORDS in hand-cut vinyl Kai leveled
by eye, slightly wrong, and has declined to fix for four years.

## the building

Four iterations. The building remembers all of them; the town
remembers most.

1. **Wagner's Hardware · 1962–2011.** Nels Wagner and then his
   son Gordon. Keys cut while you wait. The smell — linseed,
   pipe dope, paper sacks of nails — soaked into the floorboards
   so thoroughly that damp mornings still raise it. Gordon
   closed the shop the year the last commercial fishing family
   sold their license, which he maintained was not a
   coincidence, and retired to Tillamook.
2. **Nothing · 2011–2014.** Papered windows. Three years empty.
   Kai was fourteen the winter he found the back door's lock
   surrendering to patience, and the empty aisles — swept
   concrete at the back, that pale wooden lane at the front —
   became his first indoor spot. He skated it alone, evenings,
   for three years, and swept up after himself, and by the
   quiet accounting of the landlord (who knew, of course the
   landlord knew) never broke a thing except one pane he taped,
   paid for in cash at sixteen, and never explained. The deck
   notes, and Kai does not, that the building's empty years are
   when the building and Kai agreed on each other.
3. **The general store · 2014–2021.** The Pedersens. Sold what a
   general store sells — tide charts, propane, penny candy in
   actual jars, fishing tackle, one shelf of paperbacks — to
   fewer people each year, with dignity. The Pedersens' last
   act was to sell the fixtures to Kai at a price everyone
   involved understood to be a blessing.
4. **Board Lords · 2021–present.** Kai was twenty-four. First
   and only skate shop in Smolvud. The name is a small joke
   about lords of the board that the deck respects without
   examining, given what else in this saga answers to *deck*
   and *board*.

## the shop

Decks on the wall in a color run Lena once called "a paint chip
display for a better hardware store," which Kai took as the
compliment it was. Trucks and bearings live in Gordon Wagner's
original parts cabinet — forty small drawers, too heavy to move,
never moved, the hand-inked labels (WING NUTS · 3/8 · STOVE
BOLTS) left in place under Kai's tape labels, the old names
showing through like the ghost sign upstairs.

The shop is open when Kai is in it. The hours on the door say
10–6. The hours are aspirational. Regulars know the truth: if
the lumber mill gate is chained shut and the tide is wrong, Kai
is at the shop.

## the shoebox

Under the counter, left end, a shoebox (women's rain boots, size
8, provenance unexplained) holding Kai's slowsticks —
approximately fifteen at any time, the number drifting as sticks
travel to and from the Pizza Pirate drawer *(see
`slowsticks.md` · the drawer)*.

The shoebox is not stock. Nothing in it is for sale. Kai lends
sticks the way Sal's drawer lends them — you take one, you bring
it back, nobody writes anything down. But where Sal's drawer is
open to any player, the shoebox has a door policy, and the
policy is this: **Kai will show you the shoebox if you ask him
correctly, and the correct way to ask is not about the games.**

What the correct way is, the deck declines to standardize —
because Kai hasn't. A kid who asks what Kai does on the nights
the shop light stays on late has been shown the shoebox. A
collector who asked about "the cartridges" with a price in his
voice was shown, instead, the wall of decks, at length, until he
left. Lena has never asked; Lena will be shown in October,
without asking, which is its own chapter.

Among the fifteen: a copy of LOW TIDE TABLE (one of the
twenty-five); GRACKLE COUNTY with Carl Boudreaux's insert
sentence; the PERIMETER anthology; and one stick with no maker's
mark that is always in the box no matter how many are out. The
deck reserves whether Kai makes sticks or only keeps them. The
Oregon hands are, per the slowstick file, unnamed. The shop
light does stay on late, some nights.

## the cartridge on the shelf

Behind the counter, on the shelf with the tax paperwork and the
first dollar (unframed, thumbtacked), sits a gray NES cartridge:
**WARDEN AVENUE** (Bois d'Arc Softworks, 1989). Kai's father
gave it to him in 2008, when Kai was eleven. It is not for sale
in a way that is different from how the shoebox is not for sale.

Kai has played it through to its self-determined ending sixteen
times. It has, by his quiet acknowledgment, *"never once given
me the same notebook observation twice on the same lot"* — which
is, by every reasonable reading of the NES's deterministic
hardware, impossible. Kai confirmed it at twenty with a borrowed
cartridge-dumping rig: his copy contains code that appears in no
other extant dump of the game. Frasier's read, relayed through
the network the deck declines to diagram: *"a substrate-touched
copy. somebody modified the ROM. I do not know who. the
modification was good."*

The cartridge sits on the shelf, label out. Customers assume
it's decoration. The seventeenth playthrough is, by the codex,
reserved for the volume's third act, after the second time Lena
plays the SCUMM machine — the notebook game and the cabinet
having, in some sense the deck respects, things to say to each
other.

## Kai and the machine

Kai met the SCUMM machine at thirteen — the empty-building
years — and has known Sal Carratura ever since. He plays about
once every six weeks, Saturday nights after close, stick #78
(*"kai — this is the one. you'll know. — sal."*). Three
significant sessions in eight years. The third, three weeks
before vol7's chapter one, produced a message Kai has not
shared with anybody, including this file.

## meta-game integration

Board Lords is a vol7 Act III collection surface *(companion to
`scumm_machine.md` and `slowsticks.md` integrations)*:

- **the shoebox** — slowstick artifacts, browsable after the
  player earns it via the ask-correctly dialogue gate (the gate
  keys on the player asking about Kai, the building, or the
  late-night light — any topic except the games themselves).
  Borrowed sticks can be carried to Pizza Pirate and inserted;
  the machine seeds sessions from the inserted title's register.
- **the parts cabinet** — the gallery treats Gordon Wagner's
  forty drawers as a micro-archive; three drawers hold
  non-hardware items the codex reserves for the mid-act.
- **WARDEN AVENUE** — examine-only until the third-act trigger;
  its description card cross-references the digital-subculture
  file and Kai's sixteen notebooks, which he has kept, all
  sixteen, in the drawer labeled STOVE BOLTS.

The shop is the volume's quiet argument that custodianship is a
kind of love: the ghost sign kept legible, the drawers'
old labels showing through the new, the cartridge that is not
decoration, the shoebox that opens for the right question. The
building and Kai agreed on each other a long time ago.

The chapter is held.
