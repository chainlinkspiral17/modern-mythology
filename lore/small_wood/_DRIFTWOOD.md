## What DRIFT WOOD is

A black-and-white comic strip drawn by **Arthur Finch**, a
Small Wood, Oregon high schooler, during the first year of its
run — the **Class of 1998** era. It runs (or wants to run) in
the school paper, **The Timberline**, and Arthur is mailing
samples to **Portland, OR** in a stamped envelope that spends
the whole run on his desk.

The strip is about the town the way the town would never
describe itself: the estuary at low tide, the cafeteria as the
only room where three hundred people voluntarily sit in the same
direction, cashiers as the Greek chorus, fog as a thing worth
being thankful for. Arthur's register is French-cinema-via-
video-store — Truffaut, Rohmer, Eisenstein, *À bout de
souffle* rented eleven times — pointed at a crab derby flyer
commission and Tuesday's meatloaf.

**The masthead epigraph** (plate 00, the estuary, a heron, the
cannery on pilings):

> *"THE ESTUARY AT LOW TIDE. Everything that leaves this town
> goes out through here. Most of it comes back. — A.F."*

That is the whole strip in four sentences, and arguably the
whole Small Wood volume family.

## Recurring motifs

- **The bird beside the signature.** Every strip's "A. Finch —
  Small Wood, OR" carries a small bird, and it varies — gull,
  heron, crow-shaped. In strip 03 Gully draws the principal *as
  a gull* and Arthur concedes the likeness. (For deck readers:
  the bird-at-the-margin is already a cross-volume signature.)
- **The signage register.** The town's signs all speak one
  dialect: SCHOOL FOOD FUEL WHATEVER · GOOD FOOD BRIGHTER
  TOMORROW · GOOD MOVIES BETTER PEOPLE · GOOD STORIES BETTER
  PEOPLE · JOURNALISM A BRIGHTER TOMORROW · WRITE EDIT PUBLISH ·
  WRITE EDIT PRINT REPEAT · SAME STORIES DIFFERENT KIDS · BE
  KIND REWIND · LATE FEES KILL GOOD TIMES · STUDENT PRESS STILL
  EXISTS. The signs are the strip's laugh track and conscience.
- **"The real work."** Named at winter break, deferred all
  year, invoked in the last strip, and out-argued by the tide.
- **French cinema via the video store.** Truffaut (twice),
  Rohmer, Eisenstein, *À bout de souffle* and its final shot of
  the sea — rhymed by Arthur walking the actual coast road.
- **The estuary / the cannery / the heron.** The masthead plate.
  Everything that leaves goes out through here; most of it
  comes back.

## Era anchors

- **CLASS OF 1998** banner at the assembly (03).
- Mixtape contents run through the late-'90s (with Side B
  reaching for the then-new); VHS store, Lay's bag vending

- Fits the master timeline's open **vol 4-era / 1990s window**;

## Open questions (author's to rule — flagged, not fixed)

1. **"Wood."** The others address Arthur as *"Wood"* (03, 07,
   09) — presumably from the strip's title. But the survey
   lists a **Wood Larsen** ("fog") while Arthur Finch appears
   nowhere on it, and the "fog" answer is distilled from
   Arthur's own rambling. Possibilities: "Wood" is Arthur's
   town nickname and *Wood Larsen* is a separate kid; or the
   survey prints the strip's *self-insert* name (DRIFT WOOD
   starring "Wood Larsen," Arthur's alias — a strip-within-the-
   strip conceit). Reserved.
2. **Three school names.** SMALL WOOD HIGH (02) · RIVERTON HIGH
   (03, with the Class of 1998 banner) · MAPLETON HIGH SCHOOL
   (13). Drift, or the strip renaming its town per episode
   (compare the sign SAME STORIES DIFFERENT KIDS). Reserved.
3. **The girl's name.** Never stated outside the survey-list
   candidates. If Wood Larsen is the self-insert, she may be
   Chloe Martin ("sarcasm") — pure conjecture, left open.
4. **Small Wood, OR vs. COMMUNITY PLANNED's small_wood.** The
   CP region's geography was never pinned in canon; this strip
   pins *a* Small Wood on the Oregon coast — estuary, cannery,
   crab derby, fog — i.e., the vol 7 coastline's world. Whether
   CP's small_wood region is the same Oregon town (making the
   W14 "cypress" a flagged detail) or a Texas namesake is a
   **master-timeline question**; logged there.
5. **Finch / Finn.** Arthur Finch, b. ~1982–83, draws, lives on
   this coast; vol 7's Finn inherits a grandfather's sable
   brush, shortwave, and Toyota on the same coast seventy years
   on. The deck reserves whether the Finch line runs to Finn.
   (Also noted: a **Lena Kimo** in 1997 and a Lena in 2052 —
   name-echo only until ruled.)

## Cross-volume rhymes (observed, not canonized)

The masthead's estuary-and-cannery is vol 7's geography a
half-century early; the heron stands where the crow will. The
cashier-as-Greek-chorus thesis is SNACKS's bleached counter
before SNACKS. "Owning it would change the relationship" is
Sal's drawer and Cale's don't-pay-me in miniature. And "the
real work" losing to "tide's out at four" is the whole deck's
argument — the listening beats the ambition — stated by a
seventeen-year-old in strip form.
