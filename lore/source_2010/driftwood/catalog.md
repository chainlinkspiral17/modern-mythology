## Premise

*Drift Wood* is Arthur "Wood"/"Woody" Finch's own comic strip, signed "A. Finch — Small Wood, OR". It is a strip about a strip. It starts on the back page, bottom half, of the Small Wood High paper in Sept 1997, when Wood is a senior-to-be. In Sept 1999 it becomes a daily that is photocopied in the community-college print shop and pinned up as a weekly digest at the coin laundry ("between property and God. Where the reader lives"). It goes online in Jan 2004, and its first readers are in Boise. In Nov 2006 it is rebranded ROLFCOPTR ("Forty thousand people who came for a helicopter").

Small Wood is a rain-soaked central-Oregon-coast timber and crab town. Recurring places are the estuary and boat launch, Small Wood Lanes (the bowling alley) and its dumpster, Wagner's Hardware, the Bayview Video store, the coin laundry, Heceta Frame & Matting on 101, Gully's uncle's bait shack (later **Gully's Tackle & Roast**), the North Coast Community College "Lot C", and the Greyhound stop.

The calendar repeats every year: the January "rain" strip, the February crab derby (the uncle wants "the same crab, twenty bucks"), the March grunion run, the June last bell, the Fourth ("red in '71"), August tourists, the September anniversary and the November "dog night".

The spine of the story is **leaving vs. staying**. Wood is voted Most Likely to Win a Pulitzer twice. He leaves an art-school letter unopened in 1998. In Feb 2000 he gets into Cascade College of Art ("a commercial racket designed to castrate raw vision"), lets the deadline pass, and takes a $8.50/hr mat-cutting job instead. The Oct 2000 arc ends with Chloe taking the Greyhound to Seattle. Postcards follow her in 2001 (Honolulu, Barcelona). She is back on the dock in Dec 2002 ("How's Europe." "Over."), and in 2018 they share a 2 a.m. diner booth in Newport ("I count from the cafeteria"). The "real work" never arrives: the graphic novel SLASH / THE LONG WAY BACK is "coming 2003". Meanwhile Gully builds a business, and the strip keeps getting made.

The ROFLCOPTR doc frames this as the first phase ("The High School Zine Era, 1997–2003") of a 30-year chronicle that runs to 2027. The doc's Arc 1 is "The Driftwood Ledger" (Autumn 2000): the PNCA scholarship turned down, the fried-clams strip, and a Greyhound Sunday spread in which neither waves.

## Cast

- **Arthur "Wood"/"Woody" Finch (A. Finch).** The cartoonist and narrator. A cinephile pedant who quotes Truffaut, Bresson, Ozu and Daumier, and keeps putting off "the real work". He audits Composition II "so I can use the print shop" ("philosophically I'm auditing everything"), cuts mats at Heceta Frame & Matting from April 2000, and drives a Datsun he has to push-start.
- **Chloe.** Culture editor of the school paper, and the only person who sees through Wood. Her surname is Martin on the 1997 survey and "Chloe S." in 1998; the doc calls her Chloe Sterling. She makes his mixtape and signs his yearbook "hope you figure it out. hate keeps you heavy." She sends four applications, goes to Seattle and then Europe, and writes him postcards ("Take care of Barnaby for me"). She is home in Dec 2002 and in Newport in 2018.
- **Gully.** Todd "Gully" Gulliver in the doc; "Gully Reeves" / "Gully M." on the surveys. He is the plain-spoken anchor ("Maple bars. And the new tide tables"; "Rain's not nothing"). He works his uncle's crab-bait shack and inherits it. In Nov 2002 he adds an Italian espresso machine bought in Coos Bay, creating Gully's Tackle & Roast. Every February he pays Wood $20 for the crab flyer.
- **Barnaby.** A terrier-beagle mix found tied to the Small Wood Lanes dumpster on a freezing November night in 1999. He never talks. He is the laundromat readers' favorite ("the dog one"), and later the Boise readers' ("barnaby rules").
- **Gully's uncle.** Runs the crab derby and the bait shack, which he gives over to Gully. Fishing licenses, "red in '71".
- **Minor characters.**
  - Mr. Hadley (Journalism 1).
  - Danny Voss.
  - The Composition II instructor ("A thesis is a promise you make to the reader").
  - The Heceta frame-shop boss ("Raw vision needs a sharp blade, Arthur").
  - The Lot C campus-security guard.
  - The bus driver ("twenty-two years, same road").
  - Wood's grandmother in Coos Bay ("she's my readership").
  - The frame-shop customers in the "What People Frame" series.
  - idaho_in_exile of Boise, the first online reader.
- **Continuity flags.**
  - The 1998 envelope is from the Pacific Northwest College of Art, but the 2000 acceptance letter is from "Cascade College of Art" and is addressed to "Mr. Wood".
  - The 1997 strips label the class as '94, '97 and '99.
  - The postcard stamps carry dates of 2006.
  - The school paper's masthead varies: The Timberline / The Oracle / The Bruin / School Beacon.

## Proposed interlude: "Back Page, Bottom Half" (13 strips, all from the high-school run)

This is a self-contained Small Wood leave/stay mini-arc. It begins with the town facing one direction and Chloe four houses away. It moves through the estuary thesis, the yearbook inscription and Wood's vow to leave. Then comes the year of letters: the art-school envelope he won't open, Chloe's four applications against his one "media rate" back page, the rain at the boat launch, and the wrong map of Seattle. It ends with the last strip he edits, four silent panels of the estuary. Chloe's leaving is set up, and Wood's staying is already decided. All 13 strips have OCR.
