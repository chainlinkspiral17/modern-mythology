## 1. Prologue :: The Liar King

**Source:** `arcana_src/major_arcana_rough_ass_draft__1mpmXk.txt` lines 136–207 (Drive doc “major arcana rough ass draft”, 2011).

Prologue :: The Liar King

Agile child hands feel through bottom silt, grubby face so near reflected, tiny body fighting small splashing waves at the river’s edge. Over shoulder, a four foot tall frog rolls a cigarette, cork hat pulled down low on his v-shaped face, eyes set apart but centered intently, filthy denim suspenders hanging low, bare-chested, barefooted.

“Patience, pink one. I can state with maximum authority, I saw your blasted trinket this morning making my way to the jetty by way of Rooster’s Perch, so unless there was another bystander in these forsaken parts, the device should still be in this general vicinity.”

The child stands up, mostly soaked, bangs dripping loose and curly, “It ain’t mine, an you know it. Belongs to the king, said to never lose it.”

The frog inspects long fine digits up against the setting golden hour sun, illuminating the webbing, burning through rubber skin, the purple veins and dark warts can be seen all the way through the other side.

“Your misfortune, not mine, and I am doing my darnedest to help ya. Ain’t nothing to be done, save find it then. I told you where to look, can’t help if we’ve been out half the day nothing to show.”

“Well, you could help.”

“All I can do is provide a different point of view, and mostly all that caught was glint. You’re on your own, child.”

The frog reveals an industrial lighter, spins it out of back pocket, the chrome shell of the thing flashes, and flames dance across broken water glimmer. The child misses this performance, intent on something else entire and dives down after.

The frog takes a long slow breather, sucking with tiny lungfuls, tendrils of smoke itching their way through large cavernous nostrils. He eyes the water, the child’s legs kick as it tries to plunge down deeper, the frog’s glance is one of comparative animal critique for the less than inspired water impaired undersized mammalian form.

The dirty child emerges glistening, beaming, the object of all its woes, worries, and great personal significant importance clasped in love and horror against the chest. Deep breaths as it calms down and the whole of the day fogs into focus. The frog throws his cigarette into the mudflat, claps in long slow succession, the slapping disturbance dispersing nesting oystercatchers, whimbrel and snipe.

The child does not go home straight away, instead circles and lingers round the sunken corpse of the once proud “Minstral’s Green” steamship, first time since going in and exploring the guts several times in years past, but never wearing father’s doohickey, as he calls it.

The doohickey having once being sold in stores. Molecular Keep Out technology that changes physical properties to that of roughly onyx, dark and impenetrable. At a distance, it appears as if a small crystal ball hovers between two neck clasps, jutting out like a second Adam’s apple.

Sitting at the water’s edge, having gotten some distance from the frog, the youth polishes the crystal, real careful like sonic cleaner, sun scatter revealing enfolded intricacies within the ball itself. The child whispers to no one, “the projector is your protector-”

The child goes lost, holding the crystal ball up to an eye, squinting in with the other. Inside, something more than all the collected wonder and beauty of the known universe, just possibly maybe. Much more than some claim this world can naturally possess. And yet this child can look into that impossible trancelike void and survive its haunting lattices, navigate with ease, the webs and tendrils of light that float inside, geometric and organic shapes that dance and collide, explosions of color and beauty more profound than the most dazzling fireworks from crumbling billboard poster tack of far off holiday horizons, or promised in paint in Ravensway’s main gallery.

The frog clears his throat, as only a giant bullfrog could, flapping wrinkled timbre from an overstretched throat beginning to sag, so that all other noisemakers in the river swamp fall silent.

The child startles at the noise, tugged free from the bauble’s grip.

“I know you’re there,” shouts youth.

“Do you remember how you lost it yet, why we came out here to begin with?”

“We weren’t out here.”

“No, we weren’t, but you must have been.”

The child nods the affirmative, absently, and puts the necklace full on.

Frog extends his hand and the two walk into the haunted steamship, their way relit in farcast light.

“Father said this was Mother’s favorite place in the park.”

“He built it for her, did he?”

“All of it. Maybe. Mentioned seeing her ghost here.”

“Do you believe in ghosts?”

“Real ones, or just the kind like you?”

“What if they’re one and the same.”

“You mean, what if Father made her into one of you.”

“He wouldn’t have done that, would he?”

“There is nothing really all that great about his food, except for the simple fact it will make you a superhero.”

The child goes to the crystal ball, when it lights up, the portraits sitting idly in the gallery, shimmer with activity, but remain dim and shadowy. Above the crystal ball shines a piece of paper. The child goes up to read it.

Once upon a time, in the dynamic inbred world of health care industry, energy behemoths, invasive houses of law, and breeding ground for political career trajectory, and the cutthroat food service industry that fed them fat and feasted on the crumbs, there existed the major arcana. They were just like you or me, only thing that made them different was they that were closest to the action. We’ve become them now and we are your chorus. Solitary souls connected in turn, great eyes peering out across electric eyestrain yonder. Housewives, single fathers, spinster whores, the crack addicted, youth screwed in straight, the bent and broken beautiful all among them. Lived in, fresh up to bat, extraordinary human beings in every human right, the petty squabbles, the lofty dreams, the schooling and the drooling, the lease paid off bankrupt, yearning just to brush up close, take in, and make their own.

The old man just turned sixty. Twin sons ruling over some future birthright of coop competing. Split head warm docile serpents ruling an almost culinary empire. One pushed back the filth that creeped up round the door, the other locked himself away in office dealings with bureaucratic nightmares. They’d have you believe it more complex their scheming and dreaming, and it was, layers of bloody hilarious intrigue, but know when this begins they were losing the race. One of them is here with us now, the other got left behind, we won’t spoil which is amongst us yet.

The gallery is divided into different tiers. Portraits hang at various levels. The First Tier.

“It wasn’t that they were quick on the up-take, no doubt they were. It’s just, here we are.”

The people living, really truly living. That’s who we were. Smack dab, stuck in the middle of something beautiful. You just had to be there to see. They pushed us away, wanting us only so close to them. Fine, so be it. We still made it here, didn’t we.

The meals come made by hand, as the generations did it. The subtle shift in quantum foodstuff lost on old school Sicilians.

They don’t want to hear about the migrant laborers doing their best in an understaffed, poorly ventilated kitchen, trying to keep up with the literally bleeding edge, most having never lived with voices in their heads, retinal readouts, unable to speak modulated sonorous artifice through larynxd vocal cords, and denied the absolute presence of intrusive ED that permeates high tension social situations.

## 3. After work

**Source:** `arcana_src/major_arcana_rough_ass_draft__1mpmXk.txt` lines 241–256.

John meets some folk after work, a mix of coworkers and a delegation from the tribe called best friends. They go to the shadiest bar in the most expensive part of town. The drinks are, would you believe it, overpriced, the seats are leather, and the lights are as low as they can possibly go.

Smoke doesn’t hang or linger, it clings like a wild city cat forced to the highest gargoyle, having to defend itself in life against all the rest. Pleasantries are paraded about, quickly the real meat of the conversation laid bare, the gnashing begins. A one on one.

“What happened today?”

“Nothing much. Still, it never ceases to please me, this strange hub in which we live.”

“How so? There’s a reason we have that sign outside town coming and going both ways.”

“Yeah. What did the sign say? But yeah, just the nexus, between two big cities, both close by, people have to stop here, whether they want to, or not, and we see them all. Good, bad, pleasant, indifferent, ugly, beautiful, sinners, saints. It just feels like, well, the most random place in the world.”

“The sign says Take a Gamble on Graustark. Cheesy shit, but it gets them in. They got their casinos just across the bridge, but pretend all the wholesome business and suburban existence is going down over here. And I’m also sure there are more, far more random places, with a far greater cross-section of humanity, than good ol Graustark.”

“Yeah, but we got fresh off the boat folk from Asia and South America and just about everywhere else, and I know there’s been bad blood, but it’s mostly cooled off. Things are generally happy here, save for the odd massive disaster of epic proportion, or MDEPs as we call them.”

“Biblical, is what this place is.”

“Always a trip, my uncle says.”

A female arm on each shoulder, everyone watches as it comes down, settles, moves her way between each one.

“Gentlemen.”

Eyes turn up and orient.

Who will be the first to answer. Those nearest are Slim Jim Joachim and Frasier Temple, a comfortable pair of nerds with seemingly opposite trajectories. Slim Jim did the whole nice guy thug thing in his early twenties, stringy as the name would suggest, slow and easy going and quick to anger, maturing in the general sense of the word, ever more slowly becoming wise to the nature of the world, having fucked up a good deal of his brief existence already and learned from the mistakes. Frasier is a bit more difficult, an almost prodigy who refuses to go with the flow. Bespectacled with long premature gray and the skin of a babe, he almost sings out hilarity, but said hilarity has earned him enough enemies who keep him slightly hounded and success at arm’s reach.

"Ever notice how information, the really good shit, is just out of reach. "In the way that I really feel I need to do some digging, get my hands dirty, and start answering these questions for real."

"From the lowest place I can get some concrete, legit and comprehensive answers, and continue on to the very top where the great secrets hidden from us have finally come to light."
