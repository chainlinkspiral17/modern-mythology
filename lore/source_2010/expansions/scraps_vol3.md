Cameron Stephenson - An old-fashioned inventor type, has a workshop. At seventy-seven, his mind is a whirling dervish of activity. His visions of the future, of science, technology, awe and wonder are the stuff of dreams and nightmares.

Jane Huxley - A thirty-six year old woman of the moment, always looking after people as best she can, and always asking about others. A flirt, gossip, grandmotherly-type, despite being quite young.

> [crux]

The key crux of the story is this: The people that make up Iris' world are themes and variations on her ideal lives she has led, or might yet lead. They are all herself, had she made different choices.

Her world is a strange shadow world of her own choosing, represented by the various threads of existence she eeks out in her associations.

Her interactions make up her life, and the end result of her life comes down to the choices she's made, each of these is reflected in the various people who inhabit her world.

It is interesting that she finds these people here, at the end of her life. Is it a hallucination, one last day dream before dying, a day in the life, just some random chaotic nonsense. It can be anything, everything, should the player invest that into it. Or not.

The game is a parallel to the world we live in. Some dreams come true, some don't. We learn, and error, and learn some more.

It makes a statement about death as a natural process, and judges nobody in their acceptance or unacceptance of it. The odd hints at the end suggest it may not really be the end, should the players seek out a perfect ending for Iris.

Iris is a tabula rasa until the player forms her, through the interactions and connections she makes over the course of her last 30 minutes of her life.

If she remains utterly passive and never leaves the room she will get a different ending as a player who explores their universe.
