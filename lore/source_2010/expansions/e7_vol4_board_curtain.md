## 3. The board *(`vol4_board`)*

**the corkboard** — *the desk · after midnight*

Four clippings on the cork. In the drawer, with the rubber bands and the dead nine-volts, a ball of red string.

You tie off on the zine first, because it's farthest out. A world where knowing comes in shrinkwrap and kids in basements cut the seal and burn it, and somebody's uncle in a missile silo laughs about the back doors he left open.

The string goes left and down to the headline. All over town. The week the idea hit and nothing happened, and then the old professors quit coming to class.

Left again, to the blog. A kid up all night in March with a story due Monday, finishing the rough of weep. Four days later he turns it in and goes to hear poems at a theater and goes home and plays a video game to wash the day off.

The string doesn't care which of these came first. It runs back and back, through the paper, through the year.

At the left edge of the clippings it stops at a card you didn't pin. An index card, block capitals, ballpoint pressed hard enough to emboss the cork behind it.

DICKENS DEAN.

Under that, smaller, in the same hand: glass tower. Pacific.

*(choice)*
- **leave it there.** — The card stays. The string is pulled tight to it like a line to a cleat.
- **pull the string a little.** — You pull. The card holds. The string keeps coming off the ball, more than the board has room for.

*(rejoin)*

There's slack left. A lot of it.

It goes past the card, past the frame, down the wall below the board to a strip of cork somebody screwed in under the light switch. Three things are already pinned there, and the pins are rusted.

A newspaper clipping, gone the color of weak tea. The section header is SUCESOS. The crime page.

*(choice)*
- **read the headline.**
- **look at the photograph.**

*(both branches show both, in the order chosen)*

**Headline:** POR PUESTO SIN PLACA RECOGE PASAJEROS EN LA AUTOPISTA INCONCLUSA. Caracas, 1978. Somebody's ballpoint has underlined *sin placa* twice. No plates.

**Photograph:** A parade on the news, shot off a television screen. Masks with too many eyes, feathers, a float shaped like a jaguar made of hubcaps. At the edge of the crowd the halftone goes strange around one girl: a yellow slicker, a smear of blue where her hair should be. The credit under the photo says only *foto: Maribel.*

*(rejoin)*

You turn the clipping over on its pin. The back is birth notices. One is circled. The name is torn away along the fold, and what's left reads *nació en Caracas, 1978, varón.*

The string runs to it.

The second thing is a lollipop wrapper. Waxed paper, flattened by a thumb. Printed on the inside, where you'd only see it once you'd eaten the candy: VALE POR UN BOLETO · LA TIERRA DE LECHE Y MIEL.

The third pin is still in the jar, and the third thing is in your hand. You don't remember picking it up.

A cardboard placard, the kind wedged against a windshield. RUTA 77 in house paint. Chacaíto. Sabana Grande. Plaza Venezuela. Petare. Below Petare a last stop painted over so many times the cardboard has a crust, and an arrow that runs off the edge of the card.

*(cg: the route card pinned last)*

The last pin goes in.

Rain on hot asphalt. The window is shut. It isn't raining here.

Headlights sweep the ceiling once, left to right, though the street below runs the other way.

## 4. Curtain call *(`vol4_curtain_call`)*

**curtain call** — *the desk · before the sun*

The desk near dawn. Every drawer pulled. The corkboard in the window light, the red string gone brown.

Four folding chairs that weren't here before, church-basement chairs, one with a lean.

**THE WRITER** *(nervously, capping a pen)*
A moment ago I was debating whether or not to stop, and here I am stopping something I swore I'd never finish.

**THE ARTIST** *(comfortable, paint under the nails)*
Pieces click. Not all of them. The rest go in the drawer with the rubber bands.

**THE HUMAN BEING** *(sitting with a book, the same book)*
The days gone by dried up, crackled, flaked. I swept them into a dustpan. Some I kept.

**THE DIRECTOR** *(at the window)*
Hold on this. Don't cut yet.

Through the glass, the street. A car idles at the curb with its parking lights on and a cardboard sign against the windshield.

**THE HUMAN BEING** *(making hallacas at the end of the desk)*
Plantain leaf, string, whatever's left over goes inside. You tie it shut and boil it and nobody asks what's in it.

**THE WRITER**
That's the book.

**THE ARTIST**
That's every book.

**THE DIRECTOR**
Somebody's standing at the back.

There is. By the door a man stands as if he's in line for a movie, holding a slip of waxed paper the way you'd hold a ticket. He doesn't come in.

His lips move. It might be *all so very meta.* It might be the name of a street.

**THE WRITER**
Every scrap on this desk, every page in that drawer. One book. It always was. You can lift it by the string.

**THE ARTIST**
There's a city up in the mountains, an hour from the coast. Four in the afternoon it rains and the streets steam like a kettle.

**THE DIRECTOR**
Cut to: the car.

Two short taps on a horn.

**THE HUMAN BEING**
Por puesto. One seat left. That's what two taps means.

**THE WRITER**
Where does it go?

**THE HUMAN BEING**
Where it always goes. Then one stop past.

The sign in the windshield says RUTA 77. The last stop on it is a crust of paint.

**THE DIRECTOR**
Lights down. Not out.

When you look again the man at the door is gone, the car is gone, and the street is wet though it hasn't rained.

**THE ARTIST**
Say the thing.

**THE WRITER**
Which thing.

**THE HUMAN BEING**
The one from the front of the book.

Four voices, nobody first:

fly not fall.
