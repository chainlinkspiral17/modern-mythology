# NORTHWIND HARBOR
### Oneironautics Inc. · 1988 · puzzle · adventure
### seven mornings · a boy, a dog, a harbor

---

## THE BOX COPY

*5:47 AM. The sodium lamps are still on and the fog hasn't decided
anything yet. Your uncle Maddox is already down at the CLARA B.,
and the dog is waiting on the porch, because the dog is always
waiting on the porch.*

*Seven mornings. Twelve places along one harbor. Everyone here is
working, and everyone talks while they work. Nobody will tell you
what to do. Somebody already said it, two screens away.*

---

## GETTING STARTED

Northwind Harbor lives on the slowstick shelf under Volume 7. Boot
the cartridge and start the week, or continue a morning-in-progress
— the harbor keeps your place between mornings.

**Controls:** everything on screen is something to press. Move
focus with the arrows or d-pad, accept to choose. The row along the
bottom walks you to the next screen over. H (or Y on a pad) opens
the HEARD list. ESC (or B) puts the morning down. And you can
always reach out and pet the dog.

## HOW TO PLAY · THE MORNING

Every morning runs 5:47 to the boat horns at 7:00 — 73 minutes,
and the clock only spends when you do. Walking to the next screen
costs four minutes. Doing a thing costs one. Lending a hand costs
three, because hands take longer than eyes.

There is more morning than the minutes allow. This is not a flaw
in the morning. Choose.

**The horn ends the morning whether or not you finished.** There
is no failing here. An errand left open keeps — things here keep —
and it carries into tomorrow, and the town notices ("still got my
glove, then"). The horn then reads the morning back to you: whether
the errand closed, and every small thing you did that nobody asked
for.

## LISTENING

The core of the game. Haulers, the ice man, the ferry clerk — they
talk AT you, one or two lines, while doing their jobs. Everything
anyone says lands in your HEARD list, and you keep it.

Each morning has one errand — a soft chain of ordinary kindness:
a glove that fits somebody's cracking hands, a flask to fill before
the CLARA B. goes out. Its hint was said aloud, once, by someone
two screens away.

Beyond the errand, the harbor is full of smaller things: a line
overheard at the bait shop opens a little chain nobody asked for.
These are optional. They are also, some mornings, the point.

## POCKETS

Five slots, drawn as an open coat lining. Five is five. If your
pockets are full, something has to be delivered before something
else can be carried.

## BOSUN

Uncle Maddox's black-mouth cur, always on screen, with more
animation frames than you. He is the hint system: what he sniffs,
noses, or stands pointedly beside is live. When he keeps looking
up the harbor, follow him.

He can be petted once per morning. The game will never ask you to.
The mornings add up anyway. Earn enough of his trust and a new
option appears at the edge of a screen: send Bosun for a plain
object one screen over — one errand of nose per morning, delivered
like the crown jewels.

## THE SEVEN MORNINGS

1. **THE GLOVE** — the breakwater gate is chained. Start small.
2. **THE FLASK** — the coffee chain. Learn the boat horns.
3. **THE SCALE** — the fish-cleaning station. You'll see.
4. **THE LETTER** — addressed to a boat that hasn't docked in years.
5. **THE ENGINE** — the CLARA B. won't start. All twelve screens.
6. **THE FOG** — visibility two screens. The signs along the bottom
   of the screen become what each place *sounds* like. You have
   been training for this since morning one.
7. **THE POSTER** — the shortest morning. Nothing is wrong.

## THE GOOD WEEK *(spoilers · read after the seventh horn)*

On the seventh morning you walk the dog. How he walks it depends
on six mornings of your hands, and the game says so, plainly, and
never scolds — zero pets is answered too, without grudge.

If every morning's errand closed by the seventh horn — every chain,
all week — the game quietly names it THE GOOD WEEK, and tells you
the manual lied by one week. The kindness chains are not counted
toward it. They were never for counting.

At the cannery wall there is a half-torn poster. Bosun will not go
near it. The horns blow. There is no explanation, then or ever,
and we will not be answering letters about it.

## THE FINE PRINT

*Oneironautics Inc. is a small company on a real coast, making its
first of these, whatever these turn out to be. No dogs were
hurried in the making of this cartridge. The fog is accurate. The
horns are accurate. If you finish a morning with time left over,
check your pockets; if you finish with nothing left undone, check
again tomorrow.*

*A game you cannot be good at, only present for.*
