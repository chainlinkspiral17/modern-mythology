# SAM'S SUMMER SHIFTS
### RANCH · 2003 · REGISTER SIM · SUMMER STORY
### twelve weeks · one register · keep your head

---

## THE BOX COPY

*The Hi-Way 30 Stop-N-Go, four miles outside Clatskanie. Ray hands
you the register key on a lanyard that smells like his truck.
"Drawer starts at eighty even. Count it however you count things."*

*Twelve weeks. Dot at 5:40. Gus and his scratchers at 6:15. The
log-truck drivers at 6:30, buying more coffee than seems
survivable. A whole summer is going to come across that counter,
and one Tuesday night in week six, so is something else.*

---

## GETTING STARTED

Sam's Summer Shifts lives on the slowstick shelf under Volume 7.
CLOCK IN starts a new summer; the game saves at the end of every
week, and CONTINUE puts you back behind the counter. If you played
our first game, the title screen remembers how last summer ended.

**Controls:** arrows or d-pad to move focus, accept to choose.
ESC clocks out — your week is kept.

## THE THREE METERS

Everything you do moves three numbers, each 0 to 10:

- **TILL** — drawer accuracy. Register craft. The eighty even.
- **REGULARS** — your standing with the 5:40 crowd. They notice
  everything and say almost nothing.
- **NERVE** — your steadiness. **If NERVE hits zero, at any point,
  Sam sets the lanyard down and walks off the job.** The summer
  ends there. Guard it.

## HOW TO PLAY · THE SHIFT

Every week opens at the register. A line of customers comes
through — Dot, Gus, hay crews, a church bus, people you'll learn —
and each one puts their items on the counter with the prices
showing. Then: **RING IT.** Three totals, one true. Add fast, pick
right.

- Ring **80% or better** across the shift and the drawer thanks
  you at count-out: TILL +1.
- Ring **under half** right and you'll find it at count-out the
  hard way: TILL −1.

Some customers bring a **counter moment** — a dime short, an
expired coupon, a six-pack and an unfinished mustache. These are
choices, and they land on REGULARS and NERVE as much as the till.
The right ring is arithmetic. The right call is not.

Two shifts are not like the others. **One week each summer Ray is
at the coast** and you run the store solo — every till swing
counts double that week, up and down. And on **Heritage Days
weekend** (week 8) the line runs eight customers long and the fake
totals sit tight against the true one. Count like a galley cook.

## HOW TO PLAY · THE WEEK

After the shift comes the week itself: one scene, three ways to
play it. The heat wave. The kid with the candy bar in his sock.
The mystery shopper. Inventory night with Ray and the county
station on the radio. Each choice moves the meters and the notes
under each option tell you honestly what kind of move it is.

The game saves after every week. Twelve weeks makes a summer.

## A WARNING ABOUT WEEK SIX

Week six's shift rings three customers. The third one is not a
customer. You cannot prevent what happens, and you are not
supposed to — the game is what you do in the moment, and then what
you do in the weeks after, which is where summers are actually
kept. Take care of your NERVE afterward. Ask Dot about 1981.

## THE ENDINGS *(spoilers · read in September)*

Three ways a summer ends, plus the early one:

- **WALKED OFF** — NERVE reached zero. The door chime rings behind
  you one last time. Ray mails your check with a note: "Door's
  open next June." He means it.
- **KEPT THE SUMMER** — you finished. The default, honest ending:
  a bonus check and a Polaroid of the storefront marked BEFORE.
  You worked twelve weeks and the place is better and the drawer
  is eighty even.
- **MADE MANAGER** — finish week 12 with TILL ≥ 6 **and** REGULARS
  ≥ 6. The envelope is not a check. It's the door key. Ray winters
  in Yuma, and Dot, without turning around: "He'll do it."

## THE FINE PRINT

*RANCH built this game on a real highway with fictional people on
it, except Dot, who is every Dot. All register totals were
computed by hand and double-checked, which is the whole point.
RANCH is not responsible for the reflex, acquired during play, of
counting other people's change back out loud. The drawer starts at
eighty even. It should end there too. Everything in between is
yours.*
