# FEY FAIRE
### Oneironautics Inc. · 1990 · dark fantasy RPG
### six nights

---

## THE BOX COPY

*The Fey Faire arrives every seventeen years in a specific town.
This time it's yours. At the ring-toss booth a woman named
Ondine says you get six nights.*

*Meet the fair-folk — one hundred and one of them, each with a
booth, a price, and a favorite line of verse. Cross their
mirrors. Choose which court you'll leave with. Or whether
you'll leave at all.*

*Combat is what happens when a conversation fails. Try not to
need it.*

---

## GETTING STARTED

Fey Faire lives on the slowstick shelf under Volume 7. Choose
NEW GAME and answer the questionnaire honestly — the game asks
your name, six memories, and one loss, and it remembers all of
them better than you'd like. Then check your pockets: you carry
ONE implement through the gate — a cold iron nail, a paper twist
of salt, a tin whistle, or a box of lucifer matches. This sets
your attack's damage type for the whole run. Choose for the feys
you expect to fail to charm.

**Controls:** everything is a choice on screen. Move focus and
accept to walk the midway cell by cell, first-person; buttons
name every action. ESC backs out and keeps your place.

## HOW TO PLAY · SIX NIGHTS

Nights are narrative time. A night advances only when you REST
at a recruited fey's booth (full restore, 5 gold tip — and your
recruits' booths earn a coin each overnight). Explore as long as
you like inside a night. On Night 6 the Faire packs up: **attend
the Night-6 Big Top show, then rest**, and the ending fires.
The show is the gate; the Faire will not close without you in
the audience.

Deaths do not advance nights. Deaths are lateral. See THE PRICE
OF DYING, and believe it.

## NEGOTIATION · four verbs, four prices

Approach a booth and talk. Every fey can be recruited; every
verb costs differently and fails differently:

| verb | needs | on failure |
|---|---|---|
| OFFER | gold (1 + the fey's tier) | can't afford = can't offer |
| PROMISE | a clean ledger (3+ unkept promises = refused) | booth locked for the night |
| THREATEN | the fey's true name | the great ones answer with violence — combat |
| RECITE | a learned quote matching their play or court | wrong play · booth locked, they think less of you |

**A failed branch locks that booth until the night advances.
Time is the currency.** And every fey has a branch it *wants* —
read the tell line on the negotiation view. Hit the want and the
recruit is warm; buy them with OFFER and they come along coolly.
The difference feeds the endings.

Promises are a list attached to your save. Every unfulfilled one
costs you with every fey — the fair-folk gossip. All of them
**come due on closing night**, one by one, in person.

## COMBAT · when the conversation fails

Turn-based, descriptive, deadly. Actions: STRIKE, CAST, USE,
TALK (reopen negotiation mid-fight), GUARD, FLEE, and EAT (a
bought provision, +18 HP for the turn). Your implement's damage
type hits weakness at ×1.5 and resistance at ×0.5 — learn the
chord: iron cuts most feys, song beats the Unseelie, salt beats
the sea-court, flame beats the wild green things. Once per
combat a recruited fey steps in with a SECOND action of their
own.

There is no level curve and no grinding. You get stronger by
recruiting feys, learning quotes, holding the right keepsakes,
and knowing things. The first time you fight anyone, you should
expect to lose. That is the tuition.

## THE BOOTH GAMES

Three carnival booths must be beaten before their keepers will
talk terms: Cobweb's test-your-strength (swing TRUE, three
times, the band shrinking), Puck's coin-in-a-glass (track the
swaps; he cheats, the puzzle doesn't), and the Erlking's wheel
(four clues, one safe sector of sixteen — and never, ever pick
sector 16). Wins pay 3 gold and stay won, even through death.

## GOLD · earn it, spend it, hide it

Sources: booth games, negotiations, your recruits' overnight
take. Sinks: paperback quotes at the Bookstall (2g), OFFER,
rest tips, provisions at the food wagons (1g). And Morgan at
the Fortune-Teller's will **stash your gold in her strongbox
for a 2-gold fee** — stashed gold is not touched by death.
Loose gold is. Decide what you can stand to lose.

## THE TRAILER

South past the last row of the parking lot there is a 1970s
Airstream on cinderblocks. The door is unlocked. The game will
not tell you this. Inside: cots to manage your party, a bookcase
of keepsakes with passive effects (twenty active at a time), a
compendium, a map, your journal, six small mirrors you should
keep an eye on, a window that shows you what you're becoming,
and a cat named Helia who will tell you — by tail — how each
recruited fey really feels about you. No fey can enter uninvited.
It is the one place on the grounds that is yours.

The trailer's WARREN board is where a broad party pays off:
sixty-one feys live beyond the fence, reached only by errand —
a party fey of their court vouches for them, and you must field
a fey whose damage type is the thing the target respects. Every
recruit is also a key: Seelie in the party haggle your OFFERs
down, an Unseelie will step in and vouch when a threat goes
wrong, a wildfey knows the ways under the fence, a song-fey
hums you a second try at a botched RECITE.

## THE MIRRORS · six doors into Fairyland

Each mirror-realm has a gate puzzle and a great one at the
bottom of it: a bouquet built in the right order · a Tempest
line spoken at the top of the wheel · an Unseelie promise made
and KEPT · a real leaf carried in from outside the Faire · a
strongman contest won cleanly · and a tent that only exists
after Night 3, where you go by sleeping. What you find in the
sixth mirror is yours, and only yours.

**The Sundering:** Titania and Oberon are estranged. Recruit one
and the other will not look at you — a permanent fork, and it
pulls your court with it. There is one way to hold them both,
and it is not available to a first-night visitor.

## THE PRICE OF DYING

Your HP reaching zero ends the fight. You wake at the deepest
checkpoint you've earned — every recruited fey opens one at
their booth, so a deep roster is a soft landing. You keep your
recruits, promises, quotes, court standing, and won puzzles.
You lose: half your loose gold, your most recent keepsake, and
**one of your six memories, by name**. Check the trailer's
mirrors. Count the cracks. On the sixth, the Faire keeps you.

## ENDINGS *(spoilers · read after closing night)*

Seven, by court standing and stranger things:

- **A ROSE** · Seelie 10+. You keep everything except one
  season, and the rose blooms for exactly one year.
- **A RED CAP** · Unseelie 10+. You keep everything except your
  name. It fits well.
- **A STAG'S ANTLER** · Wildfey 10+. You don't really leave.
  You come home seventeen years older.
- **YOU WALKED AWAY** · leave before Night 3, barely engaged.
  You say no honestly. You do not remember it.
- **BECOME THE FAIRE** · Cricket loves you and no court owns
  you. She would like to retire. You take the stool.
- **BRING BACK THE LOST** · Wildfey 8+, the Green Man's love,
  the Green completed, a promise that matches the loss you
  named at boot. He makes one exchange. It costs you your
  most-recruited friend, and it rewrites a summer in 1976.
- **YOU FORGET WHY YOU CAME** · the sixth death. The lot is
  empty. A cricket chirps. You know what that means now.

(There is also a way to complete the sixth mirror and not come
back before closing. Mab is very sorry about it.)

## AFTER · the Faire remembers

NEW GAME never forgets which endings you've seen. Once you've
seen any ending, the title screen offers **THE OFF SEASON** — a
winter walk through the closed midway, booths shuttered, the
Big Top dark, everyone you recruited wintering in their own
way. Nothing to buy, nothing to win. Go anyway.

And after three endings, someone in the trailer wakes up. He
has one conversation in him and one word to give you — the
word that lets you mend the oldest quarrel on the grounds.

## THE FINE PRINT

*Oneironautics Inc. reminds you that the fair-folk in this
cartridge are fictional, that fictional is a spectrum, and that
you should not make promises you cannot keep to anyone,
anywhere, ever. Keep receipts. Learn your Shakespeare; it is
cheaper than gold and they cannot take it from you. The
questionnaire's answers are stored locally and consulted only
by the Faire. We did not put your bedroom in the sixth mirror.
You did. · Rocha's debut. Be kind to it.*
