# PIRATE SUMMER
### Oneironautics Inc. · 2002 · JRPG / CRPG
### one week at camp

---

## THE BOX COPY

*One week at Camp Sweetgum, on the Oregon coast, in July of 1994.
You are Sam, eleven years old, with one duffel bag and a bunk in
Cabin Sturgeon. Fourteen campers. Twelve activities. Six
evenings. One buried treasure map.*

*And one cabin counselor — the tall quiet one, with the canvas
bag he does not put down — who might, actually, be a pirate.*

*Party up with your cabin-mates and go find out.*

---

## GETTING STARTED

Pirate Summer lives on the slowstick shelf under Volume 7. Boot
the cartridge and choose NEW GAME, or CONTINUE a week already in
progress — the game saves as you play. After you have finished
one summer, the shelf card grows a COUNSELOR MODE toggle (see
the last section before the fine print).

**Controls:** WASD or arrows to walk · SPACE or E to talk to
whoever you're facing, or to use whatever's in front of you ·
TAB opens the party roster · I opens the duffel · J opens your
journal · ESC backs out of anything, then puts the week down.

## HOW TO PLAY · THE WEEK

Six days, Sunday to Friday, and a Saturday morning that reads
the whole week back to you. **Days advance only when Sam sleeps**
— walk to your bunk and use it twice (the bed asks first,
because a whole day is too much to spend on a misclick). Each
day runs through the camp's ten blocks: wake, breakfast, morning
activity, lunch, afternoon activity, free time, dinner, the
evening event, quiet time, lights-out. The whole camp keeps this
schedule — every camper and counselor is somewhere specific at
every block, and the somewhere changes when the clock does.
Missing an activity fails nothing. The week is yours to spend.

Each day opens new ground: the ponds and ranges early on, the
north bluff from Wednesday's hike, and — for those who earn it —
darker and wetter places by Thursday and Friday.

## THE PARTY · fourteen campers, one knack each

Sam leads; up to three campers walk with you. Friendship rises
by doing things together, giving the right gift, and being there
at the right moment. Befriend a camper and they'll join your
party when asked (manage it from the TAB roster).

Every camper has a KNACK, and every knack opens a door somewhere:

|---|---|---|
| Tessa Ansen | PLAN AHEAD | counselor patrol timing |
| Wu Kai | READ THE SIGN | old Portuguese, old scripts |
| Marisol Cortez | LEAD THE PARTY | social checks |
| Danny Broz | CRY ON CUE | getting out of trouble |

**Who you bring is which game you play.** Most locked things
have two keys — Reggie's key ring or Nika's fingers, Wu Kai's
translation or three campers leaning on a barrel, Ollie's lungs
or Ford's tide-sense. A solo run reaches the end. A party run
sees more of the world on the way.

## THE DUFFEL, GIFTS, AND THE JOURNAL

The duffel (I) holds what you pick up. Items can be examined,
carried to where they matter, or **given as gifts** — every
camper has things they love, and the right gift is worth a
shared afternoon. Talk to a camper, choose to give, pick from
the bag.

The journal (J) keeps what you've learned. Pirate Summer runs on
**facts**: notice something — a decal, an absence, a signature —
and everyone you talk to afterward has their own read on it.
The same fact sounds different from Xavier than it does from
Jenny. That difference is the game.

There is also a slowstick console in the mess hall with a shelf
of carts, including one nobody else has ever heard of. Boot it
after dinner sometime.

## THE PLACES THAT PUSH BACK

- **The Old Man** · the downed spruce on the north bluff. Too
  heavy for one kid. Bea knows where to put her hands; three of
  you can lever it together. What's under it changes the week.
- **The boathouse** · padlocked. Reggie's key or Nika's sneak
  opens the door; the chest at the back wants Nika's fingers or
  Priya's eye for worn hardware. Read what's inside slowly.
- **The caves** · three levels, and the sea keeps its own
  schedule down there. A barrel with old Portuguese on it bars
  level two (Wu Kai reads it, or three of you force it). A
  flooded passage bars level three (Ollie swims it, Ford times
  it). What waits at the bottom is not gold, and it is better
  than gold.
- **The ghost ship** · from Thursday on, if you know where to
  stand, there is something at anchor offshore. Reaching her
  needs Ford in the party and a reason in your duffel. She is
  not hostile. She has been waiting a long time.
- **The east forest** · off-limits, and Bear enforces it with
  the patience of a man who has caught six campers this summer.
  Nika gets you past him. The forest is older than the camp,
  and it keeps more than one kind of secret.

## THE WILSON THREAD

Wilson Ashe. New this summer. Thirty-one, tall, quiet, a canvas
bag he does not let out of his sight. Six things about him don't
add up, scattered across the week — on his water bottle, in a
Tuesday absence, in the script of the Ghost Pirate Play, under
the Old Man, in a song, and in the asking. Amelie hears what the
song means; her grandmother sang it. Notice everything, keep
your friends close, and on Friday you can ask him directly.

He will not lie to you. That's the thing about Wilson.

## FRIDAY'S BUS

There is a bus stop half a mile down the camp road. On Friday —
only Friday — the 3:41 stops there, and Sam can be on it. The
game will not stop you. The game will remember.

## ENDINGS *(spoilers · read after Saturday)*

The week resolves Saturday at 6:33 AM, by who Sam grew closest
to and what Sam finished:

- **LEFT CAMP EARLY** — you took the Friday bus. What was lost.
- **THE PIRATE (Wilson Ashe)** — all six clues found, with
  Amelie's friendship at 3+ to translate the heart of it. If Sam
  also carried the leather satchel from the caves out to the
  ghost ship and spoke to the captain — Wilson's own ancestor —
  the **absolution variant** fires instead: Wilson gets his name
  back, and stays.
- **Per-camper endings** — otherwise, your highest friendship
  (3+) among Tessa, Wu Kai, Nika, Elias, or Danny writes who Sam
  becomes: the Plotter, the Reader, the Wanderer, the Birder,
  the teacher who watches for homesick kids.
- **GOOD FRIENDS** — the shared ending. Sam remembers the
  summer for a long time. So will you.

And: 1994 is not the camp's first strange summer. A date is
scratched in the caves — July 22, 1976 — and a newspaper
clipping, a buried tin, a cassette in a hollow tree, and one
counselor's long silence are all still here for a kid patient
enough to cross-reference them. The game never makes you. The
wall keeps the year either way.

## COUNSELOR MODE *(after your first summer)*

The same week as Jenny Copeland, twenty-four, head counselor,
who has been coming here since she was ten. Rosters, cabin
checks, the medical logbook, evening head-counts — and two
Thursday decisions the campers never see: what to do about the
disturbed ground at the bluff, and whether to finally ask Wilson
the question eleven summers have kept polite. Three endings of
her own. Jenny cannot leave the camp perimeter. She has never
needed to.

## THE FINE PRINT

*Oneironautics Inc. certifies that Camp Sweetgum is a real kind
of place but not a real place, that no campers were left at any
bus stop, and that the tide tables in the boathouse are accurate
for a coast that keeps its own counsel. The song is a real song;
someone's grandmother sang it first. If you hear a word you
don't know on the shortwave at 3 AM, write it down. It will
mean something to you later. That is a promise, and we keep
those. Ask Wilson.*
