# ESTUARY 4
### Oneironautics Inc. · 2005 · restoration campaign
### set in 2016 · the archive · the water · the working season · the king tide

---

## THE BOX COPY

*It is the year 2016, and somebody has finally gone back to fix
the water. You are a watershed restoration officer, assigned the
estuary between the creek mouth and the point — and your first
week is spent where every honest restoration starts: in the
county archive, reading decade-old volunteer surveys, carefully,
one person at a time.*

*Then the field season opens, and you find out what a plan is
worth in December.*

---

## GETTING STARTED

Estuary 4 sits on the slowstick shelf under Volume 7 — Tem's
grandfather's copy, the last stick of the studio's classic run.
Everything is a choice on screen: arrows or d-pad to move focus,
accept to choose, ESC to put the season down with your place
kept. A season's tides and storms are rolled once at the start
and saved — a continued campaign meets the same weather.

## CHAPTER ONE · THE ARCHIVE

Three volunteer files from the 2000s, and the one you build your
plan on sets the campaign's whole vocabulary:

- **the 2001 tide-gate logbook** — THE LINE · hydrology first
- **the 2002 species censuses** — THE LIVING · habitat first
- **the 2006 debris-field photographs** — THE LOST · cleanup first

If a certain December 2003 king-tide notebook has already been
filed from this shelf, there is a fourth folder in the box, two
ruled lines per station, in a hand you will recognize. Choosing
it sets the plan to the register you once walked.

## CHAPTER TWO · THE THREE CALLS

The season's three hard calls, each argued back at you in the
voice of the file you trusted: the **tide gate** (repair the
2001 gate or breach it for good), the **channel** (dredge the
bar or let the point keep rearranging its doorstep), the
**plantings** (eelgrass at scale or the slow native seedbank).
The calls are plans. Then the season starts, and plans meet
water.

## THE WORKING SEASON · thirteen field weeks

Late April to November, one decision per week, and the week's
water shown before you choose:

- **BUDGET.** You open the season with **$10** of county money.
  A week on the gate or the plantings costs $1; a week in the
  channel costs $2. **The county grant window is field week
  three, mid-May, this week and never again** — spend the week
  at a desk and it pays $6. Nobody in the field will believe it
  was a real week's work. It was.
- **THE TIDE BOOK.** Each week's water is low, middling, or
  high. Gate and channel work needs the flats — **high water
  closes both for the week**. The plantings don't care.
- **CREW MORALE (0–5).** A crew in good heart (4+) works +1; a
  worn-out crew (1 or less) works −1. Resting the crew for a
  week restores 2. A **hard push** adds +1 progress to any work
  week and costs 1 morale; **careful** work costs none.
- **THE ROLL IS SHOWN.** Luck (0–2) plus your modifiers (+1 low
  water on tidal work, ±1 morale) against the job — the gate
  works at 2, the channel at 3, the plantings at 1. A strong
  week gains 2, a fair week 1, and some weeks the water wins
  and you gain nothing but what the crew learned about the site.
- **STORMS.** From mid-May on, roughly three weeks in ten. **The
  glass warns one week ahead.** Spend the warned week lashing
  down and the storm has to argue for everything it wants.
  Ignore the glass and the storm finds whatever is half-built
  and least finished, takes a week of it back, and marks it
  STORM-DAMAGED. Repairing damage costs a full week and builds
  nothing — it only makes the work whole again.

Three projects, three finish lines: the gate wants five good
weeks, the channel five, the plantings four. Thirteen weeks is
not enough to do everything well. That is the season.

## CHAPTER THREE · THE HUNTER

The cabin in the east forest, the door note gone — because he's
back; it's spring. Ashford Cade, seventies, has hunted this
watershed since before the county kept files on it. Walk the
line with him or don't. He is a second archive, oral, and he
disagrees with the county's paper in exactly two places, and he
is right about one of them. Which one depends on your plan. Ask
him about the island offshore exactly once.

## CHAPTER FOUR · THE KING TIDE

December 2016. The year's highest water arrives to inspect the
work, and it does not grade on effort. Project by project the
survey reads what you actually built: **held** · **held,
mostly — the water edited the margins** · **went, back to the
survey line**. A project the storm damaged and you never
repaired reads a full grade worse. Then you walk the wrack line
and file the survey, because that is how this line of games has
always ended.

## THE THREE SURVEYS *(spoilers · read after December)*

The ending reads two things together: whether your three calls
held to the plan you chose in the archive, and how much of what
you built survived the water.

- **THE ESTUARY REMEMBERS** — all three calls aligned with the
  plan AND most of what you built held strong. The water takes
  the shape you argued for.
- **THE WATER DECIDES** — mixed calls or a mixed season. Half
  the plan holds and the estuary edits the rest, not unkindly.
- **THE SECOND SURVEY** — a contrarian season, or one the water
  mostly took back. The work partly fails, and the failure is
  the most valuable data the county has collected in a decade.

The last line is always the same: *Filed with the county.
Someone will read it. Someone always does — it just takes the
county a while to grow the right someone.*

## THE FINE PRINT

*Oneironautics Inc. imagined the future and it looked like
better weather, not better screens. This cartridge is set
eleven years from now and contains no flying anything. The
tide tables for 2016 are as accurate as a 2005 studio could
make them for a coast that does not exist. The county grant
process has been simplified for playability; the real one also
works, eventually, and nobody believes those weeks either.
Mr. Cade appears by arrangement and answers one question about
the island.*
