# READING MODERN MYTHOLOGY
### a reader's guide to volumes 5–7

---

## THE BOOK

Modern Mythology is a literary visual novel read in volumes. From
the main menu, pick a volume on the right and a chapter within it;
NEW GAME starts at the beginning, CONTINUE returns you to your
page. Volumes 5 through 7 — *Major Arcana*, *Planned Community*,
and *Land of Milk & Honey* — are one continuous saga, and this
guide is about reading them.

## HOW TO READ

Click (or press accept on a pad) to turn to the next line. If a
line is still typing, the first press completes it and the second
advances. ESC opens the in-game menu — save, settings, back to the
shelf — and always returns you to exactly where you were.

Choices are rare — a handful across all three volumes — and every
one is a turning point, presented as a plate: the scene dims but
stays visible behind a centered column of options, ornamented like
a book plate. Options are plain text; the one under your hand
takes a thin gold underline. Some carry a quiet annotation like
"· logic 3 ·" — a skill check, read against the dots in the top
bar. Number keys work too.

## WHAT YOU WILL SEE

The presentation is a comic read in 3D space, not a film. Knowing
its grammar makes it read better:

- **Chapter cards.** At every volume and chapter boundary a plate
  appears — VOLUME N · CHAPTER X over the chapter's title — and
  waits for you. Music and the first line arrive together as it
  clears.
- **Fades.** Scene changes breathe: a short fade to black, then
  the new place revealed. Nothing hard-cuts between scenes.
- **Shots.** Within a scene the camera cuts like panels on a
  page: a wide that teaches you the room, closeups for faces and
  decisions, inserts that hold a prop — the register, the tape,
  the note — just before the text names it. Thin letterbox bars
  appear on the close panels and leave on the wides. Sometimes a
  shot drifts, a slow push-in while the text lingers; that is
  deliberate, and it is rationed.
- **Panels.** Occasionally a flat card — a receipt, a page, a
  diagram — appears over the scene in a comic border, then
  dismisses.
- **Beats.** A few times per chapter, a line lands with a
  synchronized emphasis: a sting, a breath of camera, a pulse of
  the letterbox, all at once. A held hush, a hard hit, a chill,
  or a lift. You will feel these more than notice them.
- **Kinetic text.** Rarely, three or four words in a line will
  waver, shake, or trail off. When the walls are thin, the type
  knows.

## PACE IT YOUR WAY

Everything about tempo is yours, under SETTINGS:

- **TEXT SPEED** — slow, normal, fast. The typewriter also reads
  with the prose at any speed: it holds longer at full stops,
  em-dashes, and ellipses, shorter at commas.
- **AUTO ADVANCE** — off, or 1 / 3 / 5 seconds after a line
  finishes.
- **SKIP UNREAD TEXT** — for revisiting chapters.
- **FONT SIZE**, screen size, music, effects, and controller
  rumble round out the panel.

## THE TOP BAR

A thin bar carries the volume's title with the current chapter
whispered beside it — "· CH II · A Fool Between Acts" — so you
always know where you are in the book. Beside it, five skill dots
(empathy, logic, composure, rhetoric, signal) that the rare
choices read. The menu lives on the far side.

## A CLEAN PICTURE · F4

Press F4 to hide every overlay — bar, dialogue, all of it — for a
clean look at the picture. The image itself (including the
letterbox, which is part of the picture) stays. Press F4 again to
bring the page back. Screenshot freely.

## THE GAMES INSIDE THE BOOK

Two large game insets belong to the saga, and both are entered
from the reading rather than interrupting it:

- **TAROT GAUNTLET** (Volume 5). As you read *Major Arcana*, the
  arcana surface as cards in the GALLERY; each opens a scenario
  set in one of the volume's real places at a specific hour. THE
  MAJOR ARCANA on the main menu runs all twenty-two as one
  ladder, with your reading marked alongside — reading lights the
  ladder but never grants an in-game advantage. It has its own
  guide: `tarot_gauntlet.md`.
- **COMMUNITY PLANNED** (Volume 6). *Planned Community* is a
  novel in two parts with a held middle — and at the seam between
  them, the gallery offers the game that IS that middle. Play it
  there, later, alongside, or never; the book reads cleanly in
  every case. Its guide: `community_planned.md`.

Neither game authors the novel. What they earn you is more to
read.

## THE SHELF

Volume 7 keeps a cabin, and the cabin keeps a shelf: a personal
library of slowstock cartridges — a whole catalog of games from
another timeline's history, each with its own manual. SLOWSTOCK
LIBRARY on the main menu opens it directly. A few cartridges are
playable from the start; the rest unlock as you finish others,
in the order the story of the shelf itself discovers them —
locked slots sit dim on the wood until their moment. Hover any
cartridge for its cover blurb; boot from the side panel.

One more thing, said without spoiling it: the games on that shelf
remember each other. Finishing certain things in one leaves a
token that another will quietly recognize. Endings you have seen,
things you have returned, songs you have heard — they travel.

## THE SHAPE OF THE THREE VOLUMES

Spoiler-free bearings only:

- **Volume 5 · Major Arcana.** Twenty-two cards dealt as chapters.
  It opens in a 24-hour diner shaped like a riverboat, at 3:47 in
  the morning, in a town where time is liquid and the walls are
  thin. Each arcana is a person, a place, and an hour; the deck is
  a cycle, and the cycle closes.
- **Volume 6 · Planned Community.** One summer, told in two parts
  — *Summer's Start* and *End of Summer Begins* — with the middle
  held in ellipsis. A man, a board, a network, and a tower at the
  treeline. The title of its game is the title of the book,
  inverted; the inversion is the saga's signature move.
- **Volume 7 · Land of Milk & Honey.** After the summer: a cabin,
  a family, and the shelf of old games through which the past
  keeps arriving in playable form. The gentlest of the three
  volumes, and the one where the most is finally said.

Read them in order. The volumes talk to each other, and the later
pages assume you heard the earlier ones.

---

## A CLOSING NOTE

There is no wrong pace. The book saves your place, the settings
bend to your reading speed, the games wait indefinitely, and
nothing on the shelf expires. If a line wavers on the screen, let
it. If a shot holds on an empty counter a beat longer than seems
necessary, it isn't a bug — something is being shown before it is
said. That is the whole grammar of this book: the picture first,
then the caption. Turn the page when you're ready.
