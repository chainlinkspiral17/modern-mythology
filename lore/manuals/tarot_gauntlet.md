# TAROT GAUNTLET
### the Major Arcana inset · one room · one hour · one card

---

## WHAT IT IS

Every card of the Major Arcana is a scenario, and every scenario is
a real place at a specific time: D'Ambrosio's diner at 3:47 AM, the
warehouse at 9:47 PM, the bungalow with the storage closet ajar, the
riverboat on a Friday service, the cathedral, the Roberts house, the
bar, the lighthouse. You play a *hand* — a character whose deck of
action cards is their way of being in a room — and you have a
limited number of turns to do one specific thing before the room's
hold on you closes.

The gauntlet is a board game you stand inside. The board is the
room. The difficulty is the hour.

## GETTING THERE

The arcana surface in the GALLERY as you read Volume 5 — open an
arcana's card and a scenario picker offers its setups side by side.
THE MAJOR ARCANA on the main menu runs the Fool's Journey: all
twenty-two gauntlets in canonical order as one ladder, with your
reading of the volume marked alongside (reading lights the ladder;
it never grants an in-game advantage). THE SPREAD deals three
arcana as a single run — more on that below.

## THE FIVE PHASES

Each turn cycles through five phases; the label at the top of the
screen always says which. The gold **Advance →** button is the one
accented control on the screen — it is what keeps the game moving
(a 2026-07 UI pass made sure your eye lands on it first).

- **ACTION** — play cards from your hand, walk the room, connect
  with visitors. Yours.
- **PLANNING** — buy cards from the tableau, Time refreshes,
  played starter cards return to your hand for free. Yours.
- **SHADOW** — the room takes its turn: a Gravity card flips and
  resolves against you. Read it, then Advance.
- **DRIFT** — unclaimed visitors drift toward the room's attractor
  spots.
- **UPKEEP** — cleanup. Inertia ticks. Claimed visitors edge
  toward being consumed. Then the next turn begins.

## TIME

Time is the turn's budget — 6 per turn as the baseline. Every card
costs its printed Time; moving costs Time; buying costs Time.
Unspent Time carries over and adds to the base: end a turn with 2
left and the next starts with 8. Frugality is a strategy, not a
missed opportunity.

## THE HAND AND THE TABLEAU

Two rows at the bottom of the screen. The lower row is your HAND —
click a card to play it. The upper row is the TABLEAU: non-starter
cards available for purchase during PLANNING, each showing its
remaining stock right on the tile (stock is finite; a sold-out card
disappears). Starter cards are the hand's rhythm — they return to
your hand free every turn. Bought cards are the run's shape.

## THE THRESHOLD DICE

Frame cards — WALK, SPRINT, FOCUS, SEARCH, SHORT REST, LONG REST,
DISTRACTION, GUARD, CLOSE CALL, SPEND IT, IMPROVISE — roll the
Threshold dice, and the outcome determines what they do. Each
arcana swaps in its own six-face die with its own symbols: the
Fool's die runs from LEAP (double success) through PAUSE and the
partials down to WIPE — and a wild face. FOCUS banks bonus dice
for the next roll; CLOSE CALL changes how the next roll can be
rerolled. The die is the room's weather; the cards are your gait.

## MOVING · SEEING · THE MAP

The default view is first person: you see the room from the space
you are standing in, and the game occasionally borrows your eyes
for a beat — a brief cut to where a visitor just arrived, then
back. Click **MOVE ↪** (or an adjacent space marker) to walk one
hop for 1 Time, no card needed. The expand button on the board
header opens the fullscreen top-down MAP — every space, marker,
and meeple at once. Esc drops you back into your own eyes.

## VISITORS

People arrive on schedules — some are there when you start, some
walk in mid-run, each announced. Every visitor connects through
ONE action verb at ONE place with ONE gesture, and their card
tells you the triple plainly. A connection is a four-beat arc:
**greet → listen → deliver → sit with**. Approach, exchange,
transaction, closure. The room will try to claim visitors before
you reach them; a claimed visitor left too long is consumed, and
consumed visitors are one of the ways a run ends.

Faith, who appears in the diner scenarios, is a dog. The portrait
knows it. Keep her adjacent; she matters.

## THE GRAVITY DECK

During SHADOW the room draws from its Gravity deck and the card
resolves automatically — an Inertia tick, a claim on a visitor, a
forced choice. GUARD cards played earlier in the turn absorb the
next Inertia tick; DISTRACTION cards can cancel the top Gravity
card outright. At Inertia 7+ the deck thickens. The room notices
you staying.

## WINNING · LOSING

A gauntlet win almost always has three parts: something done,
someone reached, and something *not* done — and the restraint is
usually where the card's actual lesson lives.

The Fool's canonical run, THE LEAP, is the template: assemble the
BUNDLE (stick + cloth + a contents item — it enters your hand by
itself once you hold all three), connect with at least three
visitors, keep Faith adjacent, and play LEAP at an open threshold
while Inertia is under 7.

Losses are named, and the names teach. The universal one: Inertia
reaching 12 — *the 24-hour diner of the soul*, the loop that
closes because you let the room set the pace. Beyond that, every
scenario carries its own specific named losses tied to specific
choices, so when a run ends you know exactly which door you
walked through. Sanity is the other floor: the room wears it
down; a Short Rest steadies it.

Each arcana also runs its own local economy — the Magician's
Inspiration against Stagnation and Doubt, the Priestess's Insight
and the master reel, the Empress's Bloom and Harvest, and so on.
The HUD only shows the meters your scenario uses.

## THE HOUR IS THE DIFFICULTY

Every scenario is anchored to a clock time, and the clock IS the
difficulty axis. Dawn is gentle — the world hasn't woken up.
Mid-morning is public but forgiving. Dusk is the liminal canonical
hour. Late night is crisis. Post-midnight is the vigil that has
become the room.

Every arcana ships at least three setups: an easier bookend, the
canonical scenario, and a harder bookend — same place, same hand,
different hour and different pressure. The hard one is hard
because the character is asked to sit in a role they are not
ready for, not because anything got stronger. Some arcana carry a
fourth setup, and cameo scenarios let one character's deck visit
another's room.

## THE SPREAD

The scenario picker's violet-bordered option deals three distinct
arcana face-down as one reading. Redraw freely until the first
scenario starts; then the spread is committed. Between cards:

- **Sanity carries** — damage follows you, floored at 2 so every
  third card stays winnable. Surplus does not snowball.
- **Inertia carries at half**, rounded down, capped at 3. Rooms
  remember you were just in another room.
- **On a win you keep one card** from your final hand; it opens
  the next scenario in your opening hand. This is the spread's
  one real decision.

A loss ends the spread — the reading records what completed. Three
wins is a full reading, dealt face-up with its ending tokens: the
gauntlet's first artifact that is about a sequence, not a room.

## A CLOSING NOTE

The gauntlet keeps what you find — lore tokens, endings, the
scrapbook's growing arcana pages — but none of it is required
reading, and none of it changes the novel. The cards were always
about the same thing the book is about: a person, a room, an hour,
and the one thing they will or will not do in it. Lose a run and
read the name of the loss. That's the card teaching. Draw again.
