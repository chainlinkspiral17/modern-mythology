# ROFLCOPTR · scope, and the years · 1997–2027

Two things this file fixes: **how much** of the strip the volume
actually shows, and **what happens in every year** of the thirty-
one. The foreword and afterword are written last and are not
outlined here beyond what the years hand them.

Everything below obeys `_AUTHORIAL_RULE.md`: the events are what
the strip shows (Wood's world) and what the reader infers (Arthur's
life). The afterword can name the second column later; the strips
never do.

---

## PART ONE · SCOPE

### The archive versus the book

In fiction there are over ten thousand strips and pages. The
volume is the retrospective, which is a *selection* — the editor's
cut — and the VN can only show what gets made. So the scope is two
numbers per run: what exists in the boxes, and what the reader
sees.

| run | years | in the boxes (fiction) | in the book (the volume) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · The Timberline | 1997–99 | ~40 | 14 |
| 2 · The Weekday Daily | 1999–2003 | ~1,000 | 30 |
| 3 · Newspaper, early | 2004–11 | ~2,100 dailies · ~350 Sundays | 44 |
| 4 · Newspaper, peak | 2012–Jul 2014 | ~750 dailies · ~130 Sundays | 24 |
| 5 · Low Tide | Nov–Dec 2014 | ~35 | 12 (all of it that matters) |
| 6 · The Last Sunday | 2015–2020 | ~1,500 dailies · ~250 Sundays | 36 |
| 7 · ROFLCOPTER | 2021–27 | ~2,800 pages | 40 |
| **total** | | **~10,000** | **~200** |

Two hundred units is the working target. It is the size of a real
retrospective's "selected" section, it is achievable, and it is
enough that the reader feels the years. If it grows, it grows in
Runs 3 and 6, where the years are longest.

### Three tiers of unit

Not every unit needs finished art. The tiers, and the working
split of the ~200:

| tier | what the reader sees | count | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A · drawn** | a finished strip or page as an image: the art is the content | ~70 | every Sunday landscape, every silent page, the specified pages, the last public Sunday, the final page, the corner boxes. Art-directed from `the_strip.md` |
| **B · scripted** | the strip presented as its panel script in the strip's own grid — panel captions and balloons, typeset, on the era's paper — with a spot drawing where one exists | ~110 | most dailies. Cheap to author, honest about what it is, and reads like a script in a retrospective's notes section |
| **C · implied** | dividers, counts, gaps, reprints, "no file" | ~20 | the box number and dates; the sentence *nine months of reprints*; the blank divider for January 2021 |

Tier B is the load-bearing decision: the volume can be *read* long
before it can be *seen*, and the panel-script format is already
what the brief and `sample_strips.md` are written in. Tier A gets
drawn in the order the foreshadowing index needs them
(`foreword_afterword.md`).

### Drawing order for Tier A (proposed)

1. "The Empty Chair" and "The Box" (specified; the volume's canon).
2. The final page (April 2027).
3. The last public Sunday (Dec 27, 2020).
4. The Greyhound station (Oct 2000).
5. "Lanes" (2009) and "Lanes" again (2019).
6. The dock at 11:30 PM (July 2014).
7. The three corner-box Sundays (2002, 2008, 2016).
8. The first ROFLCOPTER page (main street twice).
9. "The Carry."
10. Everything else, in run order.

### What the concept sheet already covers

Era IV Wood, the bait shop, the waterfront, the bird, the eye
sign, the stone head. Sheets still needed: Wood at seventeen (the
overcoat); Wood at twenty-six (the CRT); Wood at thirty-four (the
thrift-store suit); the strip's Chloe at three ages; Gully at
three ages; both dogs; the garage apartment interior; the frame
shop; the Timberline newsroom; the Driftwood Motel desk; Gully's
interior. See `_CONCEPT_ART.md`.

### What is out of scope

- The parody week itself as finished art (Tier B: it's a stick
  figure; the reader gets the idea from four scripted strips).
- Any strip that would require the author on the page.
- Any strip whose only job is to state a fact. If the year's fact
  can't be drawn as a gap or a background, it goes to the
  afterword.

## PART TWO · THE YEARS

Each year: the cadence and the line; what the strip shows (Wood);
what the reader infers (Arthur); the units the book takes from
that year; the margin mark. Counts are in-fiction. Units are the
book's, tier in brackets.

Ages: Arthur is born in 1980. Barnaby is found at ~1 in 1999.

### 1997 · age 17 · Run 1 · every two weeks

- **Line.** Crow-quill and ballpoint on cardstock, xeroxed. The
  cluttered balloons.
- **Wood.** September: *Drift Wood* #1 in *The Timberline*. The
  cafeteria; Truffaut to the lunch lady; corn. Wood is a brilliant
  exile. Chloe is the culture editor with the red pen; Gully is in
  the back row drawing teachers. Nine strips by December.
- **Arthur.** Editor of the paper. Most Likely to Win a Pulitzer.
  Lives in his childhood bedroom.
- **Units.** #1 [A — the first strip, drawn]; the cashier strip
  [B]; a Chloe-and-Wood marginalia strip [B].
- **Mark.** A gull.

### 1998 · age 18 · Run 1

- **Wood.** Eighteen strips. The strip finds its two-hander: Wood
  monologues, Chloe dismantles. A Sunday-sized special for the
  paper's spring issue — Wood's first landscape, the estuary at
  low tide, over-inked, with a caption that explains it. (The last
  landscape he'll caption.)
- **Arthur.** Starts the phantom graphic novel. Applies to PNCA.
- **Units.** The spring landscape [A]; two dailies [B].
- **Mark.** A gull. Once, a crab.

### 1999 · age 19 · Run 1 → Run 2

- **Wood.** March: #21, the red pen, "I'm going to Seattle." "I
  know." June: the last issue. September: five a week for the
  Newport college paper. The strip gets faster. The overcoat
  stays. A dog appears one Monday in November, under the table,
  with no introduction, and is there every day after.
- **Arthur.** Chloe leaves for college. He does not. He enrolls
  non-degree in Newport (lean) for the print shop. Finds Barnaby
  tied to the dumpster behind the bowling alley.
- **Units.** #21 [B]; the last issue [C — a divider: *the last
  issue A. Finch edited*]; the first weekday daily [B]; the dog
  Monday [A].
- **Mark.** A gull. From November, a gull with a dog beside it —
  once, then never again while the dog lives.

### 2000 · age 20 · Run 2 · Arc 1

- **Wood.** The scholarship, as the strip tells it: Wood was too
  good for it. Heceta Frame & Matting. September: the three
  Seattle dailies, then the framing shop Wednesday. October: the
  fried-clam daily; the Greyhound Sunday. Rain in vertical
  hatching.
- **Arthur.** Turns down PNCA. Takes the framing-shop job. Chloe's
  bus.
- **Units.** The scholarship daily [B]; Seattle Mon/Tue/Wed [B ×3];
  fried clams [B]; the Greyhound Sunday [A].
- **Mark.** A gull. The Greyhound Sunday: a crab, facing away.

### 2001 · age 21 · Run 2

- **Wood.** Lot C is for cowards. Wood at the frame shop learning
  the town's walls by what people frame: fishing licenses,
  diplomas, a wedding photo from 1971. A run of dailies where each
  customer's frame job is the strip.
- **Arthur.** Weekends on Box 4B: the crime noir. Sixty pages,
  2001–03.
- **Units.** Lot C [B]; two frame-job dailies [B].
- **Mark.** A gull.

### 2002 · age 22 · Run 2

- **Wood.** June 2: the logging-road Sunday with the corner box —
  *COMING 2003 · "SLASH."* A noir Sunday in October: Wood in a
  trench coat under the bowling-alley neon, played straight, never
  repeated.
- **Arthur.** The noir is going nowhere and he's told the strip it
  isn't.
- **Units.** The corner-box Sunday [A]; the noir Sunday [A].
- **Mark.** A gull. The noir Sunday: none — the only unsigned
  Sunday of the run.

### 2003 · age 23 · Run 2 → the first private stretch

- **Wood.** May 23: "For who?" "The rack." Then five a week that
  nobody prints. Gully inherits a shack by the boat launch and the
  strip spends a week on the espresso machine arriving on a
  flatbed.
- **Arthur.** Stops going to Newport. Todd opens Gully's Tackle &
  Roast. Draws for most of a year for no one.
- **Units.** The rack [B]; the espresso-machine week, one daily
  [B]; a divider [C]: *Box 5 is full. None of it ran.*
- **Mark.** A gull, then for four months no mark at all.

### 2004 · age 24 · Run 3 begins · six dailies and a Sunday

- **Line.** Brush-pen scanned, muddy digital flats. The first
  Sundays in color; the color is bad.
- **Wood.** Online. A newspaper cadence with no newspaper. Wood's
  apartment is suddenly a garage behind a tavern; no moving-day
  strip. The first watercolor Sunday, tentative, over-wet.
- **Arthur.** Puts the strip on the web. Moves to the garage
  apartment (lean: this year). A few hundred readers.
- **Units.** The first web daily [B]; the garage apartment's first
  appearance [B]; the first color Sunday [A].
- **Mark.** A gull, now digital, then hand-drawn again within a
  month.

### 2005 · age 25 · Run 3

- **Wood.** March 16: the one grease sack. Wood turns twenty-five
  in a strip where Gully brings a cake to the frame shop and the
  shop's hours are posted, shorter, in the background. The
  Sundays get better.
- **Arthur.** Twenty-five. Five hundred loose pages. Father sick.
  Hours cut.
- **Units.** The grease sack [B]; the birthday [B]; a Sunday [A].
- **Mark.** A gull, small.

### 2006 · age 26 · Run 3 · Arc 2

- **Wood.** November: the *ROLFCOPTR* week. Then *Drift Wood*
  returns with Wood at two-thirds size. The muffler-shop printout.
  Six weeks small.
- **Arthur.** The 72-hour sprint. Digg. The 509. $800 a month.
  Saved from eviction. The ROLFCOPTR guy.
- **Units.** 3:42 AM [B, the brief]; four parody strips [B]; the
  week after [B]; the muffler shop [B]; one Sunday from the small
  weeks [A].
- **Mark.** During the parody week: none. After: a gull, at two-
  thirds size.

### 2007 · age 27 · Run 3

- **Wood.** Normal size again, unannounced. The strip is
  suddenly read by forty thousand people who want the helicopter,
  and it draws them: a run of dailies where tourists in the
  background of Small Wood wear the stick figure on t-shirts.
  Nobody in the strip mentions it.
- **Arthur.** Kills the parody. Keeps the banner money a year.
  Refuses the first licensing letter.
- **Units.** The t-shirt tourists [B ×2]; a landscape Sunday [A].
- **Mark.** A gull.

### 2008 · age 28 · Run 3

- **Wood.** The coastal weeklies: the strip draws itself in a
  newspaper for the first time, next to the tide tables, and Wood
  reads it at Gully's counter. June 1: the corner box, *COMING
  2009 · "THE LONG WAY BACK."* A travelogue Sunday, Wood and
  Chloe walking the abandoned cedar mill — the strip's Chloe back
  for the first time in years, her hair different, her coat
  expensive.
- **Arthur.** Syndication in three weeklies and one Portland
  alt-weekly. The travelogue draft. Chloe visits.
- **Units.** The tide-tables daily [B]; the corner box [A]; the
  cedar-mill Sunday [A].
- **Mark.** A gull. The cedar-mill Sunday: a gull and a second,

### 2009 · age 29 · Run 3

- **Wood.** March 8: "Lanes" — the first true silent Sunday. The
  neon with the L out; the wet ring of rope on the chain-link; on
  the ridge east of town, a glow the composition treats as weather.
  Gully marries a county nurse in a daily where Wood is best man
  and the strip cuts before the speech.
- **Arthur.** Todd marries Maria. The glow on the ridge appears in
  the landscapes from here on; he never asks what it is.
- **Units.** "Lanes" [A]; the wedding daily [B].
- **Mark.** "Lanes": a gull and a dog beside it — the only time

### 2010 · age 30 · Run 3 · the first sabbatical

- **Wood.** Nine months of reprints: 2001 dailies re-inked in the
  2010 hand. The reader sees the same strips again, better drawn,
  and understands something has happened.
- **Arthur.** His father dies (lean). Nine months off. Re-inks his
  twenty-year-old self.
- **Units.** A divider [C]: *January–September 2010: reprints*; one
  re-inked 2001 daily beside its original [A — the pair is the
  point]; the return daily in October [B].
- **Mark.** On the reprints: the 2001 gull, redrawn. On the
  return: a gull, and under it, very small, a second date.

### 2011 · age 31 · Run 3 ends

- **Wood.** The strip is thirty-one; so is Wood. Wagner's
  Hardware on the corner closes in the backgrounds over a month of
  dailies and no one says so. The dog is twelve and the walks are
  shorter; the panels are wider.
- **Arthur.** The frame shop lets him go (lean). Applies at the
  Driftwood Motel.
- **Units.** The hardware-store backgrounds [B ×2]; a Sunday [A].
- **Mark.** A gull.

### 2012 · age 32 · Run 4 · Era III line

- **Units.** The motel map [B]; the Subaru's first daily [B]; two

- **Mark.** A gull, drawn now with one confident stroke.

## PART THREE · READING THE TABLE

- **The Sundays carry the years.** Roughly one drawn Sunday per
  year through the public run; more in the years the brief
  specifies. The dailies are scripted and cheap.
- **The marks are a stat line.** Gull → gull-and-dog (once) → gull
  → none → gull at two-thirds → flat stroke → scruff → gull-and-
  scruff → *ROFLCOPTER* → *Arthur Finch*. The reader who tracks
  the bottom-right corner reads the whole life.
- **The gaps are chapters.** 2003 (nobody printed it), 2010
  (reprints), 2015 (landscapes), January 2021 (no file). Each is a
  divider card with a sentence, and each is a year the afterword
  will explain.
- **The echoes stay in the background.** 2009's glow, 2011's
  hardware store, 2022's reading room, 2025's August. None is
  named. None is required.
- **The afterword gets:** the father (2005–2010), the scholarship's
  cost (2000), the money (2007, 2012), the wedding (2014), both
  dogs, the name, the burnout, the six years, and whatever the
  hardships are that the strip never drew. Those last are the only
  thing this table doesn't hold, and they're the user's to add.
