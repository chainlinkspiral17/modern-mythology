# ERAS · the line, the paper, the color, the devices

The strip's look changes four times, and inside each era the arcs
have their own palettes. Every panel prompt in
`godot/tools/comic/strips/` is prefixed with its era block from
`godot/tools/comic/eras.json`; this file is the human version, with
the things a prompt can't carry: lettering, grid, balloons, the
mark, and the strip's own visual devices.

---

## ERA I · The High School Zine Era · 1997–2003

**Runs 1–2.** *The Timberline* (biweekly), then the college paper
(weekday) and the laundromat digest.

- **Line.** Scratchy crow-quill and ballpoint, then (1999 on)
  brush-pen. Jagged, uneven weights. Heavy black spotting. Rain as
  frantic vertical hatching. No grays: everything is black or
  white and the xerox decides which.
- **Paper.** Cardstock, xeroxed hard (1997–99). Newsprint (the
  college paper). Quarter-letter xerox, stapled (the digest).
- **Color.** None. The Fourth's fireworks are white on black
  because the copier is.
- **Grid.** Biweekly: 2×2 on a half page. Daily: 1×4, square
  panels, thin hand-ruled gutters. Panel borders hand-drawn,
  slightly bowed.
- **Lettering.** Hand-lettered caps, cramped, too many words per
  balloon (1997–98) loosening to fewer (1999 on). Balloons are
  cluttered and often break the panel border. Sound effects
  hand-drawn, jagged.
- **Title.** DRIFT WOOD lettered by hand in the top-left, a
  different hand each issue in 1997, settled by 1998.
- **The mark.** A gull (two strokes), a crab (about her), none (the
  Rack; Noir Week), gull-and-dog (once, Nov 1999).
- **Sunday.** Doesn't exist. The digest **cover** (1999–2003) is
  the proto-Sunday: one image, quarter-letter, drawn on the spot.
  The Greyhound cover (Oct 2000) is the only oversized one.
- **Arc 1 palette (Oct 2000).** Brush-pen over rough blue pencil
  (the blue reproduces as faint gray ghosts in the xerox), stark
  spotting after Clowes and early Hernandez, cold coastal rain.

**Era swatch prompt:** *a reference swatch sheet for a 1990s black-
and-white zine comic strip: samples of scratchy crow-quill hatching,
ballpoint scribble, heavy black spotting, frantic vertical rain
hatching, hand-ruled panel borders slightly bowed, a cramped hand-
lettered speech balloon breaking a panel border, a tiny two-stroke
gull and a tiny crab, all on photocopied cardstock with xerox
grain, no color, no gray.*

## ERA II · The Webcomic Diaspora · 2004–2011

**Run 3.** Arthur's site on a newspaper cadence; from 2008 coastal
newsprint a week behind.

- **Line.** Brush-pen scanned. Rubbery, kinetic, blog-era minimal.
  The dailies loosen; the Sundays tighten.
- **Paper.** Bristol, scanned. Watercolor paper for Sundays,
  buckled at first (2004), flat by 2005.
- **Color.** Dailies: muddy early-digital flats, slightly
  misregistered, a limited palette that reads as the 2004 browser.
  Sundays: watercolor — over-wet and bleeding in Jan 2004,
  competent by Jan 2005, good by 2009 ("Lanes"). Rust on tin is the
  first color the strip gets right (April 2004).
- **Grid.** Daily 1×4. Sunday: 3–5 horizontal tiers, full page,
  portrait.
- **Lettering.** Still hand-lettered but cleaner; balloons stay
  inside panels now. The chorus's stick figures get lowercase, no
  punctuation. Email is drawn in a system font (Arial) the strip
  visibly resents.
- **The mark.** Gull; crab; none (parody week); gull at two-thirds
  (Nov–Dec 2006); gull-and-dog once more ("Lanes," 2009); a second
  smaller date under the mark (Oct 2010 on, sparingly).
- **Arc 2 palette (Nov 2006).** Digital flats over physical ink;
  saturated neon monitor glow cutting dark garage interiors;
  jagged hyperactive linework; the parody itself in crude
  ballpoint on nothing.
- **Reprints (2010).** 2001 scripts re-inked in the 2010 hand with
  a gray wash; the book prints pairs.

**Era swatch prompt:** *a reference swatch sheet for a mid-2000s
webcomic: samples of rubbery brush-pen line, muddy slightly
misregistered digital flat colors, a saturated CRT-glow lighting
sample, an over-wet watercolor sky that bled past its ink beside a
controlled one, rust bleeding orange into gray on corrugated tin, a
small stick figure drawn at the back of a panel, a tiny gull drawn
at two-thirds size beside a normal one.*

## ERA III · The Masterwork of Mediocrity · 2012–2020

**Runs 4–6.** Arthur's site, the coastal weeklies, from 2017 the
aggregator (which slices the Sundays).

- **Line.** Razor-sharp, ultra-confident brush and ink. Pristine
  architectural perspective (the motel lobby, the dumpster's
  rivets). Crisp silhouettes. Expressive character geometry. Zero
  crosshatch in close-ups.
- **Paper.** Bristol for dailies. 140 lb Arches cold-press for
  Sundays.
- **Color.** Dailies: black ink and at most one spot tone (gray or
  a single warm). Sundays: wet-on-wet watercolor — gray-green
  moss, coastal fog, rusted galvanized tin, bruised dusk, water
  bleeding softly over the ink edge.
- **Grid.** Daily 1×4. Sundays 3–5 tiers. 2011 (Twelve): wide
  panels. 2019: the Sunday drawn to a smaller grid because the
  papers cut it to a third of a page — the reader can measure it.
- **Lettering.** Clean hand lettering. Her notes in her cramped
  capitals; Gully's in blocky grease pencil; Wood's own notebook
  (2014) in her capitals — he learned them.
- **The mark.** Gull; crab; flat stroke (Nov 30, 2014); scruff (Dec
  21, 2014 on); scruff smaller (2019); gull-and-scruff (Dec 27,
  2020).
- **Arc 3 palette (July 2014).** Hairline brush control; blinding
  clear summer light; soft cerulean and pale linen.
- **Arc 4 palette (Nov–Dec 2014).** Desaturated wet-on-wet: raw
  umber, Payne's gray, Prussian blue, raw sienna; line goes quiet;
  water bleeds over the ink. "The Box": cerulean and titanium
  white sky, sap and hooker's greens, raw umber and sepia planks, a
  kraft-brown anchor.
- **The last Sunday (Dec 27, 2020).** Era III's line with Era IV's
  palette already arriving: ochre and slate softening in.

**Era swatch prompt:** *a reference swatch sheet for a masterwork-
era comic strip: samples of razor-sharp confident brush-and-ink
line with clean architectural perspective, a crisp silhouette, a
close-up hand with zero crosshatching, wet-on-wet watercolor
samples of gray-green moss, coastal fog, rusted galvanized tin and
a bruised dusk sky with water bleeding over an ink edge, a
desaturated Payne's gray and raw umber swatch, and a tiny three-
legged scruff of ink asleep beside a flat horizontal stroke.*

## ERA IV · The Pacific Twilight · ROFLCOPTER · 2021–2027

**Run 7.** Nobody. Boxes 21–40.

- **Line.** Spare, knowing, deeply human. Economy of line. Era I's
  black spotting brought back on purpose in the mill run (2023).
- **Paper.** Whatever's in the garage: Arches, bristol, the backs
  of motel registration cards.
- **Color.** Muted ochres, slate blues, misty sea-foam greens.
  Luminous sunbreaks in the last spring (2027).
- **Grid.** None, then some. Pages, not strips. Spreads. The one
  gridded page is the editor's (2026) — four panels, because the
  conversation wanted the form.
- **Lettering.** Almost none. One hand-lettered sentence in the
  whole private work (the mill spread). The margin: *ROFLCOPTER*,
  tiny, spelled right. The final page: *Arthur Finch, 1997–2027*.
- **Figures.** Wood absent (Jan–June 2021), then from behind, then
  a reflection, then small. The bird. The town's people at the
  ages they were.

**Era swatch prompt:** *a reference swatch sheet for a private late-
career comic page: samples of a spare knowing ink line with
generous white, soft watercolor swatches of muted ochre, slate
blue and misty sea-foam green, a luminous sunbreak wash, a figure
drawn from behind in three strokes, a reflection in rain-streaked
glass, and the word ROFLCOPTER lettered tiny in a margin.*

## THE STRIP'S OWN DEVICES

These are visual grammar the strip invented for itself. They are
canon; reuse them on purpose, never by accident.

| device | first use | what it means |
|---|---|---|
| **The narrowing panel** | Oct 2000 (Arc 1) | The town getting smaller. One panel drawn narrower than the grid; on Oct 30, 2000 a vertical slot. Retired after Arc 1. |
| **Two-thirds** | Nov 16 – Dec 2006 | Wood drawn at 2/3 scale in normal panels for six weeks after the meme. Props and Gully stay normal. Ends unannounced (Jan 22, 2007). Applied once to a landscape (Dec 10, 2006: wrong-sized from the front, right-sized from behind). |
| **From behind** | Mar 2002 (the pad), Dec 2002 (the dock) | How the strip leaves Chloe; how Wood draws himself when honest; the ROFLCOPTER default. |
| **Wide panels** | Nov 2011 (Twelve) | Room for the old dog to take his time. Reused for Barnaby II's scuttle. |
| **Lamp on / lamp off** | Jan 2006 (the Rain) | Tier 1 warm cone, last tier the same framing in blue window light. The Empty Chair's structure, planted eight years early. |
| **The empty seat / bed** | Jul 2014 (the passenger seat), Dec 2014 (the bed) | Absence drawn as an object in its place. |
| **The hand at the door** | Dec 2014 | Wood as only a hand for a week. |
| **The narrow-caption travelogue** | Sept 2008 | Captions in a travelogue voice for six weeks; retired. |
| **Noir** | Oct 2002 | One week; captions instead of balloons; no dog; unsigned; ends with the dog walking into the noir room in the normal style. |
| **Two styles in one panel** | Oct 11, 2002; Nov 15, 2006 | The real dog drawn properly beside a genre or a meme drawn wrong. |
| **The corner box** | Jun 2002 / Jun 2008 / Jun 2016 | A syndicate-style ad in the strip's display face announcing a novel that never comes. Same corner, same face. Never referenced. |
| **The chorus** | Jan 2004 – 2011 | Stick figures at the back of the panel: readers. 2 → 30. They turn to look at the dog. |
| **The hum line** | Sept 2011 on | A flat line under every motel panel: the ice machine. |
| **The sign's letters** | Mar 2009 on | SMALL WOOD LANES loses letters over eleven years. See `objects.md` for the timeline. |
| **Rhyme panels** | throughout | #1's last panel (alone at a long surface) every September; #4 and #37 (the taped rectangle); the chore coat 2000/2010; the Rain 2004/2005; Christmas Eve 1999/2017; the Lot C reprint pair. Compose them to match. |
| **Real-name leaks** | 1998, 1999, 2000, 2011, 2014 | The yearbook, the vet's chart, the principal, the frame-shop owner, the dock: the world's paperwork and one person say "Arthur." Never the strip. |
