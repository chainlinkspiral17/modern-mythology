# SAMPLE STRIPS · drafts across the five cadences

Scripted strips, one or two per era, in the brief's format (panel
by panel, dialogue in quotes). These are drafts to find the voice
and to give the art layer something to draw against. None is canon
until it appears in a scene. Dates are the in-fiction run dates.

**The character in the panels is WOOD** — Wood as he draws
himself. Wood is only ever in the margin, and in the foreword
and afterword. See `_AUTHORIAL_RULE.md`: the strip is the volume,
and the reader infers the life from it until the afterword tells
it.

The brief already specifies six strips (the fried-clam daily, the
Greyhound Sunday, the 3:42 AM daily, the muffler-shop daily, the
cocktail-hour daily, the dock Sunday) and three Sunday pages in
full ("The Carry," "The Empty Chair," "The Box"). Those are in
`_SOURCE_BRIEF.md` and `the_strip.md`. What follows fills the eras
the brief summarizes.

## Era 1 · The Timberline · every two weeks

### "Drift Wood" #1 · Friday, September 12, 1997

*Crow-quill and ballpoint on cardstock. Four panels, cluttered
balloons, heavy black. The title lettered in a hand that is trying
to look like it wasn't trying.*

1. The cafeteria. A tray of tater tots. WOOD (seventeen, the
   overcoat, indoors — the strip's lead, drawn taller than the
   author) to a lunch lady who is not looking at him:
   "Do you ever consider that this room is the only place in
   Small Wood where three hundred people voluntarily sit in the
   same direction?"
2. Lunch lady, ladling: "Corn or peas."
3. Wood, to camera, a balloon with too many words: "Truffaut
   said the film of tomorrow will be an act of love. I said corn."
4. Wide. Wood alone at a long table with his corn. Behind him,
   tiny, TODD (backwards cap) drawing on a napkin, and CHLOE
   (eyeliner, Discman) reading a paperback with the cover folded
   back. Nobody is facing the same direction.
   Margin, tiny: *A. Finch — Small Wood, OR — a gull.*

### "Drift Wood" #21 · Friday, March 5, 1999

1. The newsroom after hours. Two desks pushed together. Wood
   inking; Chloe proofing his page with a red pen she is using on
   the drawing, not the text.
   CHLOE: "Your cashier has six fingers."
   WOOD: "It's expressive."
2. CHLOE: "It's a sixth finger."
   WOOD: "Egon Schiele—"
   CHLOE: "—had the correct number of fingers and drew them
   wrong on purpose. You drew this one wrong by accident and now
   you're citing Vienna."
3. Silence. Wood adding a seventh finger.
4. Chloe, not looking up, red pen moving: "I'm going to Seattle."
   Wood, not looking up, pen moving: "I know."
   The panel is mostly the two desks and the dark window.
   Margin: *a crab.*

## Era 2 · The Weekday Daily · Monday to Friday

### Tuesday, October 9, 2001

*Brush-pen, faster. The line has learned to commit.*

1. The community-college parking lot in Newport, rain. Wood in
   the Subaru's predecessor (reserved: a Datsun) with the engine
   off, drawing on the steering wheel.
2. A CAMPUS SECURITY guy at the window, tapping. "You a student?"
3. WOOD: "I'm auditing Composition II so I can use the print
   shop. Philosophically I'm auditing everything."
4. SECURITY, walking away: "Lot C is for students."
   Wood, to no one: "Lot C is for cowards."
   He does not move the car.

### Friday, May 23, 2003 · the last one for the college paper

1. The laundromat. The digest rack, empty. Wood stapling five
   strips into a digest.
2. The laundromat's owner (reserved; could be Sal's tenant),
   folding: "You still doing these?"
   WOOD: "Five a week."
3. OWNER: "For who?"
4. Wood looking at the rack. A long beat with no balloon. Then,
   small: "The rack."
   Margin: *no gull. The first time.*

## The delusion-and-leak pattern · three samples

*The rule's core mechanism: the hope and the lie in the same
panel. Wood succeeds where Arthur didn't; the strip never corrects
it; the reader does.*

### Era 2 · Monday–Wednesday, September 18–20, 2000 · "Seattle"

Three consecutive dailies.

**Monday.** Wood and Chloe on the Greyhound, side by side, the
overcoat and the Discman, the window full of Sitka spruce going by.
CHLOE: "You actually came." WOOD: "Someone has to make sure you
don't get interesting without supervision."

**Tuesday.** Seattle. Rain that is somehow a different rain. Wood
and Chloe under one umbrella on a hill of wet brick. A gallery
window. WOOD: "Everything here is trying so hard." CHLOE: "You're
crying." WOOD: "It's the trying."

**Wednesday.** The framing shop, Small Wood. Wood cutting foam core
with the dull utility knife. Gully in the door with two grease
sacks. No reference to Monday or Tuesday. The strip never mentions
Seattle again.

*The Sunday that follows (Week 4 of Arc 1, the brief) is the
Greyhound station — Wood outside the tinted window, hands in his
coat, the bus leaving without him. The strip has told the reader
both things and has chosen neither. That is the volume.*

### Era 2 · digest cover, Friday, June 7, 2002 · the corner box

The laundromat digest's weekly cover — quarter-letter, one image,
the proto-Sunday. A landscape: the logging road, potholes full of
sky. In the bottom-left corner, boxed, hand-lettered in the strip's
own display face, as if it were an ad from a syndicate the strip
doesn't have:

> **COMING 2003 · A GRAPHIC NOVEL BY A. FINCH · "SLASH"**
> *A crime story of the Oregon coast.*

The box reappears on the Sundays of June 1, 2008 (*COMING 2009 ·
"THE LONG WAY BACK" · a travelogue*) and June 5, 2016 (*COMING 2017 · "CEDAR" · a
novel of the timber country*). Same corner, same face. Nothing
ever comes. No strip ever refers to the boxes. The reader who
notices them has understood the strip's author better than any
character in it.

### Era 3 · Thursday, November 16, 2006 · the week after

The ROLFCOPTR week is over. *Drift Wood* returns. Wood at the
drafting table, drawn at two-thirds his usual size in a panel that
is otherwise the usual size.

1. Wood, small, inking. A stack of mail on the floor: envelopes
   with a ringtone company's logo.
2. Gully, normal size, in the door: "Forty thousand people,
   Woody."
3. Wood, small: "Forty thousand people who came for a helicopter."
4. Gully: "Still forty thousand." Wood does not answer. The panel
   is mostly the mail.

*The strip is bragging and mourning in the same four panels, and
it draws its own lead smaller to do it. He stays two-thirds size
for six weeks and then, without comment, is normal again.*

## Era 3 · The Newspaper Run · six dailies and a Sunday

### Daily · Wednesday, March 16, 2005

*Digital flats, muddy. The father is sick; the strip does not say
so; the strip is about a bag.*

1. The frame shop. Wood cutting a mat. TODD in the door with one
   grease sack instead of two.
2. WOOD: "One."
   TODD: "Your dad said he wasn't hungry."
3. Wood cutting. The blade goes off the line.
4. Todd sitting on the spackle bucket, eating from the one sack,
   holding it open toward Wood without looking at him. Wood
   still cutting. Rain in the alley. No words.

### Sunday landscape · March 8, 2009 · "Lanes"

*The first fully-realized silent Sunday. Wet-on-wet.*

Three tiers. No dialogue.

1. The bowling alley from across the highway at night. Its neon —
   SMALL WOOD LANES, the L flickering — hammered by rain, doubled
   in the wet asphalt. One car in the lot.
2. Closer. The dumpster behind it, chained. A wet ring of rope on
   the chain-link where a leash was tied once. Nothing else.
3. Wide again, later. The neon off. The lot empty. On the ridge
   east of town, far and small, a glow that is not the moon and
   that the composition treats as weather.
   Margin: *A. Finch — 3/8/09 — a gull, and a dog beside it.* (The
   only time Barnaby appears in the signature while alive.)

### Daily · Thursday, August 23, 2012

*Era III line arriving. The Driftwood Motel, 2:10 AM.*

1. The night-audit desk. A CRT. A bell. Wood in a motel polo
   drawing on the back of a registration card. The ice machine in
   the hall, a sound line drawn as a low flat hum.
2. A GUEST in a bathrobe: "Do you have a map of the town?"
3. Wood, sliding the registration card across: on the back, a
   perfect ink map of Small Wood, every building, drawn in the
   ninety seconds since panel 1.
4. GUEST: "Is this... accurate?"
   WOOD: "It was in 1997."
   The hum line continues under all four panels.

### Daily · Monday, September 21, 2015 · the return from the second sabbatical

1. The drafting table. Fresh bristol. Blue-pencil grid. The
   three-legged pup asleep on Wood's foot.
2. Wood, brush loaded, to the pup: "Nine months. Maybe they've
   forgotten the helicopter."
3. He inks the banner: D-R-I-F-T W-O-O-D.
4. A comment notification on the laptop behind him, unread:
   *is this the rolfcoptr guy.* Wood: "Nobody forgets the
   helicopter."
   The pup does not wake.
   Margin: *a three-legged scruff.*

## The last public Sunday · December 27, 2020

*Full page. Four tiers. Era III at its very end; the palette
already softening into Era IV's ochre and slate.*

1. **Wide.** The garage studio, morning, gray. The drafting table
   with sixteen years of tape ghosts on its edge. The three-legged
   dog in the doorway wanting the walk. Wood at the table, coat already on.
   WOOD (small balloon): "One more."
2. **The table, from above.** A daily grid, four panels. Panel 1 is
   the cafeteria from 1997, redrawn in the 2020 hand: the tray, the
   corn, the lunch lady. Panel 2 is the same cafeteria empty. Panels
   3 and 4 are blank.
3. **Close.** The brush over Panel 3. The droplet. This time it
   drops — a single black bead on the white, dead center, where a
   figure would go.
4. **Wide, the beach.** Wood and the dog at the tide line, from
   behind, walking away from us into fog. The sketchbook under his
   arm. Sandpipers. No balloon.
   Margin: *Drift Wood — A. Finch — Small Wood, OR — 12/27/20.* The
   gull and the scruff both. Then, below the signature, so small
   the coastal papers' reproduction would lose it: *thank you for
   reading.*

*Note: Watterson's last strip is "let's go exploring." This is not
that; Wood's version is a walk he has taken every day for twenty
years. The exploring is behind him. The page's only event is the
ink dropping, which is the thing his hand refused to let happen in
"The Empty Chair." He lets it fall and goes outside.*

## Era 5 · ROFLCOPTER · the personal voyage

### Page, no date · January 2021 · the first private page

*No grid. One drawing. Ink and a single wash.*

The main street of Small Wood from the top of the hill by the
school, in 1997 on the left half and in 2021 on the right, joined
at the center line with no seam: the mill's smokestack on the left
becoming the cell mast on the right; Wagner's Hardware becoming the
empty storefront becoming (very small, a lean) a skate shop's first
sign; the same crow on the same seawall at both ends of the
drawing. Nobody in it. Then, on inspection: a seventeen-year-old in
an overcoat on the left, a man with a three-legged dog on the
right, walking toward each other and never meeting because the
drawing has no middle.

Margin, tiny, a name that has never been lettered in his hand
before, spelled right: *ROFLCOPTER.*

### Page · autumn 2023 · from the mill run (40 pages)

*Pages 12–13 of the cedar mill's last year, 1994, which Wood was
fourteen for. Researched at the library. Drawn in the Era IV hand
with Era I's black spotting brought back on purpose.*

A two-page spread. The mill floor on the last shift. Every worker
drawn from the 1994 Timberline group photo Mrs. Gable found for him
in the back room, aged nothing. The saw. The cedar smell drawn as
the only wash on the page, a pale ochre that hangs in the rafters.
In the foreground, the one thing not from the photograph: Todd's
uncle, who he never met, holding the shingle shack's keys.

No dialogue. Below the spread, hand-lettered, the only text Wood
writes in the private work that isn't a signature:

> *I was fourteen and I thought this was the end of something. It
> was the end of something. I just had the wrong something.*

### Page · the last · April 2027

The brief's final page, unchanged. A ROFLCOPTER page. The bay, the chimney, the mug,
the tide line, the sandpipers, the cedar branch. *Arthur Finch,
1997–2027.*

It is the last page of the published book. The afterword follows
it. See `foreword_afterword.md`.
