Per the user (September 2026): *the eventual scope will be about
1,500 pages of finished material, a two-volume physical set, one
volume digital — rough detail the full scope and find the best to
represent it.*

This file does both. **Part One** is the edition: what the 1,500
pages are, how they split, and the principle by which the ~10,000
strips in the boxes become the pages in the book. **Part Two** is
the complete run at rough grain: every year's arcs, fixtures and
sequences, each with a selection mark, so that what's *in* the
book is chosen against a full picture of what *exists*. **Part
Three** is production.

Nothing here contradicts `_SCOPE_AND_YEARS.md` (the per-year
spine) or `_AUTHORIAL_RULE.md`; where the earlier 200-unit sketch

### The selection principle · the editor's cut

How ten thousand become fifteen hundred. These are the rules the
in-fiction editor uses, and they're the rules we use:

1. **Every Sunday landscape is considered first.** They are the
   confessions. About half go in.
2. **Every arc the brief names goes in complete.** The Driftwood
   Ledger, the 3:00 AM Protocol, the Glass Pavilion, Low Tide at
   Alsea Bay, the 30-Year Ledger.
3. **Every strip that changes the margin mark goes in.** The
   mark is the stat line; the book can't skip a change.
4. **Every year has a floor.** No year of the public run gets fewer
   than 12 pages, so the strip never disappears; the reader should
   feel the years even where the arcs were weak.
5. **The fixtures recur.** The strip's annual fixtures (below) get
   at least one strip per year, so the book has the seasons the
   strip had.
6. **The gaps are kept as gaps.** Reprints, sabbaticals, no-file:
   a divider and a sentence, not an omission.
7. **Omitted arcs are indexed.** The back matter lists every arc
   of the run in one line, marked *in* or *not in this edition*.
   The reader can see the whole shape of what they don't have.
8. **Nothing that puts the author on the page.** The rule.

### What the digital edition adds

The engine volume is the same book, read page by page, with:

- **The mark tracker.** A persistent corner element showing the
  current margin mark, and the history of marks as a timeline the
  reader can open. The stat line, literal.
- **The box register, live.** Every strip links to its box; every
  box lists what ran and what didn't.
- **Room.** The digital edition can carry, unlockably, arcs the
  physical set only indexes — the full parody week, more of the
  2003 unprinted year, more digest covers, the reprint pairs. Not
  required; the room is there.
- **The player canon**, if built: the player as the editor,
  choosing a different 1,500 from the same 10,000
  (`_ARC_OUTLINE.md`).

## PART TWO · THE COMPLETE RUN

Rough grain: what exists in the boxes, year by year, as **arcs**
(a named multi-week storyline), **fixtures** (the annual recurring
strips), and, for the private work, **sequences**. Each carries a
selection mark for the edition:

- **●** in full — every strip of the arc
- **◐** in part — the key week, or the Sundays only
- **○** one strip — a single representative daily or Sunday
- **—** not in this edition — indexed in the back matter only

The brief's five arcs are marked with †. Everything else is
roughed here for the first time and is changeable; the point is
that the book gets chosen against a whole.

### The annual fixtures (public run, 1997–2020)

The strip had seasons. Each fixture recurs yearly; the edition
takes at least one per year (principle 5).

| month | fixture | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| January | **The Rain** | The year's first Sunday is always weather. The strip's deepest landscapes are Januaries. |
| February | **The Crab Derby** | Gully's flyer (the brief's "here's three hundred bucks"). Wood draws the flyer; the strip shows the flyer; the town's year starts. |
| March | **The Grunion Run** | Small Wood's annual festival for a fish that crawls ashore to breed (an echo of vol 2's Small Wood, felt). Wood hates it, draws it every year, and the drawings get better than the jokes. |
| June | **Last Bell** | School lets out. The strip's origin month. Wood, every June, near the high school, not going in. |
| July | **The Fourth on the Bay** | Fireworks over Alsea Bay from Gully's dock. The only night strip with color in the early years. |
| August | **Tourist Season** | The town full of strangers. The strip's cruelest and later kindest observation month. |
| September | **The Anniversary** | The strip's birthday. A daily that references #1 without quoting it. Wood a year older; the cafeteria somewhere in the panel. |
| November | **The Dog Night** | The night the dog was found, 1999. A wordless daily every November: the dumpster, then the dog bed, then (2015 on) the box. |
| December | **Does Chloe Come Home** | The Christmas week. Some years she does. The strip never says in advance. |
