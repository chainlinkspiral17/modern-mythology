## Tier 3 · The Assessment

*eye-level, low.* Tier 3 · The Assessment. Wood has set the mug on the porch rail (steam curling) and crouched on his haunches, elbows on knees. The puppy has scrambled out and stands planted on the floorboards: wiry terrier/border mix, one ear pinned back, one cocked up like a radar dish, coat like unraveled steel wool, the left hind leg ending cleanly below the hock — healed, unbothered. A sturdy tripod, chest out, barking a small gravelly puff of vapor into Wood's face. Wood stares down with zero sentimentality, eyebrows flat, mouth a line. Taped to the box with peeling electrical tape, a torn strip of butcher paper in blocky grease-pencil: Found him by the log dump in Toledo. Vet says he's four months. His name is not my problem.

## Tier 5 · The Occupation

*warm low close.* Tier 5 · The Occupation. Low angle at the base of the drafting table. The winter sun has cleared the ridge and cuts a bright warm amber blade of light across the floor. Wood has slid off his stool and sits flat on the floorboards, legs splayed, back against the sturdy pine table leg. The puppy sprawled dead asleep across his crossed ankles, chin wedged over his wool sock, breath puffing against the yarn; clutched tightly in its front paws, Wood's favorite wooden crow-quill nib holder, the end already dented by needle teeth. Wood's hand resting on the scruff of the pup's neck, thumb brushing behind the upright ear. Not drawing. Not looking at the blank paper above. Looking at the dust motes turning in the winter light.
