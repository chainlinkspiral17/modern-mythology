# THE STRIP · *Drift Wood* → *ROFLCOPTER*
## Small Wood, OR · 1997–2027 · by A. Finch

The artifact at the center of vol 10. A daily four-panel strip and a
Sunday page, drawn every day for thirty years by Arthur Finch, about
the town he never left. Over ten thousand dailies in banker's boxes
in a garage apartment behind a cedar-shake tavern.

This file holds the strip *as an object*: its eras, its materials,
its recurring pages, and the two Sunday pages the brief specifies
tier by tier. The life it recorded is in the character files and
`_ARC_OUTLINE.md`.

---

## I. THE NAMES

**Drift Wood** (1997–2020). The public strip, under one name for
twenty-three years: *The Timberline* every two weeks, the college
paper five days a week, then six dailies and a Sunday page on the
web and in a handful of coastal papers. The name never changes
because the strip never stops being about the town.

**ROLFCOPTR** (November 2006; one week, then forever). Not the
strip. A 72-hour parody drawn beside it, on clearance instant
noodles and generic citrus soda: *a stick figure screaming internet
acronyms into an uncaring abyss.* Front page of Digg. A 509 from
the server. Forty thousand visitors a day who came for the
helicopter. $800 a month in ringtone banners that pay the rent for
a year. He kills the parody after a month; the name outlives it.
For the rest of his public life he is "the ROLFCOPTR guy," and
*Drift Wood* is the strip by him. Arthur, at a wedding in 2014:
*"I run a webcomic named after a misspelled 2004 meme about an
imaginary helicopter."* — which is what strangers think, and what
he has stopped correcting.

**ROFLCOPTER** (January 2021–April 2027). The private work. When
*Drift Wood* ends he starts drawing pages for no one and signs them
with the albatross, spelled right for the first time. An imaginary
helicopter is a way of leaving a town without leaving it. The
personal voyage.

Outside the fiction the volume is *ROFLCOPTR* / *ROFLcopter*. Inside
it, three spellings are three things. The gap is the joke and then
it's the point.

Cadence, venues, counts and the burnout are in
`publication_history.md`.

## II. THE FOUR ERAS

The visual transformation mirrors the psychological one: the cruder
the teenage confidence, the scratchier the art; the more devastating
the mid-life realities, the more beautiful the artwork grows to
compensate.

### Era I · The High School Zine Era (1997–2003) — "Drift Wood"

- **Line:** scratchy crow-quill and ballpoint on cardstock,
  photocopied with harsh contrast. Sharp, jagged line weights, heavy
  black spotting, cluttered dialogue balloons. Mid-90s indie comix
  DNA — *Optic Nerve*, *Ghost World*, late *Calvin and Hobbes*
  reprints.
- **Arc 1 aesthetic (autumn 2000):** early brush-pen over rough blue
  pencil; xerox grain; stark spotting after Clowes and early Jaime
  Hernandez. Cold coastal rain in frantic vertical hatching.
- **Subject:** cafeteria existentialism; pretentious monologues about
  French cinema delivered to indifferent cashiers; the sweet,
  unspoken tension between Arthur and Chloe.
- **Ends with:** Arthur turning down the PNCA scholarship to "work on
  his masterwork" and taking the framing-shop job.

### Era II · The Webcomic Diaspora & Irony Poisoning (2004–2011)

- **Line:** digitized brush-pen on bristol with early, muddy digital
  flats. Loosens into rubbery, kinetic energy — classic newspaper
  adventure strip crossed with blog-era minimalism.
- **Arc 2 aesthetic (November 2006):** digital flats over physical
  ink; saturated neon monitor glow cutting through dark garage
  interiors; jagged, hyperactive linework.
- **Subject:** beneath the meme's shadow the storytelling darkens.
  Arthur turns twenty-five; the graphic novel is five hundred loose
  pages; his father gets sick; the framing shop cuts his hours.

### Era III · The Masterwork of Mediocrity (2012–2020)

- **Line:** Nick Derington's razor-sharp, ultra-confident brush-and-
  ink clarity — pristine architectural perspective, crisp silhouette,
  expressive character geometry. The panels breathe.
- **Sundays:** Bill Watterson-grade wet-on-wet watercolor. Gray-green
  moss, fog on 101, rusted galvanized tin, bruised dusk over the
  Pacific.
- **Arc 3 aesthetic (July 2014):** hairline brush control; blinding
  clear summer light in soft cerulean and pale linen.
- **Arc 4 aesthetic (Nov–Dec 2014):** desaturated wet-on-wet — raw
  umber, Payne's gray, Prussian blue, raw sienna. Line goes quiet;
  water bleeds over the ink edge to suggest rain and fading light.
- **Subject:** peak tragicomedy. International niche celebration;
  cannot make first-and-last without Todd. Three weeks on the
  catering at Chloe's wedding.

### Era IV · The Pacific Twilight (2021–2027)

- **Line:** synthesis of high craft and relaxed economy. Spare,
  knowing, deeply human. Palette softens to muted ochres, slate
  blues, misty sea-foam greens.
- **Arc 5 aesthetic (2026–27):** masterwork level. Watterson's fluid
  Sunday layouts with Derington's spatial discipline. Luminous
  sunbreaks through coastal cloud.
- **Subject:** the public strip has ended (Dec 27, 2020). These are
  the ROFLCOPTER pages — no grid, no schedule, no readers. Small
  Wood is half tourist Airbnbs, half boarded-up memory. Arthur
  accepts that *this* was the work.

## III. MATERIALS (as specified)

- India ink. **Hunt 102** crow-quill. **Winsor & Newton Series 7
  No. 2** sable brush. Higgins ink stains on the middle-finger
  knuckle.
- **140 lb Arches cold-press** for the Sunday watercolors.
- Bristol board for dailies, taped to a green vinyl drafting mat
  with brown kraft tape; non-photo blue pencil grid.
- A battered green-enameled architect's swing-arm lamp, clamped to
  the drafting table.
- Three jars on the sill: red sable brushes; rusted dip-pen nibs; a
  single dried sand dollar from Beverly Beach.
- A wooden-handled crow-quill nib holder with teeth marks near the
  ferrule — Arthur's, then the puppy's.
- Salt air rusts the X-Acto blades.

## IV. RECURRING PAGES

**The Sunday landscapes.** Every fourth or fifth Sunday, no jokes.
Silent multi-tier watercolor of coastal weather: rain hammering the
neon of an empty bowling alley; rain pooling in potholes on logging
roads; sunlight through Sitka spruce for twenty minutes before the
dark rolls back.

**The Arthur-and-Barnaby walks.** Where the screwball dialogue drops
and the landscape breathes. Arthur talks to the dog about aging,
dying unknown, the light on the spruce grove at dawn. The dog shakes
off salt water and drags him into the next frame.

**The fried-clam beat.** Arthur mid-monologue; Todd in the doorway
with two grease sacks; Arthur dead-eyed; a silent wide shot of both
on spackle buckets in the rain alley, chewing.

**The signature.** Bottom right, outside the border: *A. Finch —
Small Wood, OR*, the date, and a tiny stylized gull or crab. The
mark changes when Arthur does. It is the strip's emotional
telemetry, and the volume should treat it as a readable stat.

## V. THE TWO SPECIFIED SUNDAY PAGES

These are the volume's visual canon: any CG, any diorama, any art
direction for vol 10 starts here. Full text in `_SOURCE_BRIEF.md`;
this is the working summary.

### "The Empty Chair" · Sunday, November 30, 2014

Four horizontal panoramic tiers. No dialogue. Ink on 140 lb Arches;
Payne's gray, Prussian blue, raw umber, neutral tint, one accent of
warm Naples yellow / burnt sienna.

1. **The Void Under the Desk.** Ground-level wide across the
   Douglas-fir floorboards at 3:45 AM — Barnaby's eye height. The
   swing-arm lamp's amber cone on the right; indigo shadow
   everywhere else (banker's boxes, brush-water jars, oil-stained
   wall). Center: the braided wool dog bed, worn to a crescent,
   stiff white-and-tan hairs in the yarn. A grayed, tooth-pitted
   tennis ball with estuary sand under the cinderblock shelf.
   Arthur from mid-torso down on the high stool, left foot hovering
   above where the dog's ribs would press his sock. Still.
2. **The Downpour on the Glass.** Eye-level across the table to the
   wood-mullioned casement facing the gravel alley behind the
   tavern. Storm at peak: horizontal rain in resist and slate/bone
   washes; black Sitka crowns whipping in the south wind. The three
   jars on the sill. Arthur's face as a ghost in the pane — hollows,
   three days' stubble, mouth a flat pencil line, eyes through the
   glass rather than at it.
3. **The Suspended Nib.** Tight on the right hand. Zero crosshatch.
   Fresh bristol taped down, blue-pencil four-panel grid,
   terrifyingly blank. Calloused ink-black knuckle; teeth marks on
   the brush handle. Sable tip loaded, a quarter-inch above Panel 1,
   a single droplet bowed by gravity, refusing to drop. The ink
   beginning to tack.
4. **The Cut.** Tier 1's framing, lighting inverted. Lamp off. Cold
   pre-dawn neutral tint, raw umber, Payne's gray; the only light
   the bluish storm-glow through the wet window. Arthur a slumped
   silhouette, forearms flat on the board, shoulders hitched. Below,
   the empty indented bed in cold twilight.

**Margin:** *A. Finch — Small Wood, OR.* Where the gull or crab goes:
a single clean unbroken horizontal stroke.

### "The Box" · Sunday, December 21, 2014

Five panels, varied multi-tier. Minimal dialogue. Cerulean and
titanium white sky; sap and hooker's greens; raw umber and sepia
deck planks; a kraft-brown anchor.

1. **The Departure.** From the porch steps down the sloped gravel
   drive to the highway. First clear freezing morning after three
   weeks of rain; eggshell-blue sky; frost on the puddle edges. Todd
   walking away toward his idling flatbed, brown canvas Carhartt,
   hood down, breath pluming, hands in pockets, not looking back.
   Dead center on the top cedar step: a waxed corrugated produce box
   stamped **ALICE'S WHOLESALE BAIT & SMOKED MEATS**, one corner
   pushed ajar from inside.
2. **The Doorstep.** Arthur's eye level; the aluminum storm door
   creaking open. Gray thermal, unbuttoned red flannel, sock feet,
   chipped white enamel mug of black coffee. A wiry gray-whiskered
   snout emerging from shredded newspaper and a grease-stained wool
   work sock.
3. **The Assessment.** Arthur crouched, mug steaming on the rail.
   The pup planted on three legs like a tripod: one ear pinned, one
   up like a radar dish, coat like unraveled steel wool, left hind
   leg ending clean below the hock, healed. Barking a gravelly puff
   of vapor into Arthur's face. Arthur, zero sentimentality.
   Butcher-paper note taped to the box in Todd's grease pencil:
   *Found him by the log dump in Toledo. Vet says he's four months.
   His name is not my problem.*
   PUPPY: *Yip.* ARTHUR: *"I am in the middle of a deadline, and I
   don't like you."*
4. **The Infiltration.** Interior wide from the threshold. The pup
   scuttling, not limping, four damp paw-and-stump prints across the
   floorboards; bypasses the empty dog bed entirely; headfirst in
   the overturned wicker scrap basket, tail a metronome, rejected
   pencil sketches flying. Arthur in the doorway, hands on hips,
   already lost.
5. **The Occupation.** Low-angle at the table's base; winter sun
   finally over the ridge, an amber blade across the floor. Arthur
   slid down off the stool, back against the pine table leg. The
   pup asleep across his crossed ankles, chin over the wool sock,
   the crow-quill holder clutched in both paws, already dented.
   Arthur's thumb brushing the fur behind the upright ear. Not
   drawing. Not looking at the blank paper. Watching dust motes in
   the light.

**Margin:** *A. Finch — 2014.* The flat stroke from last week is
gone. In its place, a tiny three-legged scruff of ink, fast asleep.

## VI. THE FINAL PAGE · April 2027

Full-bleed, no panel borders. Alsea Bay opening into the Pacific at
low tide; morning sun through low cloud turning wet sand to a mirror
of gold and slate. Middle distance, small and sharp: the tackle
shop's chimney smoking; Chloe on the dock with a ceramic mug; Arthur
walking the tide line, sketchbook under his arm; Barnaby II charging
a flock of sandpipers. Bottom right: *Arthur Finch, 1997–2027*,
flanked by a tiny cedar branch floating out to sea.

## VII. THE MARK · gallery / unlock hooks (proposed, reserved)

If vol 10 gets a gallery layer like vol 6 and vol 7, the natural
unit is the **Sunday page**. Each arc unlocks its Sundays; the
signature mark for each week is a collectible stat. "The Empty
Chair" and "The Box" are the first two CGs to commission. The music
room's vol 10 entries, if any, are rain on tin, a two-group espresso
machine, a Subaru with a bad alternator, and the ice machine at the
Driftwood Motel.
