# TODD "GULLY" GULLIVER · the anchor
## Gully's Tackle & Roast · by the boat launch · since 2003

*An inference document. In the panels he is Gully. What follows is what the strip lets the reader infer about the man behind him. See `_AUTHORIAL_RULE.md`.*

The archetypal slacker at the back of the journalism classroom in
1997: backwards ball cap, muddy work boots, a ten-second delay,
drawing crude caricatures of teachers on the margins of discarded
draft sheets. While Arthur agonizes over whether his life will have
profound historical meaning, Todd is concerned with whether the tide
is coming in and whether the bakery has day-old maple bars.

If Arthur is Don Quixote, Todd is Sancho Panza with an espresso
tamper and a tackle box.

## II. THE ECONOMY OF TODD

He is neither ambitious nor insecure; he fits the world he was born
into. He earns enough selling seasonal crab bait to pay cash for a
used flatbed while Arthur spends nine months on an eighty-page strip
about wage labor that earns forty dollars in web ads. Arthur views
this with equal parts condescension and agonizing jealousy. Todd
harbors no resentment.

**Informal patron.** Money under thin pretexts: *"Hey Woody, I need
a flyer drawn for the crab derby, here's three hundred bucks in
cash."* Changes the alternator on the Subaru. Pins Arthur's original
pencil roughs to the grease-stained corkboard above the register,
between lost-cat flyers and local notices.

He is the only character who can tell Arthur to shut up without a
three-month sulk, because Arthur knows the affection is free of
judgment.

## III. THE PLACE (reserved as its own file)

Gully's Tackle & Roast: shingle shack, boat launch, the dock out
back where the strip's most important conversations of the 2020s
happen. The corkboard. The two-group machine. The chowder. The
concept sheet draws a cedar-shingle BAIT SHOP with a barrel by the
door and a lure hanging in the window — that is Gully's before the
espresso machine, or Gully's as Arthur draws it.
