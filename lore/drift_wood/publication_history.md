# PUBLICATION HISTORY · *Drift Wood* → *ROFLCOPTER* · 1997–2027

How often the strip came out, where, and what that did to it. The
cadence is the strip's biography: every change of schedule is a
change in Arthur's life, and the volume's acts fall on the seams.

Per the user (September 2026): biweekly in the high school paper;
weekday-daily in the college years; then a newspaper-style run of
dailies plus Sundays for a decade or two; a Watterson-type burnout;
then it continues privately and becomes a sprawling personal work.
**Drift Wood is everything before the burnout. ROFLCOPTER is the
personal voyage after it.**

---

## The names

- ***Drift Wood*** (1997–2020). The public strip, under one name
  for twenty-three years: the school paper, the college daily, the
  newspaper run. The name never changes because the strip never
  stops being about the town.
- ***ROLFCOPTR*** (November 2006, one week; then forever). The
  meme. A 72-hour parody sprint, a stick figure screaming acronyms,
  the Digg front page, the 509, the ringtone banners. It was never
  the strip's name; it was the name the internet gave *him*.
  Strangers at weddings know ROLFCOPTR. They don't know Drift Wood.
  He spends fourteen years being the guy who drew the helicopter
  strip once.
- ***ROFLCOPTER*** (January 2021–April 2027). The private work. When
  the public strip ends and he starts drawing for no one, he names
  the pages after the albatross — spelled right, for the first time.
  An imaginary helicopter is a way of leaving a town without
  leaving it. The personal voyage.

Outside the fiction the volume is *ROFLcopter* / *ROFLCOPTR* (the
engine's main menu; the user's own spellings vary). Inside it, the
three spellings are three different things, and the volume should
never confuse them.

and pages, which is the number the editor counts in 2026.

## Era 1 · The Timberline (1997–1999) · every two weeks

*Drift Wood* runs in every issue of the school paper, which comes
out every other Friday during the school year. Arthur is editor; the
strip is the one thing in the paper he doesn't edit, because he
draws it the night before deadline on cardstock and xeroxes it
himself. Chloe, culture editor, writes the paper's only real
criticism and lets him have the back page.

Two weeks is a long time for a joke to gestate. The early strips are
over-written, over-inked, and over-confident: a monologue about
Truffaut delivered to a cashier, four panels of cafeteria
existentialism that took thirteen days to distill. Todd is in the
margins of the same draft sheets, drawing teachers.

Ends June 1999 with the last issue of the year and the last issue
Arthur edits. Chloe leaves for college. Arthur stays. The paper's
new editor asks if he'll keep sending strips. He says he'll think
about it and never answers.

## Era 2 · The Weekday Daily (1999–2003) · Monday to Friday

The "college years" — his cohort's, not exactly his. Arthur turns
down the PNCA scholarship (autumn 2000, Arc 1). What he does
instead, as the lean here has it: takes non-degree classes at the
coast community college in Newport, twenty minutes up 101, mostly
so he can use the print shop, and draws the strip for the college
paper five days a week. (Alternate reading of the user's note:
Arthur goes to a college. That contradicts the brief's scholarship
refusal; the lean keeps the refusal and gives him a college paper
anyway. Decide when Act I is scripted.)

Daily changes everything. There is no time to over-write. The strip
gets faster, meaner, and better. The rubbery brush-pen line comes in
here, not later — a weekday deadline teaches the hand to commit.
Weekends off are for the phantom graphic novel; Box 4B's sixty pages
are Era 2 weekends.

The laundromat rack gets a photocopied weekly digest of the five
dailies, stapled, free. This is the *Drift Wood* most of Small Wood
actually reads. Sal Carratura's laundromat door is the one it hangs
beside, if the writer wants it.

Ends when Arthur stops going to Newport (2003, roughly when Todd
opens Gully's). He keeps drawing five a week for nobody for most of
a year. This is the first private stretch, and it's the seed of
Era 5.

## Era 3 · The Newspaper Run (2004–2020) · six dailies and a Sunday

Arthur discovers the early internet (brief, Phase Two) and puts
*Drift Wood* online on a **newspaper cadence**: a daily Monday
through Saturday, a full page on Sunday. He does this because it is
the form he grew up on — *Calvin and Hobbes* reprints, the Sunday
comics in the Oregonian — and because the form is a discipline he
can't otherwise impose on himself. The Sunday page is where the
watercolor starts.

**November 2006 · the ROLFCOPTR incident (Arc 2).** Broke and
furious that the serious pages are ignored on message boards, he
draws a one-week parody strip called *ROLFCOPTR* beside the real
one. It hits Digg. The server 509s. The advertisers want the stick
figure, not the town. He runs the parody for a month to keep the
banner money and the apartment, then kills it. It doesn't die. For
the rest of his public life, *Drift Wood* is "the strip by the
ROLFCOPTR guy." The name is now permanent; it just isn't on the
strip.

**~2008 onward:** a small coastal alt-syndication. Two or three
coastal weeklies (Newport, Florence, Lincoln City) and one Portland
alt-weekly carry the dailies in print, a week behind the web. It
pays almost nothing. It matters to Arthur enormously that the strip
is *in a newspaper*, on newsprint, next to the tide tables. He mails
the originals nowhere; he mails photocopies.

**2012–2020:** Era III's line (brief, Phase Three). The Sundays
become the point. Every fourth or fifth Sunday is a silent
landscape. Chloe's wedding (Arc 3, July 2014). Barnaby (Arc 4,
Nov–Dec 2014). The Silent Sunday Trilogy runs in print in three
coastal papers, which is where most of the town sees it.

## Era 4 · The Sabbaticals (2010, 2015)

Watterson took two. So does Arthur.

- **2010 · nine months.** After his father's death (the illness is
  Phase Two; the death is reserved, but 2010 is the lean). The
  papers run reprints of Era 2 dailies, which Arthur has to re-ink
  because the xeroxes don't reproduce. Re-inking his twenty-year-
  old self is the closest he comes to the travelogue he drafted in
  2008.
- **2015 · nine months.** After "The Box" (Dec 21, 2014). He draws
  Barnaby II every day and publishes none of it. The papers run the
  Sunday landscapes again. Readers write in about the landscapes.
  Nobody writes in about the jokes.

## The burnout · 2018–2020

The Watterson pattern, translated:

- **The name.** He has spent fourteen years being asked about a
  meme he misspelled at 3:42 AM. A streaming service wants an
  animated *ROLFCOPTR*. A sticker company wants the stick figure. A
  hardware chain wants Barnaby for a bag of dog food. He refuses
  all of it, which is the only power the name gives him, and the
  refusals cost more each year.
- **The format.** The aggregator that carries the strip to phones
  wants the Sunday page cut into a vertical scroll. The coastal
  papers want it shrunk to a third of a page. He has spent a decade
  learning to paint a five-tier page and the page is being
  unmade downstream.
- **The exhaustion.** Six dailies and a Sunday for sixteen years,
  minus eighteen months. Ten thousand days of noticing things
  turns out to cost something.
- **The town.** Small Wood is half Airbnbs. The people he drew are
  leaving or dying. The strip has started to be about people who
  aren't there.

He announces nothing. The last public *Drift Wood* Sunday runs
**December 27, 2020**. The dailies for the week before it are
ordinary. The papers find out when the January file doesn't
arrive. The aggregator posts a "hiatus" banner that is still up in
2027.

## Era 5 · ROFLCOPTER · the personal voyage (2021–2027)

The strip doesn't stop. It stops *being a strip.*

- **No grid.** The first private page, January 2021, has no panel
  borders. Then some do, some don't. Pages are the unit, not days.
- **The name.** He doesn't letter *Drift Wood* on the private
  pages. By spring 2021 they're signed, small, in the margin,
  *ROFLCOPTER* — the meme's name, spelled correctly, taken back. The
  one thing he could never shake becomes the name of the one thing
  nobody will see. An imaginary helicopter: the way out of Small
  Wood that doesn't require leaving it.
- **Sprawl.** With no deadline, the work widens. Pages that are the
  whole main street in 1997 and 2021 in one drawing. A run of forty
  pages on the cedar mill's last year (1994), which he was fourteen
  for and researched at the library. People from the strip's
  thirty years coming back at the ages they were. Todd's uncle.
  Chloe's mother. The Greyhound driver. The dog.
- **The voyage.** It goes places the strip never did — inland, up
  the river, into the mill's basement, into 1994, into the
  Greyhound's route north, into the rooms Chloe lived in that he
  never saw and draws anyway. He leaves the town for the first
  time, on paper, without moving the Subaru.
- **The town's whole record.** This is where the saga's echoes can
  live if they live anywhere: the librarian's back room, the crow
  that is always on the seawall, the glow on the ridge east of town
  that Arthur has drawn in Sunday landscapes since 2009 without
  once asking what it is. In the private work he draws it larger.
  He still doesn't ask. (Reserved; see the wiki's Echoes.)
- **The routine.** Morning shift at the Driftwood Motel, afternoons
  at Gully's with the sketchbook, evening walk with Barnaby II,
  then the garage. The brief's Phase Four. It looks, from outside,
  like a man who quit. It is the most work he has ever done.

This is the **sprawling personal work**. It is also, and the volume
should let the reader realize this before Arthur says it, the
masterpiece he spent 1998–2016 failing to write as a graphic novel.
The phantom novel was always going to be this. He just had to stop
publishing to see it.

## The retrospective · 2026–27 (Arc 5, revised)

The Seattle publisher wants *Drift Wood*, 1997–2020, complete. The
editor comes for the boxes of dailies and finds boxes 21 through
40 — six years of pages no one has seen, signed with the name she
came expecting to have to explain away.

**His work is published.** That is the happy ending, per the user,
and the lean is that *both halves* are: the book is *Drift Wood*
and then *ROFLCOPTER*, with a foreword and an afterword that tell
the author's life and hardships in prose — the only place in the
volume he is seen. The final page (April 2027) is the last page of
the book: the bay, the chimney, the mug, the tide line, the
sandpipers, the cedar branch going out. Signed *Arthur Finch,
1997–2027.* Then the afterword.

The specter of his own life is foreshadowed in the pages; the
afterword points back at them. Whether he is alive to see the book
is reserved (`foreword_afterword.md`).

## What the cadence gives the volume

- **Act breaks on schedule changes.** Biweekly → daily (1999).
  Daily → newspaper (2004). Newspaper → nothing (Dec 2020). Nothing
  → pages (Jan 2021). Each is a chapter seam.
- **A readable stat.** How many strips exist, how many were seen.
  The retrospective's count is the volume's last number.
- **A built-in structure for the player canon.** The player draws
  on the strip's schedule; when the schedule ends, the player keeps
  drawing with no one watching, and the game has to make that feel
  like the point. See `_ARC_OUTLINE.md`.
